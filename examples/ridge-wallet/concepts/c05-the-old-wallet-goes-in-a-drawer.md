## The one idea

Do not throw the old wallet away: put it in a drawer, and if you reach for it, start a return on the Ridge within 99 days of the order. Standard finishes only, because personalized items, warranty claim orders and certain limited editions cannot be returned.

## The mechanism

All inferred; every effect below is a hypothesis until a readout.

- The psychological job: he must feel that buying is not a verdict on his judgement. The status quo waits in the drawer, so the switch is not final and nobody has to know it was tried.
- Defaults and the status quo (`library/psychology/defaults-and-status-quo.md`): the old wallet stays available, which removes the switching cost the skeptic is pricing.
- Loss aversion (`library/psychology/loss-aversion-and-prospect-theory.md`): nothing is given up on day one; the reference point stays the old wallet. Used without a loss frame aimed at the viewer.
- Satisficing (`library/psychology/satisficing.md`): the catastrophe he avoids is a gadget bought off an ad; a rule that undoes it is worth more to him than a benefit.
- The question nobody asks (`library/psychology/question-nobody-asks.md`): why would he not buy? Because he does not want to be the man who was taken in (persona p3, status game, inferred).
- Insight behind the hook: "I questioned myself numerous times about spending the money on something I have not seen or how it works prior to biting the bullet and ordering." (featured review, ridge.com product page, captured 2026-10-23 (business/brief.md addendum)). Insight only; never quoted in the ad.
- Copy move: the trial becomes an object and a rule (a drawer; reach for it, start a return). The hook says "drawer", not "risk-free", and every creative prints the window from the order, the exclusions and the date of the terms.
