## The one idea

Tell the 99-day trial as the switch itself: order it, move the cards over when it arrives, keep the old wallet, and decide whether to start a return within 99 days of the order.

## The mechanism

All inferred; every effect below is a hypothesis until a readout.

- The psychological job: the viewer must see the trial as something he does, in order, not as a refund policy he might need. Each beat says what the terms allow at that point.
- Rituals and ownership (`library/psychology/rituals-and-ownership.md`): time-anchored copy (day 1, the weeks after, day 99) turns a policy into a sequence the buyer owns.
- The four forces (`library/psychology/jtbd-four-forces.md`): anxiety (the fad, the fit) and habit (the bifold still works) hold p1; the sequence answers both by keeping the old wallet available while he carries the new one.
- Endowment (`library/psychology/endowment-and-ikea-effect.md`): weeks of carrying it may make it his before the decision. Hypothetical, and it can cut both ways: the old wallet is endowed too.
- Satisficing (`library/psychology/satisficing.md`): the catastrophe he avoids is a fad bought off an ad; a reversible sequence removes it.
- Insight behind the beats: "it took me a couple of days to actually convert what I was using to this “new” type of wallet. After deciding what cards I had to have with me in the wallet, I converted." (featured review, ridge.com product page, captured 2026-10-23 (business/brief.md addendum)). One owner describes the switch as a sorting job after delivery. Insight only; never quoted in the ad.
- Copy move: day 1 is the order, because the terms count from purchase; the middle beat is the switch; the last beat is the decision, inside 99 days of the order. The copy never says what the buyer will feel, only what he can do, and every line that implies the window counts it from the order.

## Six hooks

1. (`day-1-vs-day-30`) "Day 1: the order, and the old bifold still in the back pocket. Day 99: the last day to start a Ridge return."
2. (`three-reasons`) "From bifold to Ridge wallet: order it, move the cards over, decide by day 99 from the order."
3. (`question`) "What moves over from the bifold, and what stays behind until day 99?"
4. (`specific-number`) "99 days, counted from the order, not from the day the box arrives."
5. (`callout`) "Switching from a bifold to a Ridge: start a return within 99 days of the order if the switch does not take."
6. (`pov`) "POV: the cards move over at the kitchen table. The 99 days started at the order."

Three tests: each hook carries an object or a place (the bifold, the back pocket, the box, the kitchen table) with a date or a count; each can be checked against the Returns page's 99 days from purchase; and the count from the order is Ridge's term, not a 100-day badge. No hook says what the buyer will feel or how the switch will go. Hook 5 names a situation, not a trait of the viewer. Hooks 1 and 3 say "day 99" and run only on a creative whose small print says "within 99 days of the order".
