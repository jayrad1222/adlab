## The one idea

One Ridge carried for years, held by the person who carried it, beside a new one, with the year on each and the scratches left in.

## The mechanism

All inferred; every effect below is a hypothesis until a readout.

- The psychological job: p2 must see one named owner's metal wallet still working after years before "built for life" means anything to him; the ad shows that one wallet and says nothing about others. Words have stopped working on him; Bellroy's "Wallets that last" and Ridge's own "Built to endure" are both in his feed (p2 language bank, observed).
- Costly signaling (`library/psychology/costly-signaling.md`): years of real wear cannot be bought, rendered or staged cheaply, so the object is credible where a claim is not.
- Make the invisible visible (`library/psychology/make-the-invisible-visible.md`): durability is invisible until a dated object shows it; imperfection left in reads as real.
- The pratfall effect (`library/psychology/shotton-choice-factory.md`): the scratches are named, not hidden. Saying "the plates scratch" makes "it still works" believable.
- Availability and vividness (`library/psychology/availability-and-vividness.md`): one named owner with an order date outweighs any count, and Ridge has no sourced count to use anyway.
- Implicit goal (`library/psychology/barden-implicit-goals.md`): security, an end to a recurring small failure (p2 file, inferred). The visual world is slow and plain: a hand, daylight, no box.
- Copy move: the year on each wallet is the headline; the owner's first name and the order date carry the proof; one line says what wore (the scratches, the elastic if it was replaced) and that it still holds the owner's carry. Which parts held up is said only if the photograph shows it: "the band wears and the plates do not" is hypothetical (interrogation R6), and the AI review summary's "screws loosening over time" (observed, frequency unknown) is a reason not to assume it.

Insight behind the concept, never ad copy: a featured review says "I bought my wallet in 2022 and I've been enjoying it ever since! It's still very functional, it has a bit of discoloration" (featured review, ridge.com product page, captured 2026-10-23, `business/brief.md` addendum). It shows the asset could exist. Ridge could ask a verified reviewer like this one to take part; nobody has, and the review itself cannot be used.

- Bellroy runs worn-wallet proof in leather across several ads, which are one brand's variants and one observation of the concept: 1046004753909784 (static-before-after, "DAY 1 | DAY 1000", 773 days active at capture) and 412656131438903 (video-demo, a red wallet stamped "DAY 2898 / DAY 3075", 773 days active) are the two at 773 days; 912140034982191 (new and worn pair, 216 days), 1497017115093052 ("Wallets that last. And last. And last.", 363 days) and 2165783148149714 (a worn red wallet in hand, 6 days) are the rest. Days active are facts, not verdicts.
- Nomad 1781413329550236 (keyword neighbor, phone cases, "Your Patina, Your Story", Day 1 and Day 100; the ads.csv row notes the two cases look almost identical). Astrid Leather 1741809623402993 (neighbor, "7+ years and counting!" as a card title, with no object shown for the years).
- The difference: metal, not leather; a named owner, not an anonymous hand; calendar years backed by an order record, not a day counter with no source; and no claim that wear looks good. Bellroy sells patina ("Scuffs and scrapes and creases that mean it could only ever be yours", 412656131438903). Ours shows scratches and calls them scratches, which repels the patina buyer honestly (p2 poor-fit signals).

Substantiation Ridge must hold before launch, because one dated wallet still working conveys durability and the FTC expects the advertiser to know what is typical (`library/platform/ftc-endorsements.md`, "Typical results, or say what is typical"):

- Ridge's own warranty and support records for the aluminum Ridge Wallet over the same span of years as the wallet shown: claims by failure (screws loosening, elastic, plates, finish) as a share of units sold in that period. The AI review summary already reports "screws loosening over time" (observed; frequency unknown).
- More than one owner: several dated, owner-carried wallets of similar age (five is the builder's placeholder), photographed the same way, so Ridge can see whether the one in the ad is typical.
- If either shows the wallet in the ad is better than typical, the ad does not run as written. A "results not typical" line does not fix it; the copy would have to say clearly what is typical, close to the claim, and Ridge would have to be able to say it.

Interrogation R6's pre-spend check (five recent buyers asked whether they would mind theirs looking like this) is a separate test: it asks whether the wear repels buyers, not whether it is typical.

Never in this concept: "lasts forever", "built for life" as the headline, any count of owners, any lifespan figure, any statement that wear is covered, and any word that moves from this wallet to Ridge wallets in general ("every", "all", "a Ridge lasts", "they last").

## Six hooks

Bracketed fields are placeholders filled only from the owner's order record and the photograph; no hook runs with a bracket in it.

1. (`day-1-vs-day-30`) "[Order year] and [photo year]. The same Ridge, the same owner, the plates as they are."
2. (`confession-or-pratfall`) "The plates scratch. This Ridge has been in [first name]'s pocket since [month, year]."
3. (`story-opener`) "[Month, year]: [first name] moved the cards into this aluminum Ridge. They are still in it."
4. (`question`) "What does [first name]'s Ridge wallet look like after [N] years in one pocket? This."
5. (`specific-number`) "[First name]'s Ridge: [N] years, [N] elastic bands, the same plates."
6. (`comparison`) "[First name]'s Ridge, ordered [month, year], beside a new one in the same finish."

Three tests, once the brackets are filled: each hook pictures one wallet in one hand with a year on it; each can be checked against the owner's order record and the photograph (hook 5 holds only if the photograph shows the same plates and the band count is the owner's); and one named owner's dated Ridge is a thing no other brand holds; hooks 5 and 6 now carry the owner, so neither runs for another plate-and-band wallet or reads as Bellroy's new-and-worn pair. No hook says the wallet will last for any reader, that wear is covered, or that it looks better with age. A hook whose bracket cannot be filled from the record is cut, not estimated.

## Policy and truth risk flags

Checked against `library/platform/meta-policy-pitfalls.md`, `library/platform/ftc-endorsements.md` and `library/platform/creator-category-truth-rules.md`.

- Real endorsement: the owner must have carried this wallet and must consent in writing to paid ads. No actor, no composite owner, no first-person line the owner did not say.
- Material connection: staff, family of staff, payment or free product is disclosed in the ad itself, at the top, not after hashtags.
- Typical results: one wallet is one example. Every line stays on this wallet and this owner, with no word that generalizes, and the ad runs only after Ridge holds the substantiation of typical wear listed under Proof available. No lifespan claim; no "results not typical" line in place of that substantiation.
- Warranty: the ad must not imply that wear or an older wallet is covered. Scratches, dents and discoloration are excluded, and wallets bought before 14 October 2025 are outside the Built for Life Warranty (Warranty Policy page, observed). No warranty line on this ad.
- AI and altered media: no generated or retouched wear, no composite of two photographs presented as one moment (Meta's AI and altered-media rules; never fabricate a person or a result).
- Before-and-after: not a restricted category on Meta, but fair context applies: same light, dates on both wallets (`library/copy/format-taxonomy.md`).
- Personal attributes: none; the hooks name the object and the owner, never the viewer. None identified.
- Customer language: the 2022 review is insight only. It is never quoted, paraphrased as a testimonial or attributed in the ad.
