## The one idea

Tell the professional the exact carry and its limit instead of "slim": up to 12 cards and IDs in Ridge Wallet 2.0, and nothing past that. The cash (in the strap or the clip) joins the line only when a take shows 12 cards and folded bills in one wallet, as interrogation moonshot M2 requires.

## The mechanism

All inferred; every effect below is a hypothesis until a readout.

- The psychological job: before thinness matters, the viewer must believe his fixed carry fits. "Slim" can read to him as "holds less than I need" (opposite pass row 1, inferred); a count answers that doubt with something he can check against his own cards.
- Make it precise and concrete (`library/psychology/shotton-illusion-of-choice.md`): a number with a unit ("up to 12 cards and IDs") is easier to believe and to check than an adjective.
- A stated limit as a pratfall (`library/psychology/shotton-choice-factory.md`, the pratfall effect): saying where it stops makes the rest believable and lets the wrong buyer leave without being insulted.
- The anxiety force (`library/psychology/jtbd-four-forces.md`): p4 fears that twelve is not enough and that the ID must come out of the stack every time; the ad states the count and where the ID sits in it.
- The fit objection (`library/sales/objection-taxonomy.md`, fit) answered before it is raised.
- Satisficing (`library/psychology/satisficing.md`): he is avoiding the catastrophe of a wallet that cannot hold the day's carry, not hunting the best one.
- Copy move: every line names Ridge Wallet 2.0 and carries a count or an object (the ID, the cards, the limit), and says "up to" or "stops at", never "fits 12 comfortably". The statics say nothing about cash, because the page states the card count and the cash attachments separately and does not say both fit at the maximum.
