## The one idea

State the price once with its reason, $95 with the 99 days and the warranty terms printed under it, and nothing else: no code, no struck price, no "up to".

## The mechanism

All inferred; every effect below is a hypothesis until a readout.

- The psychological job: the skeptic must stop reading the price as a gadget's price. To him a deep discount confirms the gadget suspicion (p3 purchase row, inferred); a lifetime warranty on an object marked down says "buy once" and "wait for the sale" at the same time (interrogation, Part A problem 2, inferred).
- Price is information (`library/psychology/price-is-information.md`): the price is the first copy read, and constant discounting stops the price carrying information. The same file warns that a "was" price must be a real former price.
- Anchoring and framing (`library/psychology/anchoring-and-framing.md`): a struck price anchors him on the sale, not on the object; one number with no anchor frames the purchase around what it includes.
- Costly signaling (`library/psychology/costly-signaling.md`): "put the guarantee in full, with its conditions, near the price". A company that prints its terms beside an undiscounted price is making a visible commitment.
- The power of "because" (`library/psychology/shotton-choice-factory.md`): a reason beside the price raises acceptance; here the reason is the terms, not a claim about quality.
- Copy move: price largest, then three lines of terms with dates, then nothing. The card says what $95 includes on day 1, by day 99 and if the wallet is lost.

Insight behind the hooks, never ad copy: one featured review says "I questioned myself numerous times about spending the money on something I have not seen or how it works", and another says "Simply thought that the $5 Amazon one would be the same build quality" (featured reviews, ridge.com product page, captured 2026-10-23, `business/brief.md` addendum). The doubt is about what the money buys; the card answers with terms, not with a lower number. No retailer and no knockoff is named in the ad.

## Six hooks

Every hook that names a fee or a warranty term runs only on a card that also carries the review condition and the dated line ("Claims are reviewed and can be denied; Ridge may ask for proof of purchase and photos. Terms as of 23 October 2026; they can change.").

1. (`offer-led`) "$95 for the wallet, no code. If it is lost, today's replacement fee is $17.95, up to twice."
2. (`three-reasons`) "$95, and three things that come with it: a return you can start within 99 days of the order, free defective parts, a $17.95 fee today to replace a lost one."
3. (`unpopular-opinion`) "Unpopular opinion: a $95 wallet with a $17.95 replacement fee today, if it is lost, should not need a coupon."
4. (`question`) "$95 for a Ridge. What if a part turns out defective? Ridge replaces the part free."
5. (`the-real-reason`) "The real reason to pay $95 is printed under it: start a return within 99 days of the order."
6. (`contrarian-or-myth`) "You do not need a code for this wallet. It is $95, and you can start a return within 99 days of the order."

Three tests: each hook carries the price and one checkable term ($17.95 for a lost wallet, a free defective part, 99 days from the order); each can be checked against the product page and the terms on the day it runs; and $95 with Ridge's own fee and window is not a line another brand could paste. Hooks 1, 3 and 6 are true only while Ridge runs this wallet with no code and no sale for the flight; they stop the day a code or sale appears. No hook names a deadline or a former price, and none says a replacement is automatic: hooks 1 and 3 state the fee, and the card states the review.

## The CTA

Button: `Shop now`. A price-stated CTA (`library/copy/cta-patterns.md`): "$95. Start a return within 99 days of the order." Most-aware viewers are ready for the cart; the price is already on the card, so the button hides nothing.

## Landing continuation

The click goes to the Ridge Wallet 2.0 aluminum product page (`business/site/product-aluminum-black/landing/page.txt`). At capture its first screen (`fold.png`) showed "$95" struck, "$76", "20% Off", "On Sale" and a sitewide "UP TO 40% OFF" banner. Against a card that says "$95, no code", continuity is broken, which is why the concept is held. It becomes strong only on a day the page shows one price, $95, with no sale banner on that wallet. A separate landing page at $95 while the main page shows $76 would be worse, not better: two prices for one wallet on one site.

## Policy and truth risk flags

Checked against `library/platform/meta-policy-pitfalls.md`, `library/platform/ftc-endorsements.md` and `library/platform/creator-category-truth-rules.md`.

- Price accuracy: the ad's price must match the page on every day it runs. A mismatch is a misleading claim on Meta and a continuity break. Held until Ridge decides.
- Former prices: any struck price Ridge keeps, on the page or in a test arm, must be a real former price with documentation (`library/psychology/price-is-information.md`). "Up to" must cover a real share of items. If Ridge cannot document it, that arm cannot run.
- "No code": true only if Ridge issues no code for this wallet during the flight, email included. The footer's "exclusive email only deals" makes this a live risk.
- Urgency and scarcity: none. No countdown, no end date, no "limited".
- Terms: the warranty and return terms are as of 2026-10-23, "subject to change", and rechecked at launch. The $17.95 fee is for wallets under $150 and is the fee at claim time, so hooks say "today".
- Superlatives: none. The product page's "industry-leading" is not repeated; where the card names the warranty it uses Ridge's program name, "Built for Life Warranty".
- Personal attributes: none; no hook names the viewer's finances or habits.
- Endorsements: none; the two review lines above are insight only.
