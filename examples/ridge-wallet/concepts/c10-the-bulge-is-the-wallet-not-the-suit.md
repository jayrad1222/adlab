## The one idea

The bulge in the jacket is the wallet, not the suit: name the object and the pocket, never the person, and show one documented carry (an ID, cards, cash) photographed in the same jacket, first in a bifold and then in a Ridge.

## The mechanism

All inferred; every effect below is a hypothesis until a readout.

- The psychological job: before any spec lands, p4 must recognise the pocket as his and the wallet as the thing in it that shows. He wants to look like someone whose things are in order (p4 emotional identity, inferred); the ad must never suggest he does not.
- Context beats personality (the fundamental attribution error, `library/psychology/shotton-choice-factory.md`): point at the object in the pocket, not the person or the tailoring. This is also the policy-safe frame.
- Context changes value (`library/psychology/context-changes-value.md`): the jacket pocket, not a beige tile, is where the wallet's size matters; Ridge's tiles show the object alone (1028460456846299, observed).
- Don't design for average (`library/psychology/dont-design-for-average.md`): one exact situation, the inside pocket of a jacket carrying an ID, cards and cash, that the wider middle recognises.
- Make the invisible visible (`library/psychology/make-the-invisible-visible.md`): the photo pair shows the difference instead of claiming it; the words only say what was photographed.
- The push named (`library/psychology/jtbd-four-forces.md`): the hook names the push; the photograph and the spec carry the pull.
- Mirror at the trigger stage (`library/strategy/levinger-three-realities-teep.md`) in a native register (`library/strategy/hott-ugly-ads.md`): a plain problem card and a text card beside the photo card.
- Copy move: the hook names the jacket, the carry and Ridge or its plates. No hook states a cause ("the real reason ... is the fold") and no line says "flat", "slim", "no bulge" or "disappears". The comparison is the photographs', and the copy claims only what they show for that one carry.

Insight behind the hooks, never ad copy: one featured review says the wallet "gives me just enough room to store about 5 cards comfortably" (featured review, ridge.com product page, captured 2026-10-23, `business/brief.md` addendum). So the copy says "up to 12" and never implies twelve is comfortable or thin, and the documented carry for the photographs is chosen and stated, not maximized.

## Policy and truth risk flags

- Personal attributes: every hook names the jacket, the pocket or the wallet. None says "you" with a condition, none describes a body, weight or posture, none shames the wearer. "Is your wallet ruining your suit?" is the pattern this concept avoids (interrogation R4 risk).

- Capacity: "up to 12" as the product page says, for Ridge Wallet 2.0 only; never "15", never "12 comfortably", never "12 cards and the cash at once" unless the documented carry shows it.
- Sensational content: no fear frame, no skimming threat, no "walking target". RFID is not mentioned.
- Staged photographs: the pair is real wallets with a documented carry, same jacket, same light and setup, unretouched, dated; no AI or composite.
- Endorsements: none used; the review line above is insight only.
