## Angle family

## The one idea

Print what Ridge's "lifetime" actually means, from its own Warranty Policy page: a defective part is replaced free, a lost, stolen or accidentally damaged aluminum wallet is replaced for a $17.95 fee up to two times, and scratches are not covered, all dated to the day the terms were read.

## The mechanism

All inferred; every effect below is a hypothesis until a readout.

- The psychological job: before the word "lifetime" can do any work, p2 must believe it has edges he can check. His fear is that "lifetime" means a support ticket (`personas/p2-third-wallet-in-six-years.md`, anxiety, inferred from interrogation answer 4). An edge he can read answers that fear; another adjective does not.
- Costly signaling (`library/psychology/costly-signaling.md`): a guarantee with stated conditions is a signal; the file's own backfire note is that "a guarantee with hidden terms is not a signal". Printing the fee and the cap is the visible cost.
- The pratfall effect (`library/psychology/shotton-choice-factory.md`): saying "scratches are not covered" before anything else makes the covered cases more believable.
- The accusation audit (`library/sales/voss-toolkit.md`) and the trust and tried-before objections (`library/sales/objection-taxonomy.md`, "a guarantee with no hidden conditions"): say the doubt first, then the term that answers it.
- Make it precise (`library/psychology/shotton-illusion-of-choice.md`): "$17.95" is easier to believe and remember than "a small fee".
- Satisficing (`library/psychology/satisficing.md`): the catastrophe p2 is avoiding is starting over with a fourth wallet; the loss-and-theft line takes it off the table before any benefit is sold.
- Copy move: the headline is the fee or the exclusion. The body names the three cases in Ridge's own terms. The footer dates the terms and says Ridge can change them. No adjective about the warranty, no comparison with anyone else's.

Insight behind the hooks, never ad copy: the AI-generated review summary on the product page says "a few mention durability concerns with screws loosening over time", and a featured review of a wallet bought in 2022 says "it has a bit of discoloration" (featured review, ridge.com product page, captured 2026-10-23, `business/brief.md` addendum). The first is why the free-parts line matters; the second is why the scratch line has to be said out loud.

## Six hooks

Every hook that names a fee runs only on a card that also carries the review condition and the dated line ("Claims are reviewed and can be denied; Ridge may ask for proof of purchase and photos. Terms as of 23 October 2026; they can change."), and only for the aluminum Ridge Wallet listed at $95.

1. (`specific-number`) "Lost or stolen? Today's fee for a replacement Ridge is $17.95. Up to two times."
2. (`confession-or-pratfall`) "Ridge's lifetime warranty does not cover scratches. A lost wallet is covered, for $17.95 today."
3. (`question`) "What does Ridge's 'lifetime' cost? A defective part: nothing. A lost wallet: $17.95 at today's fee."
4. (`three-reasons`) "Three answers from Ridge's wallet warranty: a defective part, free; a lost wallet, $17.95 today; scratches, not covered."
5. (`negative-or-warning`) "Before you buy a wallet 'for life', find the fee for a lost one. Ridge's is $17.95 today."
6. (`identification`) "Third wallet in six years? Ridge's wallet warranty prints the fee: $17.95 to replace a lost one today, up to twice."

Three tests: each hook pictures one event (a part failing, a wallet lost or stolen, a scratch) or one number ($17.95, two replacements); each can be checked line by line against the Warranty Policy page on the day the ad runs; and the fee, the cap and the scratch exclusion together are Ridge's published terms, so no other brand could paste them unchanged. No hook says a claim is automatic, that the fee is fixed, or that wear is covered. Hook 6 names a purchase history, not a trait of the viewer. Hook 4 now carries the three printed terms instead of the words "lifetime warranty", so it no longer runs unchanged for another brand.

## Landing continuation

The click goes to the Ridge Wallet 2.0 aluminum product page (`business/site/product-aluminum-black/landing/page.txt`). Its first screen (the captured mobile fold, `fold.png`) shows the struck $95, the $76 sale price, the review count and the add-to-cart button; the spec line "Built for Life Warranty" sits just below the button, and the fees and exclusions are one link away on the Warranty Policy page. Continuity is weak, not broken: the warranty is named, its terms are not. Two product-page lines conflict with this concept: "industry-leading Built for Life Warranty" (an unsourced superlative) and "No matter what comes your way, Ridge has your back" (broader than terms that exclude scratches, fire, flood and vandalism). Ridge decides those edits; three lines near the button (free parts, $17.95 for loss, scratches excluded) would make continuity strong. Landing on the Warranty Policy page itself is rejected: it has no way to buy.

## Policy and truth risk flags

Checked against `library/platform/meta-policy-pitfalls.md`, `library/platform/ftc-endorsements.md` and `library/platform/creator-category-truth-rules.md`.

- Misleading claims: every term is quoted from a page that says it is "subject to change". The ad carries the capture date and is rechecked against the live page the day it launches and at each refresh. If a fee, cap or exclusion has changed, the ad stops until it is rewritten.
- Fee at claim time: the fee that applies is the one in force when the claim is made, not when the wallet was bought. Hooks say "today's fee"; no hook says the fee is fixed.
- Claim approval: Ridge "reserves the right to deny any Built for Life Warranty claim" and may ask for proof of purchase and photographs. Both statics carry the review condition and the dated line (the offer line on the three-reasons card, the footer on the text-only card); no hook says a replacement is automatic.
- Fee band: the $17.95 line is for wallets under $150. The concept runs only for the aluminum Ridge Wallet listed at $95; carbon fiber at $150 carries $27.95 and is excluded from this ad. The ad itself states no product price.
- Comparison: no competitor's warranty is named, quoted or implied as worse. Hook 5 warns about a category word, not a brand.
- Landing superlative: "industry-leading" on the product page is the member's unsourced claim on the page the ad sends people to: `landing-mismatch` until Ridge edits or sources it.
- Personal attributes: hook 6 names a purchase history, not a trait, a health or a finance state. None identified.
- Endorsements: none used. The two review lines above are insight only and never appear in the ad.
