## The one idea

For someone buying for a man who says he needs nothing, Ridge narrows the choice to three named finishes (Royal Black, Alpine Navy, Matte Olive) and the gift can be returned or exchanged within 99 days of the order; an engraved one cannot be returned (the page says nothing about exchanging it), so the concept skips engraving.

## The mechanism

All inferred; every effect below is a hypothesis until a readout.

- The psychological job: the giver must feel the choice is safe before it can feel thoughtful. The fear is the gift that goes in a drawer, or a wrong guess that cannot be undone (p5 anxiety, inferred).
- Satisficing (`library/psychology/satisficing.md`): for a gift, a safe choice beats a best choice; the exchange window removes the catastrophe of guessing wrong.
- Choice overload and "make it easy" (`library/psychology/shotton-illusion-of-choice.md`) with the Goldilocks effect (`library/psychology/shotton-choice-factory.md`): the product page lists one finish "+ 27 colors" (observed); three named options are a choice the giver can make.
- Loss aversion and the reference point (`library/psychology/loss-aversion-and-prospect-theory.md`): with an exchange available, a wrong finish is a swap, not a loss.
- Context changes value (`library/psychology/context-changes-value.md`): the occasion, not the object, sets the frame; the ad opens on the gift problem, never on the wallet's features.
- Implicit goal (`library/psychology/barden-implicit-goals.md`): belonging, being the one who knew him, with security second (p5 file, inferred).
- Copy move: name the giver's situation; give three finish names; state the exchange window counted from the order; warn before engraving, because a personalized wallet cannot come back. The recipient is "he" in the copy; the viewer is never addressed by gender or by any personal attribute.

Insight: no featured review on the captured product page speaks to a giver (`personas/coverage.md`, addendum: "Nothing for p5"). The concept rests on the terms and the situation only.

## Six hooks

1. (`callout`) "For someone who says he needs nothing: a Ridge wallet in Royal Black, Alpine Navy or Matte Olive, and 99 days from your order to exchange it."
2. (`pov`) "POV: he opens the Alpine Navy and asks for black. The exchange window is 99 days from your order."
3. (`question`) "Which Ridge wallet: Royal Black, Alpine Navy or Matte Olive? Guess wrong, and exchange it within 99 days of your order."
4. (`negative-or-warning`) "Before you engrave the wallet: a personalized Ridge cannot be returned. A plain one can, within 99 days of your order."
5. (`offer-led`) "Free U.S. shipping on the exchange: send back the Alpine Navy within 99 days of your order, and once the return is approved, the Royal Black ships free."
6. (`three-reasons`) "Three things to know before buying him a Ridge wallet: Royal Black, Alpine Navy or Matte Olive; 99 days from your order; no engraving."

Three tests: each hook pictures a finish by name, an engraving, or the exchange counted from an order; each can be checked against the Returns & Exchanges page and the finish names on the product and wallets pages; and three named Ridge finishes with Ridge's exchange terms are not a line another brand could paste. The callout alone ("someone who says he needs nothing") would fail the falsify and nobody-else tests, so no hook uses it without the finishes or the 99 days beside it. Every hook that states an exchange runs only on a card that carries the page's conditions, "usable condition" and "after your return is approved" (the reasons or the footer above). Hook 5's Royal Black ships only if it is in stock (the stock flag). Hook 2 is a staged scene, labeled as such in the brief; it is never presented as a real customer's moment. No hook promises delivery, a reaction, or that the recipient can start the exchange.
