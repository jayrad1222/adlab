## The one idea

The day the old bifold splits at the fold is the day to switch: name that moment, then say how little the switch commits him to, because a return on the Ridge can be started within 99 days of the order.

## The mechanism

All inferred; every effect below is a hypothesis until a readout.

- The psychological job: before any claim about the wallet lands, the viewer must think "that is my wallet this week", then "switching can be undone". The ad shows the moment, then the exit.
- The push named at its peak (`library/psychology/jtbd-four-forces.md`): the split is the push. The return window shrinks the anxiety (will it hold what he needs, is it a fad), and the habit ("it still works", p1 habit force) loses its argument on the day the wallet stops working.
- A small life transition (`library/psychology/shotton-choice-factory.md`, habit): habits are hardest to break except when circumstances change; a wallet that has just failed is a small change of circumstance.
- Mirror at the trigger stage (`library/strategy/levinger-three-realities-teep.md`): show the situation before the product, because a problem-aware viewer recognizes the moment faster than the category.
- Availability and vividness (`library/psychology/availability-and-vividness.md`): an object and its failure point (the fold) are easier to picture than "bulk".
- Write to the edge (`library/psychology/dont-design-for-average.md`): the bifold split at the fold is the edge; owners of fat or worn wallets recognize themselves from it.
- Ugly register (`library/strategy/hott-ugly-ads.md`): a text card does not look like the catalogue tiles in Ridge's own feed.
- Copy move: the hook names the object and where it failed, then a Ridge part (plates, the elastic band) or the return window from the order. The old wallet is treated with respect in tone (no mockery, no "embarrassing" bulge; the persona's emotional identity), not with a line any brand could run. No claim that the Ridge lasts longer, and no word about the viewer.

## Six hooks

1. (`problem-naming`) "The bifold split at the fold. Next: a Ridge, and 99 days from the order to start a return."
2. (`callout`) "If the old bifold just split at the fold: the Ridge that replaces it has a return window of 99 days from the order."
3. (`question`) "Split at the fold. Buy the same bifold again, or try plates and an elastic band?"
4. (`contrarian-or-myth`) "A split bifold does not need a twin. Try plates and an elastic band."
5. (`pov`) "POV: the split bifold, emptied on the table. Up to 12 cards go under the Ridge's elastic."
6. (`specific-number`) "99 days from the order to decide if the split bifold was the last bifold."

Three tests: each hook pictures an object at its failure point (the fold); each pairs it with something checkable on Ridge's pages (a return started within 99 days of the order, plates and an elastic band, up to 12 cards under the elastic); and each names the Ridge or one of its parts, so a leather brand or another card holder could not run the line unchanged. No hook names a trait of the viewer, and none promises more than the terms or the spec state. Every hook that implies the return runs only on a creative that carries the small print.

Written CTA, the last lines of the primary text, in the reversal pattern (`library/copy/cta-patterns.md`): "If it is wrong, start a return within 99 days of the order. U.S. returns use a free prepaid USPS label. Not for personalized, warranty-claim or certain limited-edition items. Ridge return terms as of 23 October 2026; they can change." The reversal pattern is listed for product-aware viewers; it is used at the problem stage here because the exit is the idea. The exit is stated; it is not called risk-free.
