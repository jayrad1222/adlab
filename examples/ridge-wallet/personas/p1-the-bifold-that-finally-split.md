# p1: the bifold that finally split

Version 1, 2026-10-23. Prior versions are kept below the line at the end of this file; a revision never deletes a prior version.

Built from the market map's open situation "man replacing a worn bifold after years (the switch moment)" (section 5, 0 roster advertisers) and the interrogation's hypothetical customers (the man whose back-pocket wallet ruined a suit; the new father simplifying what he carries). Kept separate from p2 (the durability buyer) because the proof differs: p1 needs to see the switch (what fits, what gets left behind, the first week); p2 needs to see the object after years. Their push overlaps (a worn wallet) but their anxiety does not.

## Name as a situation

A man whose leather bifold has worn through at the fold after years in a back pocket; it is fat with receipts and cards he does not use, and this week it finally split or embarrassed him, so he is looking at slim wallets for the first time and is not sure what he would do with everything in the old one. **Inferred** from the brief's "men who carry too much in a back pocket," the interrogation default "something broke" (Q9), and keyword-neighbor ads that name the moment (Kruz 921439164088469 "replacing a classic bulky bifold"; Astrid 1741809623402993 "ordering this to replace their old"). No customer has been interviewed.

## Job to be done

When my old wallet gives out, I want to replace it with something I will not have to think about again, so I can stop carrying a brick in my back pocket without losing anything I actually need. **Inferred.**

## Trigger moment

The wallet fails or embarrasses him: the stitching splits, a card cracks at the fold, the bulge shows through a suit or ruins the line of trousers, or he sits on it one too many times. Secondary triggers (hypothetical, from the interrogation's five customers): a new baby and the urge to carry less; a trip. **Inferred.** Sources: interrogation Q9 default ("something broke"); brief, "What is missing" ("the ritual of switching wallets"); Kruz 921703880290700 primary text "formato clasico de una billetera con un diseno mas delgado" (a keyword neighbor in Costa Rica, 2026-09-28). No roster ad names the trigger (market map section 4, "The switch").

## Four forces

- **Push** (what is wrong with the current situation, in their words): the bifold is thick, worn and full of things he never uses; it sits badly in the back pocket. **Inferred.** The only market phrases close to this are competitor lines: "Une bosse dans la poche ?" (a bump in the pocket; Les astuces de Jerome 4169847356647263, 2026-05-27), "Most wallets make you dig" (Dango 1736591054090233, 2026-10-02), "Stop carrying bulk" (Ridge 1668472461558360, 2026-10-07). No customer language collected.
- **Pull** (what the new solution promises them, in their words): a flat front pocket, one object that holds the cards and some cash, and a wallet that will not wear out again. **Inferred** from Ridge's own promise "Slim, RFID-blocking, built to last for life" (ridge.com, 2026-10-23) and Kruz 921439164088469 "llevar lo esencial sin cargar de mas" (carry the essentials without carrying extra).
- **Anxiety** (what they fear about switching): where do the receipts, the loyalty cards, the folded cash and the photo go; will it hold everything he needs; will he look like he is carrying a gadget; will the cards fall out. **Inferred** from interrogation Q4 (inferred fears: "the wallet feels like a gadget, the cards fall out") and Q6 ("moving cards out of the old wallet is a ritual nobody on the site describes"). The anxiety about fit is also visible in a customer comment Andar quotes: "a small slim wallet that has a full bill compartment and it's IMPOSSIBLE to find" (1520735273186403, 2026-08-11).
- **Habit** (what keeps them in the current situation): the bifold still works, more or less; he has carried the same shape since his first wallet; the switch takes an evening of sorting that he keeps putting off. **Hypothetical.** Interrogation Q2 default: "they muddle through with a cheaper tool; nothing costs them a fat pocket and a bit of embarrassment."

## Current workaround, including nothing

Nothing, or a like-for-like replacement: another leather bifold from a department store or Amazon, or the same model again. Some move the fat wallet to a jacket pocket. What not acting costs him, in his words: unknown; the interrogation default says "a fat pocket and a bit of embarrassment" (hypothetical). Evidence that would fill this: product reviews that start "my old wallet..." and Reddit everyday-carry threads on first slim wallets.

## Awareness distribution

Mostly problem-aware (the wallet is failing; "slim wallet" is a vague category to him), with a smaller solution-aware group who has seen a metal wallet on a friend or in a gift guide and a few product-aware who already know the Ridge name from years of ads (Ridge reports 1,891 ads on keyword search; market map section 1). **Inferred**; no survey or comment evidence. Ridge's own active ads are all product-aware or solution-aware (catalogue tiles, Phantom, power bank), so this persona meets Ridge at the wrong stage today.

## Sophistication

He has heard "slim" (7 of 9 roster advertisers) and "RFID" (4 of 9) for years and is numb to both (market map section 3). He has probably seen a mechanism demo (5 of 9 advertisers: the slide, the pockets opening). He has not seen anyone talk about the moment of switching or the back pocket (section 4, both uncontested). Numb to: superlatives ("best wallet ever", Andar 1315996623672010), struck-through prices (Dango 1457214629482883). Not yet heard: what you give up and what you keep. **Inferred** from the roster.

## Emotional identity

"I am the kind of person who carries what he needs and does not fuss about it." The ad must not make him feel he was foolish for carrying a bifold for fifteen years; it should treat the old wallet with respect (it did its job). **Inferred**; no supporting quote. Bellroy's "a pocket-sized reminder of first dates, everyday routines" (412656131438903, 2024-09-09) is the closest market language to how he might feel about the old wallet.

## Implicit goal (Barden)

Explicit goal: a thinner wallet that holds his cards. Implicit goal: **order** (a cleaner version of security: fewer things, each in its place, nothing to think about). Secondary: autonomy (he chose it, nobody gave it to him). The visual world should be calm, flat, daylight, one object on a table beside the emptied bifold, not a dark gear shot. **Inferred.** Note that Ridge's current visual world (dark finishes and metal, brief "Tone and visual world") promises engineered identity instead, which serves the gear-minded man the market already covers.

## Status game

He wants to look, to his partner and to himself, like someone who sorted a small nuisance without making a production of it. He would look foolish if the new wallet turned out to be a fad he abandoned in a month (back to the bifold) or if he had to explain the gadget at a restaurant. **Inferred** from interrogation Q1 default ("someone who has their act together, to their partner and their peers").

## Proof required

He needs to see the switch itself: an emptied bifold beside the metal wallet with every card accounted for (which eleven went in, which went in the drawer), the cash in the clip, and what week one feels like. He needs a person like him, not a creator. What the stand-in brand can supply today (observed on ridge.com, 2026-10-23): the 99-day trial and the lifetime warranty as risk reversal; "Holds 1-12 cards" as a capacity claim. Missing: a real switch shown by a real owner; any customer quote about the first week; a photo of the two wallets side by side. The asset catalog has not been built yet (PROGRESS: assets pending).

## Poor-fit signals

The man who carries coins, a chequebook, twenty loyalty cards or thick folded cash will fight the elastic and return it (interrogation Q8, inferred). The man who wants soft leather and the feel of a bifold will not be happy with plates. An ad that says plainly "eleven cards and the bills; the coins and the receipts stay home" repels him without insulting him (market map hypothesis 4).

## What nobody says to them yet

All hypothetical; evidence is the market map's uncontested claims (section 4) and the gaps above.

1. The switch, shown: the evening you empty the bifold, what goes in, what goes in the drawer. Evidence: section 4 "The switch", interrogation Q6, format gap "no problem-first static".
2. The back pocket and the posture: what the object ends, not what it is. Evidence: section 4 "The back pocket"; conventions table row 8 (7 of 9 advertisers headline slim and RFID instead).
3. Respect for the old wallet: "it did fifteen years; this one does the rest" (heritage without insulting him). Evidence: the emotional identity above; no ad in the sample speaks to the old wallet.
4. Week one, in order: what feels odd on day one and what feels normal by day ten. Evidence: section 4 "What falls out, and what does not"; hypothesis 3 (the trial as a story).
5. What you give up, stated plainly (coins, receipts) so the right buyer selects himself. Evidence: hypothesis 4; Andar's cash-and-coins callout is the only honest capacity line in the roster (1428488879491057) and it is for a different buyer.
