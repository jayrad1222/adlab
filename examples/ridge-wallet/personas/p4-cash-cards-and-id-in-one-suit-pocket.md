# p4: cash, cards and ID in one suit pocket

Version 1, 2026-10-23. Prior versions are kept below the line at the end of this file; a revision never deletes a prior version.

Built from the market map's open situation "professional who needs cash, cards and ID in one slim object" (section 5, 0 roster advertisers; Andar partially, for women) and the interrogation's hypothetical traveler who lost a card to a skimmer. Kept separate from p1 because the job differs: p1 wants to carry less; p4 needs to carry a fixed set (cash for tips and taxis, an ID shown often, a work badge, several cards) and wants it flat in a jacket or trouser pocket. The proof he needs is capacity shown honestly, not the switch.

## Name as a situation

A man who wears a jacket or a suit most days, travels for work, shows his ID at airports and front desks, still carries some cash, and needs all of it in one object that does not spoil the line of the pocket; a card holder is too small and a bifold too thick. **Inferred** from Blessedeal 1687936999471670 ("Designed for men who want security without the bulk", "From boardrooms to airport lounges", 2026-09-17), Urban Traveller & Co. 982877924781062 ("keep cash, receipts, and business cards organized", 2026-07-22), Kitchen & Home Hub 1624052195780903 ("commuting, business, and travel", 2026-09-18), and the brief's "Who it seems to be for". No customer has been interviewed.

## Job to be done

When I am dressed for work and moving between meetings, airports and taxis, I want everything I have to show or pay with in one flat object, so I can get through the day without digging, bulging or losing a card. **Inferred.**

## Trigger moment

A card skimmed or lost on a trip; a new job with a badge and a dress code; a suit pocket bulge noticed in a mirror or a photo; a tailor's comment. **Inferred.** Sources: interrogation's hypothetical "the traveler who lost a card to a skimmer"; Blessedeal 1687936999471670 "Your wallet is a walking target" (2026-09-17); Bellroy 861547736912965 "Secure, organized, stealthy" (2026-03-19). No roster ad names the trigger for wallets.

## Four forces

- **Push** (what is wrong with the current situation, in their words): the bifold ruins the pocket line and makes him dig at the counter; the card holder he tried had nowhere for cash or the ID he shows ten times a day. **Inferred.** Market phrasing: "bulky wallets with cheap RFID sleeves? They ruin your suit line and still leave you guessing" (Blessedeal 1687936999471670, 2026-09-17); "Most wallets make you dig" (Dango 1736591054090233). No customer language collected.
- **Pull** (what the new solution promises them, in their words): one flat object, cards protected, ID visible, cash in the clip. **Inferred** from Ridge "designed to carry cards and cash safely and securely" (1028460456846299, 2026-09-17) and Urban Traveller 982877924781062 "A dedicated billfold and extra compartments keep cash, receipts, and business cards organized".
- **Anxiety** (what they fear about switching): that twelve cards is not enough; that cash in a clip looks cheap or falls out; that the ID has to come out of the stack every time; that metal looks like a toy with a suit. **Inferred** from interrogation Q4 and from Andar's only honest capacity line, aimed at women: "those of us who still carry cash. And coins. And more than three cards" (1428488879491057, 2026-10-17). The comment Andar quotes ("a small slim wallet that has a full bill compartment and it's IMPOSSIBLE to find", 1520735273186403) is this anxiety in a customer's words for a different product.
- **Habit** (what keeps them in the current situation): the bifold has a place for everything; a second card holder in another pocket for the overflow; moving the wallet to the jacket. **Inferred.**

## Current workaround, including nothing

A thick leather bifold moved to the inside jacket pocket; or a card holder plus a money clip plus the ID loose; or a Secrid-style mechanical case (Urban Traveller 982877924781062 shows the sixteen-card version at a retailer). Nothing costs him a bulge in the suit and the daily dig. **Inferred**; no quote. Evidence that would fill this: Ridge reviews from people who mention "suit", "work", "ID", "cash clip"; Reddit r/malefashionadvice and r/EDC threads on wallets for suits; comments under Blessedeal's ad.

## Awareness distribution

Mostly solution-aware: he has tried or looked at card holders and knows the category's limit (no cash, no ID window). Some problem-aware (the bulge, the skimming scare) and some product-aware who know Ridge from gift guides. **Inferred**; no survey evidence. No roster ad speaks to him at the solution-aware stage for wallets; the nearest are keyword neighbors.

## Sophistication

Medium. He has heard "slim" and "RFID" (market map section 3); the skimming fear has been sold to him by neighbors (Blessedeal, Kitchen & Home Hub) with emoji and "military-grade" claims he discounts. He has seen capacity claims as numbers ("Holds 1-12 cards", Ridge 1402989268694829; "room for up to 16 cards", Urban Traveller 982877924781062). Numb to: security theatre, "boardrooms to airport lounges" copy. Not yet seen: a real working day's carry laid out and loaded into the wallet, with the ID and the cash handled. **Inferred** from the roster.

## Emotional identity

"I am the kind of person who is prepared and does not fumble." The ad must not show him digging or flustered; it should show the day running smoothly with the object barely noticed. **Inferred**; no quote. The neighbors' line "security without the bulk" (Blessedeal 1687936999471670) is the market's version of this identity.

## Implicit goal (Barden)

Explicit goal: one slim object for cash, cards and ID. Implicit goal: **discipline** (everything in its place, carried on purpose), with security second (cards protected, nothing lost). The visual world should be a working day: a jacket pocket, a front desk, an airport tray, a taxi, in natural light; not a stump in the woods (Dango 1822401658948250) and not a dark finish shot. **Inferred.** Bellroy's "Secure, organized, stealthy" (861547736912965) is the closest roster promise and it is for a bag.

## Status game

He wants to look, to colleagues, clients and the person at the front desk, like someone whose things are in order. He would look foolish if the cards fanned onto a counter, if he had to pull the ID from under the band every time, or if a client noticed a gadget. **Inferred** from interrogation Q1 ("someone who has their act together") and Q4.

## Proof required

A real working day's carry laid out (the ID, the badge, eight cards, the cash) and loaded, with the ID shown coming out and going back; a jacket pocket photographed from the side with the bifold and then with the Ridge; a tradesman, a nurse or a pilot showing their own carry ("what's in my wallet", market map section 8 gap). A capacity claim stated small and checkable. What the stand-in brand can supply today (observed, ridge.com 2026-10-23): "Holds 1-12 cards", the money clip and cash strap options on the collections page, the 99-day trial. Missing: any working-day demonstration, any professional owner, any side-by-side pocket photo. The asset catalog has not been built yet.

## Poor-fit signals

The man who carries coins daily, a chequebook, or more than twelve cards; the one who needs a passport in the same object; the one who wants a bill compartment rather than a clip (Andar's Lo exists for exactly that buyer, 3633657103475043). An ad that says "cards and the bills in the clip; coins and the passport go elsewhere" repels him honestly.
