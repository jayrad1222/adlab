# p2: third wallet in six years

Version 1, 2026-10-23. Prior versions are kept below the line at the end of this file; a revision never deletes a prior version.

Built from the market map's "durability buyer tired of replacing wallets" (section 5, served by Bellroy only, in leather) and the brief's "built to last for life" promise. Kept separate from p1 (the switch moment) because the proof differs: p1 needs the switch shown; p2 needs the object after years, dated, and a warranty that is believed. Their push overlaps (a worn wallet) but p2's anxiety is about the next five years, not the first week.

## Name as a situation

Someone who has already replaced a wallet two or three times, each one wearing through at the same place, and who is now choosing on the single question of whether this one will be the last. **Inferred** from Bellroy's persona evidence across six ads ("buyer who has had wallets wear out", 1497017115093052; "someone who has replaced cheap wallets and wants one that ages well", 912140034982191) and the brief's lifetime-warranty promise. No customer has been interviewed.

## Job to be done

When the wallet I bought to last has worn out again, I want to buy one I can stop replacing, so I can be done with the question for good. **Inferred.**

## Trigger moment

The second or third wallet failing the same way; noticing the fold wearing through and remembering the last one; a warranty claim on a previous wallet that went nowhere. **Inferred.** Sources: Bellroy 1497017115093052 "Wallets that last. And last. And last." (2025-10-24) addressed to the buyer who has had wallets wear out; interrogation Q9 default ("something broke"). No roster ad names the trigger for metal.

## Four forces

- **Push** (what is wrong with the current situation, in their words): the wallet he paid for wore out; it was not the last one after all. **Inferred.** Market phrasing: "Wallets that last." (Bellroy 912140034982191, 2026-03-20, implies the alternative); "The cheap bag rips." (Ekster 2117113273022536, 2026-08-06, a different product, same logic). No customer language collected.
- **Pull** (what the new solution promises them, in their words): a wallet that gets better or at least stays the same; a warranty he will never need. **Inferred** from Bellroy 412656131438903 "We are better and better with age" (2024-09-09, 773 days running) and ridge.com "Built to endure. For life" (2026-10-23).
- **Anxiety** (what they fear about switching): "lifetime" means a support ticket and a form; metal scratches and dents and looks worse, not better, after a year; the elastic band is the weak point and wears like the leather fold did. **Inferred** from interrogation Q4 ("the 'lifetime' promise turns into a support ticket") and the fact that the market only shows aging for leather (market map section 4, "The metal after five years").
- **Habit** (what keeps them in the current situation): he knows leather; leather aging is a story he has been told for years (Bellroy, Nomad "Your Patina, Your Story", 1781413329550236). Buying the same material again feels safe even though it failed. **Inferred.**

## Current workaround, including nothing

Buying another leather wallet, a step up in price each time, from a brand that promises aging well. Some buy a cheap one on purpose and expect to replace it. What not acting costs him: another wallet every two to three years and the annoyance of moving everything each time. **Inferred**; no quote. Evidence that would fill this: Amazon one-star reviews of leather bifolds (the failure point), Reddit r/wallets "what lasted longest" threads, Ridge warranty-claim stories if the member can share them with consent.

## Awareness distribution

Mostly solution-aware to product-aware: he has bought wallets deliberately before, knows the slim category and probably the Ridge name, and is weighing materials. A smaller problem-aware group has just noticed the wear and has not yet decided to change material. **Inferred**; no survey evidence. This is the stage Bellroy's six durability ads speak to (product-aware with proof).

## Sophistication

High for this persona. He has seen the day 1 versus day 1000 wallet (Bellroy 1046004753909784, 773 days running), the day counters (412656131438903), and "7+ years and counting" (Astrid 1741809623402993). He has heard "lifetime warranty" from 5 of 9 advertisers (market map section 3) and treats it as table stakes. Numb to: the warranty line by itself, "built to last" as words. Not yet seen: metal after years, a warranty claim shown being honoured, the person who carried it. **Inferred** from the roster.

## Emotional identity

"I am the kind of person who buys once and buys right." The ad must not make him feel he has been fooled before; it should treat his previous wallets as reasonable choices that were let down by the material. **Inferred**; no quote. Closest market language: "Bellroy. Good choice." (1497017115093052, 2025-10-24).

## Implicit goal (Barden)

Explicit goal: a wallet that does not wear out. Implicit goal: **security** (an end to a recurring small failure; a decision he will not revisit). The visual world should be slow, worn, honest: a used wallet in a hand, scratches visible, daylight, no box. **Inferred.** Bellroy already occupies this goal in leather with a slow pan on a worn red wallet (2165783148149714, 2026-10-16); the opposite pass should test whether metal can own the same goal with a harder proof (dated wear) rather than a different goal.

## Status game

He wants to look, to himself and to anyone who asks about the wallet, like a person whose things last because he chose well. He would look foolish if the metal wallet dented or the band went slack within a year, in front of the same people who saw him replace the last one. **Inferred** from interrogation Q1 and Q4 defaults.

## Poor-fit signals

The buyer who wants the object to soften and take on patina will be disappointed by metal that scratches rather than ages; send him to leather without apology. The buyer who expects "lifetime" to mean free replacement for anything: the captured Warranty Policy (2026-10-23) covers loss, theft and accidental damage for a $17.95 fee on wallets under $150, twice at most, and does not cover scratches, dents or discoloration (corrected 2026-10-23; the version-1 line said loss or theft was not covered, before the policy page was read). The buyer who treats the discount banner as the reason to buy; he will churn to the next sale (market map section 7). An ad that shows real scratches and calls them scratches repels the patina buyer honestly.

## What nobody says to them yet

All hypothetical; evidence is the market map's uncontested claims and the gaps above.

1. The metal after five years, dated, in the owner's hand. Evidence: section 4 "The metal after five years" (Bellroy does it for leather; nobody for metal); section 6 proof arms race.
2. What wears and what does not, named honestly: the band is a wear part and is replaced; the plates are not. Evidence: Ridge EU sells a replacement elastic (1616785086769214) but no ad explains why; the objection table above.
3. A warranty claim shown end to end, not just the word. Evidence: 5 of 9 advertisers say "warranty"; none shows one honoured (section 3 and 6).
4. The three-wallets math: what he has spent on wallets that failed, versus one price stated once. Evidence: section 7 (the room above "always on sale" is empty except Bellroy); hypothesis 8. Numbers must come from the member, not be invented.
5. Scratches as a record rather than damage, if the member can stand behind it: metal's version of Bellroy's "could only ever be yours". Evidence: Bellroy owns the leather version (412656131438903); the opposite pass for metal is untested.
