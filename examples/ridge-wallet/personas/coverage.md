## What the table says

- Every open situation is open at the trigger and exploration stages. The roster's formats cluster at purchase (offer cards, catalogue tiles) and at evaluation for one advertiser (Bellroy's worn leather). Levinger's note that small rosters leave exploration and evaluation thin holds here (market map section 8).
- Ridge's own twelve ads touch four of the five personas by accident (catalogue copy about cards and cash, the warranty line, the NFL gift callout) and name none of them. The ads meet p3 at his stage and give him nothing new to believe.
- The proof column is the same object three times: a real, dated, owner-carried wallet with consent (p2, p3, p4) or a real owner's switch (p1, p5). None exists in the asset catalog, which has not been built yet. The assets step should look for this first.
- Awareness splits are the builder's reading of where each situation sits; none has a source. The first customer-language pass (reviews, Reddit everyday-carry threads, gift-guide comments; see each file's language bank) is what turns them from inferred to observed.

## Evidence limits

- No customer language was collected in this run; every language bank is competitor and brand language with sources and dates, plus a list of where to look.
- All video rows are still-reviewed (no transcripts); hooks quoted from videos are on-screen text or captions.
- Neighbor ads (Monos, Blessedeal, Kruz, Jerome, Kitchen & Home Hub, Embrosh, Astrid, Urban Traveller) are keyword matches, several outside the US market; they show how the wider market talks, not what the roster does.
- Ad counts include variants from one advertiser; count the advertisers when saying "the market does X".
