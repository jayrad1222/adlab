# p3: the man who thinks it is a gadget

Version 1, 2026-10-23. Prior versions are kept below the line at the end of this file; a revision never deletes a prior version.

Built from the market map's open situation "skeptic who fears a metal wallet is a gadget" (section 5, 0 advertisers) and interrogation Q4's inferred fears. Kept separate from p1 because his push is weak and his anxiety is the whole story: p1 is pushed by a failing wallet and worried about fit; p3 has seen the product, maybe many times, and has decided it is a toy. The proof he needs is a demonstration of what does not go wrong, plus a person like him who was skeptical.

## Name as a situation

A man who has seen Ridge ads for years, maybe picked one up in a store or at a friend's, and put it back because it looked like a gadget: plates and an elastic band, cards that might slide out, a money clip that could scratch a phone. He is open to a slimmer wallet but not to carrying something that needs explaining. **Inferred** from interrogation Q4 (inferred fears: "the wallet feels like a gadget, the cards fall out, the money clip scratches"), the brief's "What is missing" ("a reason the metal is better than leather for this buyer"), and Monos's two ads that recruit thin-wallet shoppers away from Ridge toward leather (1589827862670498, 1030406983143162). No customer has been interviewed.

## Job to be done

When I want a slimmer wallet, I want one that behaves like a wallet and not like a device, so I can carry it without thinking about it or defending it. **Inferred.**

## Trigger moment

Seeing the product on someone he respects and watching it work without fuss; a friend's 99-day trial story; or the same push as p1 (a worn bifold) arriving at a man who has already rejected metal once. **Inferred.** No ad in the sample names this trigger. Monos 1030406983143162 opens "THE RIDGE WALLET / BUT WHAT IF" (2026-08-11), which is the market acknowledging that some thin-wallet shoppers have looked at Ridge and want an alternative.

## Four forces

- **Push** (what is wrong with the current situation, in their words): weak. His current wallet is fine or merely fat; the slim category appeals but the metal version does not. **Inferred.** No customer language collected.
- **Pull** (what the new solution promises them, in their words): a flat pocket and a solid object, if it can be trusted to hold cards and not scratch things. **Inferred** from ridge.com "Slim, RFID-blocking, built to last for life" (2026-10-23) and Dango's "Engineered for those who don't just carry, they engage" (1771865427052597, 2026-09-24), which is the gear-minded version he is wary of.
- **Anxiety** (what they fear about switching): cards sliding out onto a floor; the clip scratching a phone or a car door; fiddling with the band in front of a cashier; looking like he bought a gadget off an ad; the "lifetime" promise turning into a support ticket. **Inferred** from interrogation Q4; the market never addresses it (market map section 4, "What falls out, and what does not").
- **Habit** (what keeps them in the current situation): a wallet is a wallet; leather is what adults carry; the thin-leather alternatives (Bellroy, Monos, Andar) give him slim without the gadget feel. **Inferred** from the Monos and Bellroy ads that recruit this exact buyer (1589827862670498, 1497017115093052).

## Current workaround, including nothing

A slim leather sleeve or card holder (Bellroy Slim Sleeve at $135, Bellroy landing page capture for 1046004753909784, 2026-10-23; Monos leather card wallets), or the old bifold with fewer cards in it. Nothing costs him only the fat pocket. **Inferred.** Evidence that would fill this: Ridge reviews that begin "I was skeptical"; Reddit r/EDC threads asking "do the cards fall out of a Ridge"; comments under Ridge ads.

## Awareness distribution

Mostly product-aware: he knows Ridge by name and has an opinion. A smaller solution-aware group knows metal wallets exist but not the brand. Almost none problem-aware or unaware; the problem (a fat wallet) is not what holds him back. **Inferred**; no survey evidence. Ridge's catalogue tiles ("Upgrade your wallet to Ridge") meet him at his stage but give him nothing new to believe.

## Sophistication

The highest in this set. He has seen the mechanism demos (5 of 9 advertisers), the finish drops (Ridge Phantom 1402989268694829, NFL 2211059562779279), the superlatives and the discounts, and he is numb to all of them because none answers his objection. He has probably also seen the leather brands' "slim without the metal" positioning (Monos, Bellroy). Not yet seen: anyone demonstrating that the cards stay in, the clip does not scratch, or what the 99 days are for. **Inferred** from the roster and market map section 4.

## Emotional identity

"I am the kind of person who is not taken in by clever products." The ad must not try to win him with cleverness, finishes or a creator's enthusiasm; it must let him check something for himself. **Inferred**; no quote. He would respond to "I was skeptical" from someone like him, not to "the best wallet ever" (Andar 1315996623672010).

## Implicit goal (Barden)

Explicit goal: a slim wallet that works. Implicit goal: **security** in Barden's sense of not being fooled, with a strand of autonomy (he decides, the ad does not). The visual world should be plain and unstyled: a kitchen table, one take, no music, the wallet dropped, shaken, turned upside down; the opposite of the dark engineered world Ridge uses now (brief, "Tone and visual world"). **Inferred.** Every roster advertiser in metal promises excitement or engineered identity (Dango "engage", Ridge finishes); nobody promises "nothing will go wrong", so the goal is uncontested (market map section 4).

## Status game

He wants to look, to his peers and to himself, like someone who was right to wait and then bought for a reason. He would look foolish if a card fell out in front of someone after he had switched, or if he had to admit the ad got him. **Inferred** from interrogation Q1 and Q4 defaults ("they paid and nothing changed, and someone they told finds out").

## Proof required

A demonstration of what does not happen: the wallet upside down, shaken, dropped, the cards still in; the clip against a phone screen; the band after a year. A person like him saying "I was skeptical" with consent, not a creator. The 99 days explained as "this is what the trial is for". What the stand-in brand can supply today (observed, ridge.com 2026-10-23): the 99-day trial, the lifetime warranty, the "patented" design claim (unverified in this run). Missing: any demonstration ad of the failure modes, any skeptic-turned-owner, any sourced count behind "5 star reviews". The asset catalog has not been built yet.

## Poor-fit signals

The man who wants soft leather in the hand will not be converted by any demonstration; the honest ad lets him go. The man who wants a coin pocket or carries more than twelve cards. The man who hates the engineered look on principle rather than for a reason. An ad that opens "if you want leather, buy leather; if you want to know whether the cards fall out, watch this" repels without insult.

## What nobody says to them yet

All hypothetical; evidence is the market map's uncontested claims and the objection table.

1. What falls out, and what does not: the upside-down test, the clip on the phone, the band after a year. Evidence: section 4 "What falls out, and what does not"; interrogation Q4.
2. "Live with it for 99 days" told as a story rather than a line. Evidence: section 4; hypothesis 3; both Ridge EU and UK state the trial without dramatising it.
3. A skeptic who switched, in his own words, with consent. Evidence: proof types in the roster are unboxings and unsourced counts (section 6); no "I was skeptical" ad in the sample.
4. Where leather wins, said by Ridge: coins, feel, patina. Honesty as the sophistication move against superlatives (section 2, "not another superlative"). Evidence: conventions table row 2.
5. The mechanism without the gear voice: the same slide demo Dango runs, captioned "listen", with no "engage" copy. Evidence: hypothesis 5; Barden note above (every metal advertiser promises excitement or engineered identity).
