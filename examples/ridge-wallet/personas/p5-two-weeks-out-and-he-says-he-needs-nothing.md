# p5: two weeks out and he says he needs nothing

Version 1, 2026-10-23. Prior versions are kept below the line at the end of this file; a revision never deletes a prior version.

Built from the market map's open situation "gift receiver's spouse or parent choosing for a hard-to-buy-for man" (section 5, 0 advertisers; Ridge's NFL ad hints) and the interrogation's hypothetical "gift buyer at a loss two weeks before a birthday". Kept separate from every other persona because the buyer and the user are different people: the push, anxiety and objections belong to the giver (will he like it, will it look thoughtful, can I return it), and the proof is about the recipient's reaction, not the wallet's performance. The fan and finish collector the market already serves (Ridge NFL, Thread collabs) is not this persona; that buyer chooses by team or colour and is covered (section 5, "served").

## Name as a situation

A partner, parent or adult child with a birthday, Father's Day or Christmas two weeks away for a man who says he does not need anything, who has given the usual things already, and who wants something he will actually use and that looks considered. **Inferred** from Ridge's own NFL gift ad ("Get the Football Fan what they want", 2211059562779279, 2026-10-12), the site category title "5 reasons why women love the Ridge wallet" (ridge.com, 2026-10-23), Embrosh 1955265108477113 ("Forget perfume. Forget wallets. Give your boyfriend something nobody else can give him", 2026-09-26, a keyword neighbor arguing against wallets as gifts), and the interrogation's hypothetical five customers. No customer has been interviewed.

## Job to be done

When a date is coming for a man who is hard to buy for, I want to give him something he will carry every day and did not know he wanted, so I can see him use it and know I chose well. **Inferred.**

## Trigger moment

The calendar: two weeks before a birthday, Father's Day or the holidays, often after a gift-guide search or a "what do you want" conversation that went nowhere. Secondary: noticing his wallet is falling apart (which makes the giver a proxy for p1). **Inferred.** Sources: interrogation's hypothetical "the gift buyer at a loss two weeks before a birthday"; Ridge 2211059562779279 headline "The Perfect Gift for the Football Fan" (the only roster ad that names a gift occasion); market map section 11, hypothesis 6 (the holiday window).

## Four forces

- **Push** (what is wrong with the current situation, in their words): he says he needs nothing; last year's gift sat in a drawer; the date is close. **Inferred.** The market's only gift language is Ridge's "Get the Football Fan what they want" (2211059562779279) and Embrosh's "Forget perfume. Forget wallets." (1955265108477113, 2026-09-26), which says what the giver has already tried. No customer language collected.
- **Pull** (what the new solution promises them, in their words): something he will use every day, that looks engineered and considered, that can carry his team, his initials or a finish he would pick; a visible upgrade from the worn wallet she has noticed. **Inferred** from ridge.com (finishes, licensed drops, "Elevate your everyday", 2026-10-23) and the NFL ad ("The perfect combination of slimness, durability, and team pride").
- **Anxiety** (what they fear about switching): he will not switch from the wallet he has carried for years; it will look like a gadget to him; the wrong size, colour or team; it will be the thing in the drawer again; it cannot be returned after the date. **Inferred** from interrogation Q4 default ("they paid and nothing changed, and someone they told finds out") read from the giver's side, and from the switch ritual nobody describes (Q6).
- **Habit** (what keeps them in the current situation): the safe gifts (a shirt, a bottle, a gift card); asking him and getting "nothing"; buying from the brands she already knows. **Inferred.**

## Current workaround, including nothing

Gift guides, a gift card, a shirt, a bottle, or asking his friends. Nothing costs her the drawer gift and the small disappointment on the day. **Inferred**; no quote. Evidence that would fill this: gift-guide comment sections ("bought this for my husband"), Ridge reviews that start "got this for my dad" or "my boyfriend", Reddit r/gifts threads on hard-to-buy-for men, Amazon "verified purchase" reviews around December and June.

## Awareness distribution

Mostly product-aware of the brand through gift guides and years of ads (the Ridge name is familiar) but unaware of the wallet problem itself (she has not thought about his back pocket until she looks). A smaller problem-aware group has noticed his wallet is worn and is buying for that reason. **Inferred**; no survey evidence. The right ad opens on the gift problem, not on the wallet.

## Sophistication

Low for the category and high for gifting. She has not heard "slim" and "RFID" as a buyer and is not numb to them, but they mean nothing to her. She is numb to "the perfect gift for..." (Ridge 2211059562779279) and to limited-edition scarcity (Ridge Phantom 1402989268694829, Andar "rarely in stock" 2323595761732589). What she has not seen: the recipient's reaction, the return policy as the giver's safety net, the switch shown so she can picture him doing it. **Inferred** from the roster.

## Emotional identity

"I am the kind of person who notices what people need before they say it." The ad must not make her feel she is guessing or buying a gadget off an ad; it should make the choice feel observant (she noticed the worn wallet) and safe (he can swap it). **Inferred**; no quote. The market's version is "Get the Football Fan what they want" (Ridge 2211059562779279), which gives her his team as the reason; the open version gives her the worn wallet as the reason.

## Implicit goal (Barden)

Explicit goal: a gift he will use. Implicit goal: **belonging** (Barden's affiliation territory: being seen as the person who knew him), with security second (a safe choice, returnable). The visual world should be the moment of giving and the moment of use a week later, in a kitchen or a hallway, warm light; the product small in frame. **Inferred.** Ridge's current gift ad uses team identity and stadium energy (excitement); the opposite pass tests warmth over fandom (hypothesis 6).

## Status game

She wants to look, to him and to the family watching him open it, like the one who gets him. She would look foolish if he said "I have a wallet" and put it away, or if he went back to the old one within a month. **Inferred** from interrogation Q1 and Q4 defaults read from the giver's side.

## Proof required

The recipient's reaction and, more, the recipient still carrying it a month later (a person like him, with consent); the return and exchange policy stated as the giver's safety net ("if he wants a different finish, swap it"); delivery before the date; a simple way to choose (three finishes for three kinds of man, not thirty). What the stand-in brand can supply today (observed, ridge.com 2026-10-23): the 99-day trial, fast free shipping, thirty-plus finishes and licensed drops, the NFL line. Missing: any giver testimonial, any "a month later" proof, a chooser, an engraving or personalisation offer (interrogation Q11 inferred, unverified on the site in this run). ## Poor-fit signals

The giver buying for a man who carries coins, cash wads or many cards; the one who wants a luxury-logo gift (the metal wallet reads as gear, not luxury, except the gold edition 1668472461558360); the one shopping on the clearance rack (Thread 1084877057196159 serves her). An ad that says "for the man who carries eleven cards and hates a fat pocket; not for the one with a coin jar in his pocket" repels her without insult and helps her choose.

## What nobody says to them yet

All hypothetical; evidence is the market map's uncontested claims, hypothesis 6 and the objection table.

1. "For the man who says he does not need anything": the giver's situation named, not his team. Evidence: section 5 open row; hypothesis 6; the only gift ad in the roster selects by fandom (2211059562779279).
2. The swap as the giver's safety net: the 99-day trial and finishes told to her ("if he wants black, swap it"). Evidence: the trial exists on the site and in Ridge EU/UK copy, and nobody tells it to a giver.
3. The switch she can picture him doing: the evening he empties the old wallet, shown so she believes he will. Evidence: section 4 "The switch"; the giver's main anxiety above.
4. A month later: a giver's story about the recipient still carrying it, with consent. Evidence: proof in the roster is unboxings and unsourced counts (section 6); no giver testimonial in the sample.
5. A chooser: three finishes for three kinds of man, instead of thirty. Evidence: Ridge EU 1616785086769214 offers "over 30 styles and colors" with no help choosing; the objection table (fit).
