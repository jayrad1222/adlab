## Part A. Diagnosis

Ranked. The ranking is the analyst's read (inferred), and its cues are below: Ridge's own ads are catalogue tiles and drops (item 1), every captured page opens on a sale (item 2), and its two costliest promises appear as banner lines (item 4). On that read, Ridge does not have a marketing problem. It has a perception problem: it sells a buy-once object backed by two of the most expensive promises in the category, and presents it like a gadget shop that is on sale. The price signal and the missing ritual are how that perception gets made. Trust is the asset it is not using.

**1. Perception.** The skeptic sees a gadget, and Ridge's own ads agree with him. Ridge's 12 ads in the pull are catalogue tiles with no persona (1028460456846299, 1382045620249355, 3176541469344352), a tech catalogue (1110797578128915), two copies of one power-bank demo (1081840734441981, 1644214914045449; one observation), a limited-edition video with a man to camera (1402989268694829, "The end of millennial gray"), a gold-plated wallet (1668472461558360), football fans "gatekeeping" (2211059562779279 and its duplicate 38844454545168783; one observation), and EU and UK catalogue copy (1616785086769214, 4072538929716078; identical copy, one observation) (`competitor-ridge-self.md`, observed). The site sells wallets beside rings, luggage, power banks, chains and drinkware, with Batman editions and finishes "pulled straight from the newest iPhone lineup" (brief.md, observed). Competitors name Ridge: Monos 1030406983143162 opens on the caption "THE RIDGE WALLET" over a leather wallet, and Monos 1589827862670498, a dynamic catalogue ad, names "Ridge, Ekster & Bellroy" (keyword neighbors; text observed; whom Monos targets is unknown). Answer 4 is a default; its gadget fears (cards falling out, the clip scratching) are the builder's inferred addition. One featured review states that fear in a customer's words: "I was a little worried that my new wallet would be too tight like my last one and scratch off the edges and numbers on my credit cards" (observed customer language, insight only). p3 is that man, and the market map's section 5 finds no advertiser speaking to him (section 5 is inferred from the rows; `personas/p3-the-man-who-thinks-it-is-a-gadget.md`, inferred). ## Part B. The seven highest-leverage recommendations

Budget: finance.md's $6,000 a month is a placeholder. At placeholder values `plan/economics.md` gives a target CPA of $47.38, $1,184.40 per concept, and 5 concepts a month judged to 25 purchases each; all placeholder. finance.md's price of 110 is a placeholder too; the Ridge Wallet 2.0 in Aluminum, Royal Black lists at $95 (observed). Every test below reads hook rate and hold rate (video) or CTR (static) after the impression floor first, then CPA only at 25 purchases (`library/finance/decision-rules.md`). The floors are not yet set in finance.md. Three cautions. A purchase can be returned "within 99 days of your purchase", so a CPA judged at 25 purchases stays provisional until day 99 after the 25th purchase (inferred from the captured terms). Every arm that restates the trial or the warranty quotes the captured wording, carries the capture date, 23 October 2026, and says the terms can change; Ridge's warranty page says so itself. And seven recommendations, three moonshots and the opposite pass are more than one month of slots at the placeholder budget, so the order below is the running order. R2 and M3 are not runnable as of the capture date; R5, R6 and R7 each wait on a capture, a landing or an asset.

### R1: Tell the 99-day trial as the switch, counted from the order

- Mechanism: `library/psychology/rituals-and-ownership.md` (time-anchored copy turns a policy into a ritual the buyer owns); `library/psychology/jtbd-four-forces.md` (shrinks anxiety and habit, the two forces holding p1); `library/psychology/satisficing.md` (removes the catastrophe of a fad bought off an ad); `library/psychology/endowment-and-ikea-effect.md` (99 days of carrying it is ownership before the final decision).
- Evidence: market map section 4, "The switch" and "What falls out" (inferred); section 5, switch row, no advertiser (inferred from the rows); hypothesis 3; site, "Live with it. If it's not everything we promised, return it for a full refund" (observed); the trial as a bare line in 1616785086769214 and 4072538929716078 (one observation); the featured review that converted "After deciding what cards I had to have" (observed customer language, insight only); Returns & Exchanges terms (observed 2026-10-23); answer 6 (default); p1 and p3 "proof required" (inferred).
- Cheapest test: one 15-second video, one take on a table: the old bifold emptied, what goes in, what goes in a drawer. Then three cards, counted from the order because the terms count from the purchase: "Day 1: you order.", "Any day to day 99: exchange it for another finish; after your return is approved, the exchange ships free in the US.", "Day 99: the last day to start a return. US return label free; it must be in usable condition." End line: "Ridge return terms as of 23 October 2026; they can change." Run it against a catalogue tile in the shape of 1028460456846299. Both arms land on the same page: the product page of the wallet shown, which on the Royal Black capture repeats "99-DAY RISK-FREE TRIAL", "Free shipping + returns", the warranty and "Holds 1-12 Cards" (observed). The tile's own landing, the wallets grid, opens on the sale banner and 272 results and repeats none of the story (observed). Read CTR for both arms at the floor (the tile has no hook rate); report the video's hook and hold rate as diagnostics; then CPA at 25 purchases, with returns read at day 99. One of the five placeholder slots.
- Falsifier: the switch video's CTR is not above the tile's at the floor; or at 25 purchases its CPA is not below the tile's; or its CPA wins and returns by day 99 erase the gap.
- Risk: truth: the cards state captured terms only, not what customers felt; no "by week five you will love it". Delivery time eats into the 99 days and was not captured, so no card counts from arrival. "Risk-free" is the site's word; the terms exclude "Personalized items, warranty claim orders and certain limited edition items", do not refund original shipping, require "usable condition", and charge international customers for a tracked return. So the ad runs to US audiences only, on a standard finish, never on a personalized or limited wallet. Continuity: partial. The sale banner sits on the landing while the sale runs; the ad carries no price, so nothing contradicts it, but the banner pulls toward the wait-for-the-sale reading. Policy: low. Reputational: inviting doubters can raise returns (refund_rate 0.06 is a placeholder hypothesis), and telling the trial as a story makes any gap between "risk-free" and the terms louder.

### R2: State one price with its reason; stop leading wallets with the discount

This is an owner pricing decision, not a copy change, and it is not runnable as of the capture date: on 23 October 2026 a sitewide sale banner heads every captured page and the named product is on sale.

- Mechanism: `library/psychology/price-is-information.md` (the price is the first copy read); `library/psychology/costly-signaling.md` (a lifetime promise is more credible from a company that does not need a sale to sell); `library/psychology/anchoring-and-framing.md` (a struck price anchors the buyer on the sale, not on the object).
- Evidence: the sale banner on every captured page and the Royal Black wallet at $95 list, $76 at capture (observed 2026-10-23); market map section 7 (inferred); the discount end is held by Dango 1457214629482883, 1102071902279022, 2937471213255837 and Thread 1084877057196159, 1285822420071926 (observed); "BUNDLE & SAVE UP TO 26% OFF" (1668472461558360, observed); the Built for Life fee table (observed 2026-10-23); p2 and p3 purchase-stage rows (inferred); hypothesis 8.
- Cheapest test: two offer cards on one named product, Ridge Wallet 2.0, Aluminum, Royal Black, list price $95 (observed). Arm A states the real list price with its reason, in the warranty's captured terms: "$95. Lose it, and Ridge replaces it for $17.95. Up to twice." with "Built for Life Warranty terms as of 23 October 2026; Ridge says they can change. Claims are reviewed and can be denied; Ridge may ask for proof of purchase and photos." Arm A runs only when no sale is live on that product's landing; otherwise the ad says $95, the page says less, and continuity breaks. Arm B, the sale framing, runs only while a documented sale is live, with a real former price at which Ridge actually sold the product and, if the card says "up to", a real share of items at that cut, all recorded in offer.md before the card is built. The two arms need opposite conditions, so they cannot run on one product at one time: the test is either sequential (which confounds the period with the price) or split across two named finishes (which confounds the finish), and the readout says which. Both arms land on their product page. Read CTR at the floor, then run each to 25 purchases and compare contribution per ad dollar (hypothesis 8: judge this one on economics, not CTR). Two of the five slots. Preconditions: Ridge's real unit cost (COGS and shipping) in finance.md, because a placeholder margin percentage overstates contribution at a sale price; returns read at day 99.
- Falsifier: at 25 purchases each, with real unit cost and returns read at day 99, A's contribution per ad dollar (purchases times contribution per order net of returns, over spend) is below B's.
- Risk: owner decision: Ridge loses some buyers who wait for sales, a buyer section 5 marks overserved already (inferred). FTC: B's former price must be one Ridge actually charged, and "up to" must cover a real share of items. Truth: A's reason is the captured fee and limit; if Ridge changes them, the card is wrong that day. The $17.95 fee applies to wallets under $150, so the card fits this $95 product only. Loss is covered and cosmetic wear is not, so the card must not imply the wallet stays new. Continuity: A breaks the moment any sale reaches its landing.
