## The one idea

A price card that shows $97 once, says what the $97 pays for in the About page's own words, and crosses nothing out.

## The mechanism

All inferred; every effect below is a hypothesis until a readout.

- The psychological job: the viewer must read the price as a statement about what the thing is (the founders' own builds), not as a deal to haggle over or wait on.
- The price is the first copy read (`library/psychology/price-is-information.md`): telling the viewer what the number means turns "mid" (market map section 7, inferred) into a signal. The same file notes that a charm ending like $97 reads as deal-led; the reason line has to do the work the number cannot.
- Red sneakers (`library/psychology/shotton-illusion-of-choice.md`): declining the strikethrough that 6 of 12 advertisers run can read as confidence, but only to a viewer who has seen the convention, which p6 has (offer cards in their feed, persona file, inferred).
- The first number sets the frame (`library/psychology/anchoring-and-framing.md`): with nothing struck through, the first number on the card is $97 itself, not a bigger fake one. The anchor p6 brings is the tool subscription; the reason line moves the comparison from "a tool" to "someone's working builds".
- No manufactured now (`library/psychology/hyperbolic-discounting.md`; evidence rule 5): a rise or a countdown is the usual "act today" lever, and fake deadlines are a trust killer. The page's rise line is the founders' call (interrogation R4); the ad does not use it.
- The price-stated CTA (`library/copy/cta-patterns.md`): states the number and the terms so there is no hidden price behind the click.
- Copy move: the price once, the page's line as the reason (as the offer_line, not the largest text), the terms that are observed (7 days free, cancel anytime), and the kicker naming the chat-tab situation, AI and the founders so a stranger can tell who the $97 is for and what it pays for; nothing struck, nothing rising, no other product's price.
