## The one idea

The beginner's fear is the first week nobody can picture, so the ad shows week one, the 7 free days after joining, as three plain steps the founders have written, before it asks for anything.

Week one is defined here as the first 7 days after joining, which are the 7-day free trial (About page: "7 days free", observed). The steps must fit inside those 7 days; the paid membership starts after them.

## The mechanism

All inferred; every effect below is a hypothesis until a readout.

- The psychological job: the beginner must be able to picture day one, and see that nothing in it is a test, before the price lands.
- Remove the uncertainty (`library/psychology/perception-as-lever.md`): the bus countdown does not bring the bus sooner, it removes not knowing. Not knowing what happens after the click is the cost this persona pays.
- A time-anchored ritual (`library/psychology/rituals-and-ownership.md`): a first week with numbered steps gives the viewer a way to own the experience instead of "poking around" (answer 6's default).
- Shrink time and effort (`library/strategy/hormozi-value-equation.md`): three small steps lower the imagined effort and the time to a first thing done, without a bigger promise.
- Avoid the catastrophe first (`library/psychology/satisficing.md`): the beginner is avoiding looking foolish more than seeking the best option; a known first week removes the risk before any dream is offered.
- One path, no menu (`library/psychology/defaults-and-status-quo.md`): three steps in order are a default, which beats a library of lessons for a viewer who does not know where to start.
- Copy move: the kicker names AI and the founders; the headline names the object (week one, three steps on one card); the three numbered slots carry the founders' steps; the offer line closes. The viewer is never called behind, new, scared or non-technical.
