# p2: the truck is the office

Version 1, 2026-10-23. Prior versions are kept below the line at the end of this file; a revision never deletes a prior version.

Evidence base: `business/brief.md` (Daniel runs an operations software company and a custom fabrication business), `business/interrogation-answers.md` (founders' hypothetical "the field-business owner"), `analysis/market-map.md` sections 4, 5, 8 and 11, `analysis/ads.csv`. No customer interviewed. Labels: observed, owner-provided, inferred, hypothetical.

Why this is split from p1: same push (admin after hours), different proof. This owner is away from a desk most of the day with a crew, gear and trucks; they need to see AI used from a phone between jobs and inside a trade business like the founders' fabrication business, not at a laptop. The field or trade row is one of the three open rows in the market map (section 5, 0 of 12 advertisers).

## Name as a situation

Hypothetical (founders' persona sketch, 2026-10-23; standing brief 2026-10-18): the owner of a field or trade business whose day is on site with a crew, whose quotes are typed in the cab between jobs, and whose scheduling, gear tracking and callbacks happen on a phone or not at all until the evening.

## Job to be done

Hypothetical: when I am on site and the next quote, the schedule change and the supplier question all land on my phone at once, I want to handle them without stopping the crew or waiting for tonight, so I can keep the jobs moving and stop losing work to the contractor who answered first.

## Trigger moment

Hypothetical (interrogation answer 9 is a default): a lost bid because the quote went out two days late; a double-booked crew; a piece of gear nobody could find; a slow month that shows how many callbacks were never made. No ad in the sample names a trigger for this situation. The nearest is Planet Smart City India (1068918252179664, 1421746219828247), a water-management tool for facility managers, which names "juggling" as the problem but is a different category. Evidence needed: ask the founders which moment made them build the first feature of his operations software; ask five trade owners the same.

## Four forces

- **Push** (owner-provided, answer 2, applied to this situation; inferred where noted): admin by hand after hours; "fewer people than the work needs". Inferred: the specific push is that the office work cannot be done where the day is spent, so it waits, and waiting loses jobs.
- **Pull** (observed, About page 2026-10-23): "simplify operations"; "Our builds as they happen: the prompts, systems, wins and mistakes behind the work." Inferred: the pull is seeing a fabrication business run its own operations with AI, which is what the founders' fabrication business is (brief, owner-provided).
- **Anxiety** (owner-provided, answer 4): being sold a course by someone with no real business. Inferred: the first-week fear is that it only works at a desk, and that the crew sees the owner fiddling with a phone instead of running the job.
- **Habit** (owner-provided, answer 2): by hand after hours; watch AI content and change nothing on Monday. Inferred: the habit is the whiteboard, the group text and the paper quote book, which all work on site without a signal.

## Current workaround, including nothing

Inferred, no source beyond the founders' sketch: a group text with the crew, a whiteboard or paper calendar, quotes typed on a phone or done at night, gear tracked by memory. "Nothing" costs late quotes and lost bids (hypothetical; no numbers). Evidence needed: ask the founders what the fabrication business used before its operations software and what the first automated task was; the answer is both the workaround and the proof.

## Awareness distribution

Inferred, no survey evidence: mostly problem-aware (the pain is daily and named) and largely unaware that AI applies to a trade business at all, because every AI ad they see shows video, code or "agencies" (market map sections 5 and 8). A few solution-aware who use a field-service app already. Product-aware: effectively none; 5 members on the page (observed).

## Sophistication

Inferred: lower than p1 for AI claims, because the sample's AI ads do not reach a trade audience with trade examples. They have heard "no experience needed" (9 of 12 advertisers) and seen the founder-to-camera selfie (6 of 12; 1054913050869810, 28771184185810174, 1086075447748545). They are likely numb to tool news and "15 new tools" listicles (1633617958211625, 960956262925605), which have nothing to do with a job site. The mechanism claim they have not seen: a real schedule, a real quote and a real gear list handled by AI inside a fabrication or field business (section 4).

## Emotional identity

Inferred (answer 1 and the About page contrast): "I am the kind of person who does the work with my hands and runs a real crew; I do not sit at a desk and I do not buy courses." The ad must not make the owner look like a student or like someone who has been doing it wrong. Showing a founder in a fabrication shop (owner-provided assets: dated warehouse photographs, answer 12) is the mirror.

## Implicit goal (Barden)

Inferred: autonomy (the day is theirs again; nobody chasing them for a quote) with security second (nothing falls through). Explicit goal: run quotes, scheduling and callbacks with AI from wherever the work is. Visual world: a shop floor, a truck cab, a phone screen with a real message; daylight, not a studio. No roster ad uses this world; the only day-in-the-life ads are the pottery studio's (1425066189041578, 1448220497223234), a name collision.

## Status game

Owner-provided (answer 1): the owner who runs a tighter operation than their peers, to their crew and the people they compare notes with. Inferred: also to their customers, who notice the fast quote. Foolish outcome: the crew seeing the owner distracted by a phone for no result, or a customer getting a wrong AI-written quote (hypothetical).

## Poor-fit signals

Owner-provided (answer 8): wants it done for them, wants a guarantee, wants individual build reviews or a response-time promise. Inferred for this persona: wants a field-service app recommended and installed for them (the community shows builds, it does not do them); has no reliable time after hours in season; expects a founder to answer their specific shop's question (the brief rules out response-time promises). Repel without insulting: show a founder building for the fabrication business, state "we show our own builds; we do not build yours."

## What nobody says to them yet

All hypothetical, with the evidence for the gap.

1. "A fabrication shop runs on this. Here is Tuesday's schedule." Gap: no advertiser shows an operating business (section 4); the founders' businesses are real (owner-provided).
2. "It works from the cab." Gap: every demo in the sample is a laptop screen (sections 3 and 8).
3. "If you already run something, this is for you." Gap: 9 of 12 advertisers select on "no experience needed"; nobody selects on experience of running a business (conventions.md, row 3).
4. "Here is the gear list the AI got wrong, and what we changed." Gap: mistakes language absent (section 4); the rejected-result demonstration exists (answer 12).
5. "We will not tell you what you will earn." Gap: income implication runs at 4 of 12 advertisers (conventions.md, row 2); the brief rules it out anyway.
