# p1: the quote still in drafts at 10pm

Version 1, 2026-10-23. Prior versions are kept below the line at the end of this file; a revision never deletes a prior version.

Evidence base: `business/brief.md`, `business/offer.md`, `business/interrogation-answers.md` (founders' hypothetical customer "the buried business owner"), `analysis/market-map.md` sections 4, 5 and 11, `analysis/ads.csv` rows cited by id. No customer was interviewed. Labels: observed, owner-provided, inferred, hypothetical.

Why this is its own persona and not merged with p2 (the field owner): the push is the same (admin after hours) but the proof differs. This owner works at a desk and needs to see a real quote email or schedule handled by AI; the field owner needs to see it work from a phone in a truck with a crew waiting. The freelancer or consultant named in the founders' seventh hypothesis is folded in here when they do their own admin after hours; the freelancer who wants to sell AI services is the overserved market row and is listed under poor-fit signals.

## Name as a situation

Hypothetical (from the founders' persona sketch, interrogation answers 2026-10-23, and the standing brief 2026-10-18): the owner of an existing small business who does the quotes, callbacks and invoices themselves after the day's work, has said for a year that they would hire someone or fix the system, and tonight is opening the laptop again with the same quote still in drafts.

## Job to be done

Hypothetical: when the day's paid work is done and the admin is still waiting, I want to get the quote, the follow-up and the invoice out without doing each one by hand, so I can close the laptop before 10pm and stop losing jobs to the slow reply.

## Trigger moment

Hypothetical, from interrogation answer 9 (default, not owner-confirmed): "something broke, or they got a bill, or they saw someone else doing it." Candidate triggers to test: a job lost because the quote went out late; a tax or bookkeeping bill that shows how much admin cost; a peer at a supplier counter saying they let AI write their follow-ups. No ad in the sample names a trigger for this situation; the closest is Hashtag NoCode's stock-alert automation (2391436374631797, 2219084038830459), which shows the admin task rather than the moment it became unbearable. Evidence needed: five intake calls or a comment thread asking "what happened the week you decided."

## Four forces

- **Push** (inferred from owner-provided answer 2): the admin is done by hand after hours; it costs "evenings, repeated mistakes" and leaves "fewer people than the work needs" (owner-provided wording, 2026-10-23). The quote is late, the follow-up is forgotten, the invoice goes out at the end of the month instead of the end of the job.
- **Pull** (observed, About page 2026-10-23): "Learn to use AI to buy back your time, earn more and spend less"; "simplify operations and understand your numbers"; "the prompts, systems, wins and mistakes behind the work." Inferred: the pull that fits this persona is seeing a real operator run the same admin with AI before trying it themselves.
- **Anxiety** (owner-provided, answer 4): "they fear being sold a course by someone with no real business." Default, answer 4: "they paid and nothing changed, and someone they told finds out." Inferred: a second anxiety is time, since a community with no defined first week (answer 6, gap) looks like one more thing to keep up with.
- **Habit** (owner-provided, answer 2): "they watch AI content and change nothing on Monday; they do admin by hand after hours." The by-hand routine works, slowly, and they know every step of it. Inferred: the sunk cost is the spreadsheet or the invoicing tool they already pay for.

## Current workaround, including nothing

Owner-provided (answer 2): doing it by hand after hours, and watching AI content without changing anything. Inferred: a mix of an invoicing or job tool, text-message follow-ups from memory, and a spreadsheet nobody else can read. "Nothing" costs evenings and repeated mistakes (owner-provided words; no numbers verified). Evidence needed: ask five owners what tool the quote lives in today.

## Awareness distribution

Inferred, no survey evidence: mostly problem-aware (they know the admin is eating evenings and can name the tasks); a smaller share solution-aware who have seen an AI demo or tried a prompt once; almost none product-aware, because no advertiser in the 156-row sample talks to this situation (market map section 5, 0 of 12 advertisers) and Operators Workshop has 5 members (observed). Unaware owners exist but are expensive to reach on a small budget.

## Sophistication

Inferred from the market map (section 2, stage 3 moving to 4): this owner has heard "learn AI, no experience needed" from most of the roster (9 of 12 advertisers; 1071018978717933, 1708167586945432, 2032976233999151, 1125643543498156), has seen the "ad is the demo" structure (3366900353513536, 836000928775594, 2391436374631797, 1106125972107418) and the crossed-out price (1403311474997024, 1623416949192396, 1419356802968044). They are numb to member counts without sources (1894923588153698, 1040148231921228) and to income lines. Mechanism explanations they have seen: prompt next to output, node-graph pipelines (890665233928271), "tools are not the skill, directing is" (1611195850348762). What they have not seen: a real business being run with AI, with the mistakes shown (market map section 4).

## Emotional identity

Inferred, from the About page contrast ("not just from people who are selling a course with no real experience", observed 2026-10-23) and interrogation answer 1: "I am the kind of person who runs a real business and does not fall for internet courses." The ad must not make them look like a beginner, a mark, or someone who needs rescuing. It can show them as an operator comparing notes with other operators.

## Implicit goal (Barden)

Inferred: security (control over the operation, no surprises, the books and the quotes in order), with autonomy second (the evenings back). Explicit goal: get quotes, follow-ups and invoices done with AI. Visual world to match: a real desk or counter, dated paperwork, a calm screen, not a lifestyle montage. Every roster advertiser promising "freedom" or income (AI Operator CLUB 1125643543498156; AI Video Bootcamp 1894923588153698) is selling adventure or excitement; security is open.

## Status game

Owner-provided (answer 1): they want to look like "the owner who runs a tighter operation than their peers, to their partner, their crew and the people they compare notes with." Foolish outcome (default, answer 4): paying for something and nothing changing, in front of the person they told. Inferred: the second foolish outcome is sending a customer an AI-written quote with an error in it.

## Proof required

Inferred: a dated operating artifact, which is a real quote email, a real schedule or a real decision with its limitation shown (market map section 6); a person like them (an owner, not a creator); a first week laid out. The member can supply today (owner-provided, answer 12): dated warehouse and store photographs, real software screens, approved account screenshots with strict meaning (deposits are not profit; business spend is not ad spend), and one honest rejected-result demonstration. Missing: a defined first week (answer 6), any member result, any named customer, any number that is not the founders' combined-sales figure. The founders' revenue line ("over $1.5M a year in combined sales", observed) is authority proof, not proof that this persona's admin gets easier.

## Poor-fit signals

Owner-provided (answer 8): anyone who wants it done for them, a guarantee, individual build reviews or a response-time promise. Inferred: the owner who wants someone to build the system for them (an agency buyer); the freelancer who wants to sell AI automation to other businesses (served by Maker School, AI Operator CLUB and AI Automation Society; 1094023723542322, 1384090043489432, 1534027074837411); anyone expecting a curriculum with a certificate (Kurs Claude Code PRO's "74 lekcje w 8 modułach, egzamin i certyfikat", 1419356802968044). What repels them without insulting them: showing the founders working on their own businesses, not on the member's (answer 8), and a plain "no done-for-you, no guarantees" line.

## What nobody says to them yet

All hypothetical; each names the evidence for the gap.

1. "We run the businesses you are hearing about, and we show the AI process inside them." Gap: no advertiser shows an operating business (market map section 4); the About page already makes the claim (observed).
2. "Use it on your own operation, not to sell AI services." Gap: 10 of 12 advertisers sell the route to clients; the owner row is empty (section 5; hypothesis 4).
3. "Here is the quote email we sent on Tuesday, and the part the AI got wrong." Gap: every proof in the sample is a demo or an unsourced number (section 6); the founders hold approved dated screens (answer 12).
4. "Here is what did not work." Gap: mistakes language is absent from all 106 roster ads (section 4); the About page promises "wins and mistakes".
5. "$97 because we show you our own numbers, not a highlight reel." Gap: the price has no framing beyond the rise to $129 (section 7; hypothesis 6).
6. "This is not for you if you want it done for you." Gap: nobody in the sample says who it is not for (brief, "What is missing"; hypothesis 7).
