# p5: the beginner who feels behind and fears breaking something

Version 1, 2026-10-23. Prior versions are kept below the line at the end of this file; a revision never deletes a prior version.

Evidence base: `business/brief.md` (audience includes "curious beginners who do not own a business"), `business/interrogation-answers.md` (founders' hypothetical "the nervous beginner"), `analysis/market-map.md` sections 3, 5 and 11, `analysis/ads.csv`. No customer interviewed. Labels: observed, owner-provided, inferred, hypothetical.

Why this is its own persona and not merged with p6 (the dabbler): the beginner has not really tried; their anxiety is breaking something and looking foolish, and their objection is fit ("I am not technical"). The dabbler has tried and stopped; their objection is tried-before. The proof differs: the beginner needs a first week laid out; the dabbler needs to see a workflow that stuck.

## Name as a situation

Hypothetical (founders' sketch, 2026-10-23; brief, owner-provided): someone who hears about AI every day at work or from friends, feels they are already behind, has not opened a tool beyond a chat window, and is afraid that if they try they will break something, waste money, or be found out as the person who does not get it.

## Job to be done

Hypothetical: when everyone around me seems to be using AI and I have not started, I want a safe first step shown by someone patient and real, so I can stop feeling behind without risking a mistake in front of anyone.

## Trigger moment

Hypothetical: a colleague or boss asking "did you try AI for that?"; a friend's AI-built thing shown at dinner; a news story about jobs. The market names the "behind" trigger directly: "Everyone in your office is using AI" (The Claude Club, shared primary text, 1101916785561103, first seen 2026-10-06); "Rewolucja AI odjezdza ... a Ty nie wiesz, jak na tym zarobic?" ("the AI revolution is leaving, and you do not know how to earn from it", AI Operator CLUB 1123979320082708, first seen 2026-10-16); "Around half of people are worried they'll lose their job to AI" (80,000 Hours 2671836623213205 card text, first seen 2026-06-09). No ad names the fear of breaking something. Evidence needed: comments under those ads; r/ArtificialIntelligence and r/ChatGPT beginner threads.

## Four forces

- **Push** (inferred from the market-voiced lines above): feeling behind while everyone else seems to be moving. Owner-provided (brief): "curious beginners who do not own a business" are part of the audience.
- **Pull** (observed, About page): "If you're new to AI, we're here to assist you!"; "Build with AI: create useful tools, websites, content and automations." Inferred: the pull is watching real people do real tasks, with mistakes shown, so the beginner's own mistakes feel normal.
- **Anxiety** (hypothetical, founders' sketch): fears breaking things; default (answer 4): paid and nothing changed, and someone they told finds out. Inferred: the loudest anxiety is looking foolish in a community of people who already know more.
- **Habit** (owner-provided, answer 2): watching AI content and changing nothing. Inferred: the habit is asking the one friend who knows, or avoiding the subject.

## Current workaround, including nothing

Inferred: nothing, or a free chat tool used like a search engine; a free "intensive" signed up for and not attended (Hashtag NoCode's Imersão gratuita, 1467334001994239, is the shape of this workaround). "Nothing" costs the feeling of being behind and, at work, the tasks others now do faster (hypothetical, no numbers). Evidence needed: ask in intake what they have opened and what stopped them.

## Awareness distribution

Inferred: a large unaware or barely problem-aware share (they feel behind but cannot name the task AI would do for them); a problem-aware share who know the one task; few solution-aware. This is the stage the roster chases hardest with free entry (7 of 12 advertisers, section 3) and "no experience needed" (9 of 12), so the beginner is served but by volume funnels.

## Sophistication

Inferred, lowest of the six: this viewer has not heard many mechanism claims. They have seen "no experience needed" (1071018978717933, 1708167586945432, 2032976233999151, 1125643543498156), free immersions (1467334001994239, 1054913050869810), "learn Claude in 3 hours" (3519104148251249) and the age-41 testimonial (3030674797298673). They are not yet numb; they are overwhelmed. The claim they have not seen: a slow, honest first week from people who admit their own mistakes (section 4, claim 2), and anyone saying "you cannot break it".

## Emotional identity

Inferred: "I am the kind of person who is careful and does things properly; I am not stupid, I just have not had time." The ad must not call them behind, old, non-technical or scared; those are implied personal attributes (Meta policy) and insults. Show the situation (a chat window open with nothing typed) and the founders admitting their own mistakes, which makes caution look normal.

## Implicit goal (Barden)

Inferred: security (nothing breaks, nobody laughs) with discipline second (a first small thing finished). Explicit goal: learn to use AI on one real task with people who answer questions. Visual world: calm, one screen, a patient person, daylight; not fast cuts, not a founder shouting at a phone. The sample's beginner ads use the shouting selfie (AI Operator CLUB 1054913050869810) or an offer card (The Claude Club 3519104148251249); calm is open.

## Status game

Owner-provided (answer 1): the beginner who is "doing something with AI" to friends who only talk about it. Foolish outcome: breaking something at work or asking a question in a community and being made to feel slow (hypothetical). The ad can promise the first and must not promise what the brief rules out (unlimited support, response times).

## Proof required

Inferred: a first week laid out step by step; a patient person like them (a founder who was also new once, if true; unknown); a demonstration where the founder makes a mistake and recovers. The member can supply today: the About page's "If you're new to AI, we're here to assist you!" (observed; close to the support promises the brief limits, so handle with care), one honest rejected-result demonstration (answer 12). Missing: the first week itself (answer 6, gap), any member who started from zero, any way to show "you cannot break it" that is true. The standing brief rules out unlimited support and response-time promises; the proof for this persona must not drift into them.

## Poor-fit signals

Owner-provided (answer 8): wants it done for them, a guarantee, individual reviews, a response-time promise. Inferred for this persona: wants hand-holding on every step (the brief rules out unlimited support); wants a certificate or a 3-hour finish (3519104148251249, 1419356802968044); has no task of their own to apply AI to and expects the community to invent one; expects it free. Repel without insulting: "we show our builds and answer what we can; we are not a help desk," and the plain $97 with no strikethrough.

## What nobody says to them yet

All hypothetical, with the evidence for the gap.

1. "You cannot break anything in week one. Here is week one." Gap: no ad names the fear of breaking something; the first week is also the offer's biggest gap (answer 6).
2. "We made the expensive mistakes so you can make cheap ones." Gap: mistakes language absent from the sample (section 4); the About page already says it (observed).
3. "Nothing here is free, and here is why that is better for you." Gap: 7 of 12 advertisers lead with free (section 3); conventions.md row 8 inverse.
4. "You are not behind. Most people have not started." Gap: the market sells the "behind" fear (1101916785561103, 1123979320082708); nobody relieves it. Needs a source before it can be a claim; without one it is a framing, not a number.
5. "Here is a real person's first task, not a toy." Gap: beginner ads show lesson lists and tool tours; none shows a beginner's first real task (sections 5 and 8).
