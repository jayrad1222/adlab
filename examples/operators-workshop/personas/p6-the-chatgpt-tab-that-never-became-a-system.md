# p6: the ChatGPT tab that never became a system

Version 1, 2026-10-23. Prior versions are kept below the line at the end of this file; a revision never deletes a prior version.

Evidence base: `business/interrogation-answers.md` (the founders also named "the ChatGPT dabbler"), `analysis/market-map.md` sections 2, 5 and 11, `analysis/ads.csv` rows whose persona_inferred reads "has tried a few ChatGPT prompts and still does the work by hand" and "still do everything manually". No customer interviewed. Labels: observed, owner-provided, inferred, hypothetical.

Why this is split from p5 (the beginner) and from p1 (the owner): this person has already tried. They used a chat tool for a few things, got a result once, and nothing stuck; the work is still done by hand. Their objection is tried-before, not fit, and their proof is a workflow that survived contact with a real week, not a gentle first step. They may be an owner, an employee or a freelancer; the situation is the same and the proof is the same, so one persona.

## Name as a situation

Inferred from market-voiced cues (The Claude Club 1101916785561103, "office job professional or business owner who has tried a few ChatGPT prompts and still does the work by hand"; Skool 2032976233999151, "non-coders using Claude and AI who keep watching tutorials but still do everything manually") and the founders' sketch: someone with a chat tool open in a browser tab for a year, who has asked it to draft an email or two, and who still does every repeated task by hand because nothing they tried became a system.

## Job to be done

Hypothetical: when I have already tried AI on a task and it did not stick, I want to see how someone turned a one-off prompt into a thing that runs every week in a real business, so I can stop re-doing the same work and stop feeling that I am the reason it did not work.

## Trigger moment

Hypothetical: doing the same manual task for the fortieth time and remembering the prompt that almost worked; a colleague's automation doing in a minute what took an hour; a tool bill for something unused. The market names the stuck state: "non-coders using Claude and AI who keep watching tutorials but still do everything manually" (Skool 2032976233999151, primary text, first seen 2026-09-04); "Your AI videos & images look fake because you're learning from scattered YouTube tutorials and have no one to ask when you're stuck" (AI Video Bootcamp 28188256047504517, first seen 2026-08-25); "HAVING THE TOOLS ... BETWEEN SIMPLY AND ACTUALLY DIRECTING" (AI Video Bootcamp 1611195850348762, on-screen, first seen 2026-09-08). Evidence needed: comments under those ads; r/ChatGPT and r/ClaudeAI threads titled "how do you actually use this at work".

## Four forces

- **Push** (inferred from the market-voiced lines): tried it, got one result, still doing everything by hand; "no one to ask when you're stuck". Owner-provided (answer 2): watch AI content and change nothing on Monday.
- **Pull** (observed, About page): "the prompts, systems, wins and mistakes behind the work"; "create useful tools, websites, content and automations." Inferred: the pull is the word "systems": seeing a prompt become a process that runs in a real business.
- **Anxiety** (inferred): that it did not stick last time because of them, and will not stick this time either; default (answer 4): paid and nothing changed. Owner-provided (answer 4): sold a course by someone with no real business.
- **Habit** (inferred): the by-hand routine is reliable; the chat tab is there for emergencies; the half-built prompt library is a sunk cost that is easier to ignore than to finish.

## Current workaround, including nothing

Inferred: a chat tool used ad hoc for drafts; saved prompts in a notes app; the real task still done manually. The market describes this state in its own words ("still do everything manually", 2032976233999151; "have no one to ask when you're stuck", 28188256047504517). "Nothing" costs the repeated hour and the quiet belief that AI is for other people (hypothetical). Evidence needed: ask in intake "what did you try, and what happened the second week".

## Awareness distribution

Inferred: solution-aware by definition (they have used the tool) and problem-aware about their own stall; some product-aware for Claude Code or n8n communities that promise to fix the stall (Skool co-brands); not product-aware for Operators Workshop. This is the stage the market map says most roster advertisers write to (10 of 12 run solution-aware ads, section 2), so the mechanism has to be better, not louder.

## Sophistication

Inferred: high for tool claims, since they follow tool news (Maker School listicles 1633617958211625, 1836279407805960; Skool "#1 trending Github repo" 1807946097023857). They have seen "the ad is the demo" (5 of 12, section 3), "tools are not the skill, directing is" (1611195850348762, 1994721787907520), agent org charts (1105898642086436), "stop watching tutorials" (1086075447748545). They are numb to new-tool announcements and to "learn X in 3 hours". What they have not seen: a prompt shown next to the business process it became, with the week it broke and the fix (section 4, claims 1 and 4).

## Emotional identity

Inferred: "I am the kind of person who keeps up and tries things; I am not a laggard, I just have not found the version that works for my job." The ad must not say they did it wrong, and must not sell them another tool. It can say the stall is normal and show the moment a founder's own prompt failed and was rebuilt.

## Implicit goal (Barden)

Inferred: discipline (a system that runs without them re-deciding every week) with security second (not being the person whose AI thing broke). Explicit goal: turn one-off prompts into repeatable business processes. Visual world: a before-and-after of the same task, the prompt and the process side by side, a plain screen; not tool logos, not a hype montage. The sample's solution-aware ads promise excitement through tool news (1633617958211625, 1807946097023857); discipline is open.

## Status game

Owner-provided (answer 1): the owner who runs a tighter operation than their peers; the beginner who is "doing something with AI". Inferred for this persona: to look like the person at work or in their circle whose AI thing actually runs, to colleagues who only talk about it. Foolish outcome: telling someone "I automated it" and having it break the next week (hypothetical).

## Poor-fit signals

Owner-provided (answer 8): wants it done for them, a guarantee, build reviews, response-time promises. Inferred for this persona: wants a prompt pack or template library to download and nothing else (free lesson lists at Hashtag NoCode and The Claude Club serve that, 1467334001994239, 3519104148251249); wants the founders to debug their specific workflow (ruled out by the brief); chases each new model instead of finishing one system (tool-news habit, 1384073453905007). Repel without insulting: "we show our systems and how they broke; we do not fix yours," and no download-and-done promise.

## What nobody says to them yet

All hypothetical, with the evidence for the gap.

1. "Here is the prompt, and here is the Tuesday it runs on." Gap: the sample shows prompts next to outputs (890665233928271, 1106125972107418), never next to a business process that recurs (section 4, claim 1).
2. "It did not stick for us either, the first time." Gap: mistakes language absent from all 106 roster ads (section 4, claim 2).
3. "No new tool. The one in your tab is enough." Gap: tool-news is the dominant exploration format for this viewer (Maker School listicles, Skool "trending repo"); nobody says stop switching.
4. "Three businesses, one system each, dated." Gap: every proof is a demo or an unsourced count (section 6); the founders hold dated screens (answer 12).
5. "Bring the task you keep redoing." Gap: nobody asks the viewer to bring their own recurring task; every competitor supplies a curriculum or a client roadmap (sections 3 and 5).
