# Interrogation answers: Operators Workshop

Example run. The founders were not interviewed for this example; answers marked **default** are the intake defaults accepted by the builder, answers marked **owner-provided** come from the founders' standing brief (2026-10-18) or the About page. A member answers these in chat during `/adlab:intake`.

## Batch one: the customer and the fear

1. **Who is the customer trying to look like after buying, and to whom?** owner-provided (inferred from the brief's audience): the owner who runs a tighter operation than their peers, to their partner, their crew and the people they compare notes with; the beginner who is "doing something with AI" to friends who only talk about it.
2. **What does the customer do instead today, including nothing, and what does nothing cost them?** owner-provided: they watch AI content and change nothing on Monday; they do admin by hand after hours. Cost in their words: evenings, repeated mistakes, "fewer people than the work needs". No numbers verified.
3. **Which claim on your site could a competitor make word for word?** default, confirmed by the brief: most of the About page.
4. **What is the worst thing a buyer fears if this goes wrong?** default: they paid and nothing changed, and someone they told finds out. Owner-provided addition: they fear being sold a course by someone with no real business.
5. **What do you do that is inefficient on purpose?** owner-provided: the founders share their own builds as they happen, including mistakes, instead of a polished curriculum. The inefficiency is the point.
6. **What is the ritual of using it, and what does the first week look like?** default (gap): they sign up, poke around, and either use it or forget about it. The brief has no defined first week; this is a hypothesis to fix in the offer.

## Batch two: price, fit and proof

7. **What does your price say about you?** owner-provided: $97/month is a hypothesis; the page frames a lock-in before a rise to $129. Inferred: it says "serious but not premium" in a category where free communities and $9 tiers exist.
8. **Which customers are wrong for you, and how would an ad repel them?** owner-provided: anyone who wants it done for them, a guarantee, individual build reviews or a response-time promise. An ad repels them by showing the founders working on their own businesses, not on the member's.
9. **When someone decides to buy, what just happened in their life?** default: something broke, or they got a bill, or they saw someone else doing it. Hypothesis.
10. **What would have to be true about you that none of your competitors can say?** owner-provided: three founders running real businesses (ecommerce, fabrication, operations software) and showing the AI processes they use in them, including the failures. Inferred: most competitors sell the method; these sell the operating record.
11. **If you had to make it more expensive and better, what would you add?** default: more support, maybe a done-with-you option. Note: the standing brief rules out delivery promises, so this default is not usable in ads.
12. **Which proof do you have that you are not using because it feels too small or too odd?** owner-provided (from the creative work of October 2026): dated warehouse and store photographs, real software screens, approved account screenshots with strict meaning (deposits are not profit; spend is not ad spend), and one honest rejected-result demonstration.

## The five customers (hypothetical, from the founders' seven persona hypotheses)

The buried business owner (quotes, callbacks, invoices after hours); the field-business owner (crews, gear, trucks away from desks); the solo seller (messages, returns, restock, fragmented numbers); the 9-to-5 builder (nights and weekends, skeptical of income promises); the nervous beginner (feels behind, fears breaking things). The freelancer/consultant and the ChatGPT dabbler were also named. None are interviewed customers; label them hypothetical.
