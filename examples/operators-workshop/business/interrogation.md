## Part A. Diagnosis

Ranked. The business does not have a marketing problem. It has a legibility problem first, a category problem second, and it is sitting on the antidote to the market's trust problem without using it.

**1. Legibility.** Nobody can picture Tuesday. The About page is 188 words and most of its claims can be made word for word by a competitor; the brief names four: "Learn to use AI", "Buy back your time", "Build tools, websites, content and automations", "Direct answers and support inside the community" (brief.md, inferred; answer 3, default: "most of the About page"). What nobody in the sample says is the three founders' operating record and the "wins and mistakes" promise (market map section 4). There is no first week: "they sign up, poke around, and either use it or forget about it" (answer 6, default gap). Time delay and effort are unstated (offer.md, inferred). All six persona files list "first week" as missing proof (coverage.md). Hypothetical: the buyer is ambiguity-averse (the stance of `library/prompts/interrogation.md`); if so, the page gives them nothing to resolve it with.

**2. Category.** The business is letting itself be judged as an AI course, in a market where 10 of 12 advertisers sell the route to clients, freelancing or creator income (section 4, observed in the rows) and none is inferred to talk to the "owner of an existing small business who wants to run it better with AI" (section 5, inferred from the rows; adjacent rows do address owners as buyers of video or tools: 710487895220380, 1101916785561103, 1587585239519935). $97 sits between the $9 pole (1403311474997024) and the $184 pole (Maker School end cards) with no framing (section 7). The brief's audience spans beginners, builders and owners (owner-provided): designed for average (`library/psychology/dont-design-for-average.md`). offer.md already asks whether this is "a business community that happens to use AI" (inferred). An undecided category reads as a course.

**3. Trust.** The category has poisoned it: income implications from 4 of 12 advertisers (conventions.md row 2; example ids from the rows 1761940881625624, 1013033575121449, 1129626886138176), guarantees from 5 of 12 (section 3; 1020236937432064, 1656903502712603, 1013033575121449, 2341052899967522, 1094023723542322), member or team counts from 6 of 12 (section 3; 1894923588153698, 1040148231921228), which section 2 calls unsourced for three of them (AI Video Bootcamp, Arcads, AI Operator CLUB). The buyer's fear is "being sold a course by someone with no real business" (answer 4, owner-provided). The page's one proof is a founder revenue line, authority proof and exactly the number p4 discounts (`personas/p4-...md`, inferred). The founders hold dated warehouse and store photographs, real software screens, approved account screenshots with strict meaning, and one rejected-result demonstration (answer 12, owner-provided). None is on the page, and none is cataloged, so consent and permissions for each are unknown.

**4. Price signal.** $97 "says mid and nothing else" (section 7, inferred). The only framing is the rise to $129, which copies the lapsed-checkout urgency card one roster advertiser runs (AI Automation Society, 4201948366761992 and 1750739099623552: two variants, one observation; conventions.md row 7). No Maker School ad in the sample shows a price rise. No date or commitment for the rise exists in offer.md. p6 is inferred to compare $97 to a $20 tool subscription (`personas/p6-...md`, gap).

**5. Social proof.** Five members on the About page, observed 2026-10-23. Six of 12 advertisers run a member or team count (section 3). This cannot be won on counts and must not be faked (`library/rubrics/evidence-integrity.md` rule 4). The way out is proof by type: in the copy and stills read, nobody shows a dated operating artifact (section 6, observed).

**6. Ritual.** None (answer 6, default). `months_retained: 4` in finance.md is a placeholder with no mechanism behind it (`library/psychology/rituals-and-ownership.md`).

**7. Status.** Weak. Answer 1 names who the member wants to look like (owner-provided), but the page's only status line ("not just from people who are selling a course with no real experience") is status for the founders, not the member.

**8. Perception.** Weak. The deliberate inefficiency (builds as they happen, mistakes included; answer 5, owner-provided) is the point, but unframed it reads as "unpolished" rather than "honest". Addressed by R6 (hypothesis).

**9. Context.** Not yet; no ads exist. It becomes a problem if batch one copies the founder-to-camera selfie 6 of 12 advertisers run (section 8). See R7.

### Before any test runs

Every test below lands on the About page. Four lines on it (observed 2026-10-23) conflict with the recommendations. The founders decide each; whatever they decide, the page edit ships before any test runs.

- "earn more", in the tagline "Learn to use AI to buy back your time, earn more and spend less.": an income implication. The standing brief rules income claims out, Part D row 2 inverts the income convention, and `library/platform/creator-category-truth-rules.md` and `library/platform/meta-policy-pitfalls.md` treat implied earnings as a results claim. An ad that refuses to promise income cannot land on a page that implies it.
- "even after it rises to $129": a stated rise with no date or commitment in offer.md. It is the urgency R4 keeps out of every ad, and a rise that never comes is deceptive pricing (`library/rubrics/evidence-integrity.md` rule 5). Remove it, or document a real, enforced rise date in offer.md first.
- "Get direct answers and support from us" and "If you're new to AI, we're here to assist you!": both sit close to the support and response-time promises the standing brief rules out. They contradict R5's "no response-time promise" line and are the drift R1's risk line warns against.

- Risk: truth: week one must be what actually happens and must not drift into support or response-time promises (standing brief). Policy: low. Reputational: a week one not delivered is the "paid and nothing changed" fear made real (answer 4).

Keep it out of cold traffic until then; read cold, a small number is a social-proof problem, not a signal.

**M3. Name the tracks after the businesses.** "The ecommerce track" and "the fabrication track" instead of "Build with AI" and "AI + Business Tactics". Mechanism: keep it concrete (`library/psychology/shotton-illusion-of-choice.md`); `library/psychology/context-changes-value.md`. Persona: p2 and p3, exploration. Cheapest test: a what's-inside static (the 1467334001994239 shape) with business-named tracks against the current names; read CTR.
