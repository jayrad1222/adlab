## 2. Sophistication stage and the required move

**Stage 3 moving to 4, with stage-2 noise.** Ten of twelve roster advertisers run solution-aware ads; mechanism claims appear from 7 of 12 (the ad is the demo; prompt next to output; "tools are not the skill, directing is"; demo-first automation videos). Bigger-claim inflation is loud but concentrated: income lines, "#1", "unlimited", member counts without sources (AI Video Bootcamp, Arcads, AI Operator CLUB). Identification moves exist (callouts "for non-coders", "become the expert", hiring-poster statics) from 4 of 12.

**Required move (Schwartz):** not a bigger claim. A better mechanism with checkable proof, carried by identification: name the exact person and situation, then show the operating process that only this team can show. The sample has no advertiser showing a real business being run with AI; it has tool demos, lifestyle montages and promises of clients.

## 4. Uncontested claims (true about the member, said by nobody in the sample)

- **"We run the businesses you are hearing about, and we show the AI process inside them."** No advertiser shows an operating business; the sample shows tools, montages and client promises. Observed: the About page names three founders with three businesses; the standing brief rules out guru framing.
- **"Here is what did not work."** Mistakes language is absent from the copy, on-screen text and stills of all 106 roster ads; the spoken tracks of the videos were not heard (no transcripts), so this is a claim about what was read, not about what was said. The About page already promises "wins and mistakes".
- **"Use it on your own operation, not to sell AI services."** Ten of twelve advertisers sell the route to clients, freelancing or creator income. The owner who wants a better-run business is not addressed once.
- **"A dated, checkable artifact instead of a count."** Every proof in the sample is a demo or an unsourced number; nobody shows a dated screen, a real decision or a real limitation.

## 6. Proof arms race

Roster ads by proof type (advertisers of 12, from `stats-roster.md`): none 10, demo 8, social-proof 3, authority 3, testimonial 2, data 1. The race is unsourced numbers versus demonstrations of AI output. A different proof type would be distinctive: a dated operating artifact (a real quote email, a real schedule, a real decision with its limitation), which is what the founders have and nobody else can show. Testimonials are rare and, where present, unverifiable (3030674797298673 is a pre-release tester).

## 8. Format mix and gaps

Roster formats (advertisers of 12): founder-to-camera 6, demo 6, screen recording with picture-in-picture 4, offer card 5, listicle 2, skit 2, what's-inside static 1, review card 1, carousel 1. Captions on every video. Gaps: no mini-documentary of a real business (the only day-in-the-life ads are the pottery studio's, a name collision); no text-only "ugly" ad; no founder note; no comparison-with-doing-it-alone; no "mistakes and lessons" video; no two-founder dialogue. The founder-to-camera format is saturated in this feed; the same face in a warehouse or at a real screen is not.

## 11. Ten testable hypotheses

1. An ad that shows one real operating decision inside one of the founders' businesses selects the open personas (owner, trade, seller) better than any "learn AI" line. Test: hook rate against a "learn AI" control.
2. "Here is what did not work" as a hook holds attention longer than a promise hook for the skeptical 9-to-5 builder. Test: hold rate.
3. A dated screen (a real quote email, a real schedule) as proof outperforms a count badge on CTR with the owner persona. Test: CTR, same body.
4. "Use it on your own business" beats "get your first client" for Operators Workshop's persona even though the market runs the latter 5 to 0. Test: hook rate and trial-start rate.
5. A founder note static (text-only, typed letter) competes with founder-to-camera on hook rate at a fraction of the production cost. Test: static versus video, same angle.
6. Naming the price with a reason ("$97 because we show you our own numbers, not a highlight reel") lifts trial-to-paid over the bare price. Test: landing variant.
7. A "who this is not for" line (no done-for-you, no guarantees) raises CTR among the right persona and lowers it among the wrong one. Test: CTR and trial quality.
8. The demo-first structure (result before the tool) transfers from Hashtag NoCode's automation ads to a member's real workflow. Test: hold rate.
9. The retargeting callout that names the objection a visitor held (Maker School's pattern) works for trial-not-started visitors without a guarantee. Test: trial starts from the retargeting set.
10. Two founders in dialogue (a format absent from the sample) reads as more credible than one face. Test: hold rate and comments.

## 12. Evidence limits

- No transcripts: every video is `still-reviewed`; spoken tracks were not heard. Install transcription (`/adlab:setup --with-transcription`) before trusting any claim about what a video says.
- Caps: 15 ads per advertiser. AI Video Bootcamp (837 reported), Hashtag NoCode (444), Arcads (245) and Maker School's keyword count (5,645, mostly unrelated) are samples.
- Three CTtheDisrupter videos and the Miva videos were not downloaded; those rows rest on posters and copy.
- Landing pages: captured for the first 40 ads only; `landing_continuity` is unknown for the rest.
- Two name collisions in the typeahead: "Maker School" (a San Diego pottery studio) and "Kurs Claude Code PRO" (a Polish course matched to Claude Code Club); both are kept in the rows and excluded from the synthesis. "The Claude Club" (India) is a lookalike.
- Three discovery keyword searches with very large result counts (50,001 reported) returned zero loaded ads; the page did not render results in the wait window. Rerun with a longer settle or narrower terms.
- No EU transparency blocks were captured; the ads in the sample do not appear to have delivered in the EU.
- 29 keyword neighbors were skipped as noise (no category term in the copy); 14 of those are listed in the batch file.
- Nothing here is performance data. Hook rates, hold rates and CPAs come only from the member's own readouts.
