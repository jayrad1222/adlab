# Cialdini's seven principles (Robert Cialdini, Influence, 1984; new and expanded edition 2021)

## The idea in three sentences
Cialdini's *Influence* identified six principles that move people to say yes: reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, liking, authority and scarcity. The 2021 edition adds a seventh, unity: shared identity, the sense that the asker is one of us. For ads, each principle is a mechanism you can name in a competitor's creative and deploy deliberately in your own, with the caution that audiences and platforms now detect the fake versions.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Reciprocity: something given before the ask. Example: a free tool, template or audit offered with no form in the ad.
- Commitment and consistency: a small yes first. Example: "answer three questions" quiz funnels, or "if you have ever said X, then..."
- Social proof: counts, faces, reviews. Example: "4,000 members," a scrolling wall of names, a star rating in the first frame.
- Liking: similarity and compliments. Example: a creator who is visibly the persona, opening with "if you are like me..."
- Authority: credentials, uniforms, press. Example: "as seen in," a doctor in a coat, "20 years in the trade."
- Scarcity: limits on time or quantity. Example: "doors close Friday," "12 spots," a countdown.
- Unity: in-group language. Example: "for tradies, by tradies," "people in our group," family framing.

## Use it in ours
- Hook: unity or liking first (who this is from and who it is for), because they set whether the rest is heard.
- Body: one authority signal that is true and checkable, one social-proof item with a name attached, one reason for any scarcity.
- Static: a real face with a real credential, or a real count with a date. Not a stock doctor.
- Video: a creator who matches the persona (liking) describing what they got for free first (reciprocity), then what they bought.
- Landing page: give before you ask (a calculator, a checklist). Make the small commitment visible (a quiz, a plan selector) before the big one.

## How it backfires
Scarcity is the most abused principle and the most policed: false deadlines and fake stock counts are misleading claims under Meta and TikTok policy and under consumer law, and repeat "last chance" campaigns train the audience to ignore them. Authority claims need substantiation; "doctor-recommended" without a named doctor is an FTC problem. Social proof must be real and current, and testimonials must reflect typical results or carry a disclosure. Reciprocity reverses when the gift looks like bait. Unity reverses when the brand claims membership of a group it does not belong to; the group notices first.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation
- What can you give away in the ad itself, with no form, that is actually useful?
- Which of your authority signals could a journalist verify in five minutes?
- Which group does your customer belong to that your competitors are not part of, and are you?
