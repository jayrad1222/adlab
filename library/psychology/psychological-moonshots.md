# Psychological moonshots (Rory Sutherland, Alchemy, 2019)

## The idea in three sentences
A psychological moonshot is a small change in framing, naming, defaults or the order of information that produces a large change in behaviour, at a fraction of the cost of an engineering moonshot. Sutherland's examples run from the bus-stop countdown to renaming a fish so people will eat it. Ad Lab's interrogation asks for three per business, each with a mechanism, a cheapest test and a falsifier.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- A renamed category or product. Example: a "membership" where everyone else sells a "course," or a "clinic" where everyone else is a "studio."
- Information in an unusual order. Example: the price first, the benefit second, the proof third.
- A reframed unit. Example: "per client won" instead of "per month"; "per day" instead of "per year."
- A default changed. Example: the annual plan preselected, or the call booked by default with an opt-out.
- A single word carrying the offer. Example: "founding members" instead of "early-bird discount."
- The same product sold as a different thing. Example: a budgeting app sold as a "couples' Sunday ritual."
- Small, visible reversals of an annoying norm. Example: "no sales call, the price is on the page."

## Use it in ours
- Hook: test a rename. The same offer under two names, one conventional, one reframed, with everything else held constant.
- Body: reorder. Put the element the persona cares about most first, even if it is the price or the exclusion.
- Static: one word, one visual. Moonshots are small enough to fit on a single frame.
- Video: the founder explains the rename in one sentence. "We call it a clinic because..."
- Landing page: defaults and order. Which plan is preselected, what is above the fold, what the button says. These are the cheapest tests on the page.

## How it backfires
A rename that misdescribes the product is a misleading claim: a recorded course called "coaching" will be reported. Default changes that trap the buyer (preselected add-ons, hard-to-find cancel) are dark patterns and draw regulatory attention; the FTC has acted on them. Reframed units must still show the real total price. The mechanism also reverses when the market is sophisticated: in a category that has seen every rename, a new name reads as a gimmick, and the Schwartz sophistication stage should be checked before proposing one.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation
- If you had to call your product something other than what everyone else calls theirs, what would you call it?
- What do customers see first on your page, and is that what they care about most?
- Which small annoyance in your category could you remove and announce?
