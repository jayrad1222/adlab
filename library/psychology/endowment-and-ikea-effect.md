# Endowment effect and IKEA effect (Kahneman, Knetsch and Thaler, 1990; Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012)

## The idea in three sentences
People value a thing more once they own it: in Kahneman, Knetsch and Thaler's mug experiments (1990), owners demanded roughly twice what non-owners would pay for the same mug. The IKEA effect (Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2012) adds that effort invested raises valuation too; people priced their own assembled furniture and origami above identical items made by others. For ads and onboarding, this means trials, "yours already" language and a small early effort can raise both conversion and retention.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Ownership language before purchase. Example: "your plan is ready," "we built your dashboard, come and see it."
- A free trial or free tier framed as possession. Example: "keep the templates even if you cancel."
- Personalisation delivered before the ask. Example: a quiz that produces "your result" and then sells the fix.
- Effort invited early. Example: "spend five minutes setting up your first project" in the onboarding ad.
- Customisation shown. Example: the user's name, logo or colours on the product in the creative.
- Co-creation framed as a feature. Example: "you choose the three habits, we build the schedule."
- Sunk-effort retention copy. Example: "you have logged 14 days, do not stop now."

## Use it in ours
- Hook: give the viewer something that is already theirs. "Your first week's content plan is below."
- Body: describe the small setup effort as part of the value, not as a cost to apologise for.
- Static: the product with the persona's details on it: a mocked-up "your" dashboard, with the mock clearly a mock.
- Video: a screen recording of someone building their own version in two minutes, then using it.
- Landing page: a quiz, calculator or configurator that produces a personalised artefact before the price. Trial language that stresses what the buyer keeps.

## How it backfires
"Your plan is ready" when nothing has been built is a false claim. Personalised mock-ups must not imply the business already holds the viewer's data. Free trials that convert to paid by default without clear notice are a regulatory problem. The IKEA effect reverses when the effort fails: a setup that is too hard produces abandonment, not attachment, and Norton's own work notes the effect depends on completing the task. Sunk-effort retention copy ("do not throw away your progress") slides into manipulation if the product is not working for the user. And endowment makes switching away from a competitor harder, which is a reason to show migration as effortless rather than to attack the old tool.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation
- What could a prospect own, build or customise before they pay?
- What is the smallest effort a new customer makes in week one, and does it produce something they can see?
- What do customers keep if they leave, and do you tell them?
