# Don't design for average (Rory Sutherland, Alchemy, 2019)

## The idea in three sentences
The average customer does not exist; an ad written for the middle of a distribution speaks to nobody in particular, and it is the extreme cases that reveal what the product is really for. Sutherland's rule is to design for the edges, because an edge case described precisely is recognised by the middle as "close enough to me," while a generic description is recognised by nobody. In Ad Lab, personas are situations rather than demographics, and the best hooks speak to one edge so specifically that the middle sees itself.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- A hyper-specific opening situation. Example: "if you run a two-chair salon and your Tuesday is empty..."
- Numbers that could only belong to one kind of person. Example: "47 open tabs, three of them are quotes you have not sent."
- Explicit exclusion. Example: "not for agencies with a sales team" or "if you already have a bookkeeper, skip this."
- The opposite failure in the competitor set: "for busy professionals," "for small business owners," "for anyone who wants more clients." Flag these as average-designed.
- Edge-case testimonials. Example: a 61-year-old starting a business, a single parent running a clinic at night.
- Format choices that only an extreme user would tolerate. Example: a 4-minute ad for a high-ticket niche, or a 6-second ad for impulse buyers.

## Use it in ours
- Hook: write for one person in one situation. If the hook could run for a different business, it is too average.
- Body: keep the specifics (tools, times, amounts, job titles). Specific words are what the middle recognises.
- Static: one subject, one setting, one object that belongs to the edge persona. No collage of "everyone."
- Video: cast the edge, not the middle. A creator who is visibly the extreme case is more believable to the whole range.
- Landing page: a persona switch ("I am a solo coach / I run a team") outperforms a page that tries to be for both.

## How it backfires
Edge-case targeting in Meta ads must not imply personal attributes: "if you are over 50 and in debt" names the viewer's age and financial state and will be rejected. Write the situation, not the person: "the quote that has been sitting in drafts since Tuesday." Over-specific hooks also shrink reach if the edge is tiny and the middle cannot see itself; the test is whether a broader viewer reads the line and thinks "that is me, roughly." And one edge is not the whole bank: rotate edges across the concept set so the account does not depend on a single persona.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation
- Who is your most unusual happy customer, and what did they say the product did for them?
- Which customers are wrong for you, and how would an ad repel them?
- If you could only sell to one person this month, who, and what would you say to only them?
