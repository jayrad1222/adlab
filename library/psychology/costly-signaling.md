# Costly signaling (Rory Sutherland, Alchemy, 2019; after Zahavi's handicap principle)

## The idea in three sentences
Advertising works partly because it is expensive and visible: the effort itself signals that the advertiser expects to be around, and expects the product to earn the money back. Sutherland takes this from biology (Zahavi's handicap principle, where a costly display is credible because a weak animal could not afford it). For a small business the lesson is that a visible, effortful gesture can carry more trust than any claim, because a gesture cannot be faked cheaply.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- Production value out of proportion to the offer. Example: a $29 product advertised with a cinematic shoot and a known presenter.
- A long-running campaign. Example: the same creative live for 180 days or more; the spend is the signal.
- Physical-world gestures. Example: "we send every new member a hand-written card" with the card on screen.
- A founder who shows up repeatedly. Example: daily or weekly talking-head videos from the same person over months.
- Guarantees with teeth. Example: "if you do not get a client in 90 days we refund you and pay for your next month elsewhere."
- Visible investment in the customer's result. Example: "we assign a named coach, here she is," rather than "support included."
- Deliberate slowness or scarcity. Example: "made in batches of fifty, shipped on Fridays."

## Use it in ours
- Hook: lead with the gesture, not the claim. "I write every welcome note by hand" beats "we care about members."
- Body: name what the gesture costs you (time, money, capacity) and why you do it anyway. The cost is the proof.
- Static: show the effort in one frame: the stack of notes, the founder at the desk at 6am, the batch on the bench.
- Video: a process clip with no voice-over for the first few seconds can read as more honest than a polished pitch.
- Landing page: put the guarantee in full, with its conditions, near the price. A guarantee with hidden terms is not a signal.

## How it backfires
Cheap-looking signals of expense read as fake and cost more trust than they buy: stock footage dressed as a shoot, a "limited batch" that never sells out, a guarantee the terms page quietly voids. Guarantees are regulated: the FTC expects stated conditions to be honoured, and Meta rejects ads with misleading claims. A gesture that is effortful for the business but invisible to the buyer signals nothing; the effort has to be seen. And the signal flips when the audience is price-led: heavy production on a budget offer can read as "I am paying for their ads."

## Questions it adds to the interrogation
- What do you do for customers that costs you real time or money and that competitors would never bother with?
- Which of your guarantees would you be nervous to print in full? That is the one worth printing.
- What have you been doing consistently for longest? Could the ad show the track record rather than describe it?
