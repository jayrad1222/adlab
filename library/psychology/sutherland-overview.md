# Rory Sutherland overview (Rory Sutherland, Alchemy, 2019)

## The idea in three sentences
Rory Sutherland is Vice Chairman of Ogilvy UK, co-founder of its behavioural science practice, and the author of *Alchemy* (2019). His argument is that people decide with a parallel logic of meaning, risk, status and context, then explain the decision in rational terms afterwards. Ad Lab's interrogation is written in his frame; this file indexes his concepts and is the home page for the `psychologist` agent, the "Chief Psychology Officer" he says every company is missing.

### The rules from Alchemy
Paraphrased. The seed treats the list as complete; verify ordering against the book before citing a rule by number.

1. The opposite of a good idea can also be a good idea.
2. Don't design for average.
3. It doesn't pay to be logical if everyone else is being logical.
4. The nature of our attention affects the nature of our experience.
5. A flower is simply a weed with an advertising budget.
6. The problem with logic is that it kills off magic.
7. A good guess which stands up to observation is still science. So is a lucky accident.
8. Test counterintuitive things, only because no one else will.
9. Solving problems using rationality is like playing golf with only one club.
10. Dare to be trivial.
11. If there were a logical answer, we would have found it already.

### Psycho-logic in one paragraph
Psycho-logic is Sutherland's name for the reasoning people actually use. It weighs how a choice feels, what it signals to others, what could go wrong and what the context implies, before it weighs features and price. The rational case is assembled afterwards to justify a decision already made. So the viewer must first feel the offer is safe, fitting and worth being seen with; only then does the logical argument get a hearing. Ads that make no sense on a spreadsheet but keep running are usually satisfying psycho-logic.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- No feature claim, long run time. Example: a founder-to-camera ad with no offer stack, still live after 200 days.
- A visible, effortful gesture instead of a benefit. Example: "every order is checked by me" with a shot of it happening.
- The product in a context the category never uses. Example: a budgeting app shown at a wedding.
- A deliberate inefficiency presented as the point. Example: "we only take twelve clients a month."

## Use it in ours
- Hook: name the feeling the buyer needs to be true first.
- Body: one reason for psycho-logic, one for the spreadsheet, in that order.
- Static: show the gesture or the deliberate inefficiency.
- Video: founder on camera, process on screen, context shift in two seconds.
- Landing page: remove uncertainty first, then make the offer.

## How it backfires
Counterintuitive ideas with no mechanism are just contrarian. Cheap imitations of costly signals read as fake. Status lines that imply the viewer's own situation can breach Meta's personal attributes rules. Every reframe must be testable; every "people do X" claim cites a source or is labelled a hypothesis.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation
- What does a customer need to feel is true before your rational case can land?
- What do you do that is inefficient on purpose, and why?
- After buying, who does the customer get to look like, and in front of whom?
