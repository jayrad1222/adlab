# The opposite pass (Rory Sutherland, Alchemy, 2019)

## The idea in three sentences
Sutherland's first rule is that the opposite of a good idea can also be a good idea: Red Bull launched with a small can, a strange taste and a high price, every one of them the inverse of the soft-drink convention, and every one of them a signal of potency. The opposite pass is the deliberate version: take each convention in the category and write its inverse, then ask whether the inverse is also a good idea, for whom, and by what mechanism. It is a mandatory step in `/adlab:angles`, and the prompt lives in `../prompts/opposite-pass.md`.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- An ad that inverts the category's dominant claim. Example: everyone promises "fast results," one ad promises "slow, and it sticks."
- Price position inverted. Example: the only premium offer in a race-to-the-bottom category, or the only cheap one in a premium category.
- Format inverted. Example: a plain text-on-white static in a feed of glossy UGC, or a 3-minute ad where everyone runs 15 seconds.
- Proof inverted. Example: "we have no testimonials yet, here is why you should be first" against a wall of five-star reviews.
- Tone inverted. Example: dry and understated where the category shouts.
- Audience inverted. Example: a product for the "wrong" buyer: accounting software for people who hate accounting.
- Scarcity or abundance inverted. Example: "there is no deadline, decide when you are ready."

## Use it in ours
- Hook: take the category's most repeated opening line from the market map and write its opposite as the first line.
- Body: state the inversion and the mechanism in one breath. "We are slower because every plan is read by a person, which is why ours get followed."
- Static: invert the visual convention. If the set is bright and busy, go sparse and quiet.
- Video: if everyone uses a creator, use the founder; if everyone uses the founder, use a customer with no script.
- Landing page: test the inverted claim as the headline against the conventional one; keep the rest of the page constant so the test is clean.

## How it backfires
Inversion for its own sake is contrarian noise. The pass rejects any inversion that has no mechanism (why would this be better for this persona?) and no cheapest test. Some conventions exist because of policy: inverting "no before-and-after images" or "no income claims" is not an opposite pass, it is a rejection. Inverted price positions change the economics: check the finance decision rules before testing a premium inversion. And some conventions are the market's accumulated learning; the pass surfaces hypotheses, it does not override evidence.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation
- What does every competitor in your category say, and what would it mean if you said the reverse?
- Which of your "weaknesses" could be presented as the point?
- What would you have to be true about you that none of your competitors can say?
