# People like us do things like this (Seth Godin, This Is Marketing, 2018)

## The idea in three sentences
Godin's argument is that marketing works through identity: people adopt products because "people like us do things like this," and the marketer's job is to find the smallest viable audience, earn permission to talk to them, and make something remarkable enough that they tell the others. The phrase is Sutherland's status point in different words, and in Ad Lab a persona is a "people like us" group and the ad names the "things like this." *Purple Cow* (2003) and *Permission Marketing* (1999) supply the other two halves: be worth remarking on, and earn the right to show up.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- A group named, not a demographic. Example: "for trades who quote on their phone," "for coaches who hate selling."
- The behaviour of the group stated as a norm. Example: "the ones who are serious book the call."
- In-group vocabulary used without explanation. Example: niche slang, tool names, inside references.
- Visible exclusion of the wrong audience. Example: "if you want a quick fix, this is not it."
- Permission assets. Example: "join the newsletter," "get the weekly note," with the ad selling the subscription, not the product.
- Remarkability signalled as the proof. Example: "the thing clients keep asking about."
- The founder as a member of the group. Example: "I ran a clinic for nine years before building this."

## Use it in ours
- Hook: name the group and the behaviour. "Owners who send quotes the same day use this."
- Body: describe the group's norm and show the product as how the group does it. Repel the wrong buyer in one line.
- Static: casting and setting that the group recognises as its own, including the imperfect details.
- Video: a creator who is unmistakably a member, speaking as a peer, not as a presenter.
- Landing page: a "who this is for / who it is not for" block, and a low-commitment permission path (newsletter, free tool) for the viewers who are not ready.

## How it backfires
Naming a group by a protected or sensitive attribute (age, religion, health status, financial state) breaches Meta's targeting and personal-attributes rules; name the behaviour and the situation instead. Claiming membership of a group you are not part of is detected by the group first and costs more than it earns. The smallest viable audience can be too small for paid social to learn on; the finance rules set the floor. And "people like us" copy that excludes too hard reads as cliquish to the undecided middle who would have bought.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation
- Which group does your best customer belong to, in their own words, and what do people in that group do that outsiders do not?
- Which customers are wrong for you, and how would an ad politely repel them?
- What do your customers remark on to each other about you?
