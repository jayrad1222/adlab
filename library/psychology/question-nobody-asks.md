# The question nobody asks (Rory Sutherland, Alchemy, 2019)

## The idea in three sentences
Most businesses ask "how do we sell more," which produces more of the same ads. Sutherland's move is to ask the question nobody in the room has asked: why would anyone not buy this, and what would make the not-buying feel risky? The answers expose the real objections, the status quo the product is competing with, and the reframes that make inaction the uncomfortable option.

## Spot it in a competitor ad
- An ad that names the cost of inaction rather than the benefit of action. Example: "every month you wait, the quote you did not send is a client you did not get."
- The non-buyer addressed directly. Example: "if you have looked at this three times and closed the tab, this is for you."
- "What you are doing instead" named. Example: "you have a spreadsheet, it works, and it is costing you Sundays."
- Inaction made socially visible. Example: "your competitors switched in March."
- An objection stated in the ad before the viewer can raise it. Example: "you think you do not have time to set this up. It takes 11 minutes."
- Risk moved onto doing nothing. Example: "the cost of a missed filing is higher than the cost of the service."

## Use it in ours
- Hook: open with the reason people do not buy, in their words from the interrogation. "I thought I could just do it myself."
- Body: show what "nothing" costs, with the persona's own numbers if the intake gave them. Then answer the top objection directly.
- Static: a split that shows the status quo honestly (not mocked) next to the alternative.
- Video: a customer describing why they almost did not buy, then what changed. Ask for the hesitation on camera; it is the most credible part.
- Landing page: an objections block written as "you might be thinking" with plain answers. Put the biggest one first.

## How it backfires
Making inaction feel risky slides into fear-mongering fast, and Meta rejects ads that use alarmist language or imply the viewer's personal situation ("you are losing money right now" aimed at a financial-distress audience). The cost of inaction must be true and must come from the business or the customer, not be invented for effect. The question also reverses when the real reason people do not buy is that the product is wrong for them; in that case the honest ad repels, and the business needs the finance gate, not a reframe.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation
- Why would someone who needs this not buy it? List the top three reasons in their words.
- What does the customer do instead today, including nothing? What does "nothing" cost them?
- What would have to be true for not buying to feel like the risky choice?
