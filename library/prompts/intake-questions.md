# Intake questions

## How to ask them

The `intake` skill asks these after it has drafted `business/brief.md` from the site, so the member is not asked things the site already answers. Two batches of six, then the closing question. Every question comes with a default the member can accept by saying "default" or pressing enter; the default is written into the answers file labeled `default accepted` so the interrogation knows the member did not actually say it. A member who answers in a sentence or two is giving the interrogation more than a member who answers at length; say that before batch one.

Answers are written verbatim to `business/interrogation-answers.md`, labeled owner-provided, with the date. They are the member's words and may be quoted in the interrogation and the persona language banks. They are not evidence about customers unless the member is quoting a customer, in which case note that.

Related: [`interrogation.md`](interrogation.md), [`../psychology/status-and-identity.md`](../psychology/status-and-identity.md), [`../psychology/doorman-fallacy.md`](../psychology/doorman-fallacy.md), [`../psychology/rituals-and-ownership.md`](../psychology/rituals-and-ownership.md), [`../psychology/price-is-information.md`](../psychology/price-is-information.md), [`../psychology/satisficing.md`](../psychology/satisficing.md), [`../psychology/jtbd-four-forces.md`](../psychology/jtbd-four-forces.md), [`../strategy/dunford-positioning.md`](../strategy/dunford-positioning.md), [`../psychology/make-the-invisible-visible.md`](../psychology/make-the-invisible-visible.md).

## Batch one: the customer and the fear

**1. Who is the customer trying to look like after buying, and to whom?**

- Default: "Someone who has their act together, to their partner and their peers."
- Why it is asked: most purchases are partly about who the buyer gets to be and in front of whom. The answer names the status game, which decides the visual world of the ads and what the ad must never make the buyer look like. See `../psychology/status-and-identity.md`.

**2. What does the customer do instead today, including nothing? What does "nothing" cost them, in their words?**

- Default: "They muddle through with a spreadsheet or a cheaper tool, and nothing costs them evenings and a bit of embarrassment."
- Why it is asked: the real competitor is usually the workaround or inaction, not the advertisers in the roster. The cost of nothing, in the customer's words, is the push force and the raw material for problem-led hooks. See `../psychology/jtbd-four-forces.md` and `../strategy/dunford-positioning.md` (competitive alternatives include "do nothing").

**3. Which claim on your site could a competitor make word for word?**

- Default: "Probably most of the home page."
- Why it is asked: the market map will count table-stakes claims; this answer tells the interrogation how aware the member is of being indistinguishable, and it starts the search for the one thing only they can say.

**4. What is the worst thing a buyer fears will happen if this goes wrong? What would make them look foolish?**

- Default: "That they paid and nothing changed, and someone they told about it finds out."
- Why it is asked: buyers satisfice; they avoid the catastrophe before they chase the dream. The ad has to remove that fear before any promise lands. See `../psychology/satisficing.md`.

**5. What is the one thing you do that is inefficient on purpose, and why?**

- Default: "Nothing on purpose; we try to be efficient everywhere."
- Why it is asked: the inefficiency is often the thing that carries the meaning (the doorman). If the member says "nothing", the interrogation will look for the inefficiency they have not noticed, or for the one they removed and should not have. See `../psychology/doorman-fallacy.md`.

**6. What is the ritual of using it? What does the first week look like?**

- Default: "They sign up, poke around, and either use it or forget about it."
- Why it is asked: products with rituals are stickier and easier to show in an ad. "What the first week looks like" is also the single best anxiety-lowering body copy. See `../psychology/rituals-and-ownership.md`.

## Batch two: price, fit and proof

**7. What does your price say about you?**

- Default: "That we are reasonable; we priced to be a bit under the main competitor."
- Why it is asked: price is read before copy. A price set to be "a bit under" says "slightly worse version of them". The interrogation checks the price signal against the positioning. See `../psychology/price-is-information.md`.

**8. Which customers are wrong for you, and how would an ad repel them?**

- Default: "People who want it done for them, or who want a guarantee; we have not tried to repel anyone."
- Why it is asked: a persona's poor-fit signals come from here, and an ad that repels the wrong buyer is cheaper than a refund. It also surfaces promises the member must never make.

**9. When someone decides to buy, what just happened in their life?**

- Default: "Something broke, or they got a bill, or they saw someone else doing it."
- Why it is asked: this is the trigger moment for every persona file and the moment a hook should open on. Life transitions (new job, new baby, new city, new year) are where habits break.

**10. What would have to be true about you that none of your competitors can say?**

- Default: "We care more and we are more hands-on."
- Why it is asked: "we care more" is the most common answer and a competitor can say it word for word. The interrogation pushes until the answer is a fact a stranger could check: a number with a source, a policy, a person, a place, a refusal.

**11. If you had to make the product more expensive and better, what would you add?**

- Default: "More support, maybe a done-with-you option."
- Why it is asked: the answer reveals what the member believes the real value is, and often names a costly signal or a premium tier that would change the price signal. See `../psychology/costly-signaling.md` and `../strategy/hormozi-offer-stack.md`.

**12. Which proof do you have that you are not using because it feels too small or too odd?**

- Default: "A few nice messages and a handful of screenshots we never did anything with."
- Why it is asked: the catalog step will find these, but the member knows where they are. Small, odd, specific proof (a thank-you text, a photo of the workshop, a refund that was never requested) is more believable than a polished claim, and it is usually the only proof a small business actually has. See `../psychology/make-the-invisible-visible.md`.

## The closing question: five customers

**Tell me about three to five real customers, or real situations, one at a time. For each: what was going on for them when they found you, what they did instead before, what they were worried about, and what they said afterwards if they said anything.**

- Default: none. If the member has no customers yet, ask for three to five situations they have personally seen or been in, and label them hypothetical in the answers file.
- Why it is asked: personas are built from situations, not from demographics, and the fastest way to a true situation is a real person the member can picture. Anything the member quotes a customer saying is recorded as the member's recollection, owner-provided, not as a testimonial, and cannot be used in an ad without that customer's consent (see `../rubrics/asset-catalog-schema.md`). Names are not needed; roles and situations are.

## What intake writes

`business/interrogation-answers.md` with each question, the answer or `default accepted`, the date, and the owner-provided label; plus the five customers section. The persona builder, the psychologist and the copywriter all read this file. Do not summarize the answers; keep the member's words.
