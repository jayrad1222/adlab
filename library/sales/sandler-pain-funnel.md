# Sandler pain funnel (David Sandler, *You Can't Teach a Kid to Ride a Bike at a Seminar*, 1995; Sandler Training)

## The idea in three sentences

The Sandler system treats pain as the only reliable reason anyone buys, and the pain funnel is its ladder of questions from a surface complaint down to the cost and the feeling underneath. Each rung gets a more honest answer than the one above: tell me more, be more specific, how long, what have you tried, did it work, what did it cost, how do you feel about that, have you given up. In Ad Lab the funnel is a hook-writing exercise run once per persona; the answers at the bottom rungs are the hooks.

## Spot it in a competitor ad

- A "how long" hook: "How long has the group been stuck at the same number?"
- A "what have you tried" list in the body: "You tried daily posts. You tried a challenge. You tried a discount."
- A "did it work" beat, usually a flat "it didn't."
- A cost line: "That's six months of posting for nothing."
- A feeling line near the end: "And you're starting to wonder if it's you."
- A "have you given up" close: "If you've stopped believing paid ads work for communities, this is for you."
- A transcript that reads like one side of a conversation, or a creator reading DMs aloud and answering them. That is the funnel performed.

## Use it in ours

- Hook: write from the "how long" rung or the "have you given up" rung. Those two rungs produce hooks that select the right viewer and repel the wrong one.
- Body: the "what have you tried" rung as a short list in the persona's words, then the "did it work" beat. This is also an accusation audit, so it pairs with the [Voss toolkit](voss-toolkit.md).
- Static: one line from the feeling rung, stated as a situation and not a diagnosis. Headline and nothing else.
- Video: run the funnel aloud as a monologue. Each rung is a cut. The creator answers the questions as if they were the viewer a year ago.
- Landing page: the funnel becomes the first section, rung by rung, ending on the cost. The offer section opens right after.
- DM or sales page: use the real questions in order. Do not skip rungs. The reader will answer the cost and feeling questions only after the earlier ones.

## How it backfires

- The funnel is a diagnostic, not a weapon. Copy that drills into pain and then offers nothing is cruelty, and comments will say so.
- The feeling rung touches mental health and body image in many categories. Second-person statements about how the viewer feels can trip Meta's personal attributes rule. Phrase feelings as the situation's effect, not the viewer's state.
- "Have you given up" can read as contempt when the creator has no standing. Earn it with a pratfall or a shared history.
- Running every rung in a seven-second hook produces a wall of questions. Pick one rung per asset.
- A funnel built from the member's guesses, not from intake, reviews or comments, produces hooks nobody recognizes.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation

- Run the full funnel for this persona. What is the answer at each rung, in their words?
- Which rung does the competitor's hook come from?
- How long has the viewer had this problem, and does the ad acknowledge that?
- What have they already tried, and does the ad name it?
- What has it cost them, stated fairly?
- Is the feeling line about the situation, or about the person?
