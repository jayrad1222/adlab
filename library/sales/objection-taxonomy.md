# Objection taxonomy

Purpose: a reference for the eight recurring objections, the copy move that answers each, two original example lines, the proof that closes it, and the policy caution where one applies. Each objection should be heard in the member's intake, reviews or comments before an asset is built around it. The taxonomy is a checklist, not a source of truth.

## Price

How it sounds: "That's a lot." "I can't justify that right now."

Copy move: reframe the unit. Price per day, per use, or against the cost of the workaround the viewer already pays for.

Example lines:
- "It's three dollars a day. The freelancer you hired last month cost more and left."
- "Compare it to the ad spend you'll waste learning this alone."

Proof: a plain cost comparison the viewer can check.

Policy caution: the per-day figure must reconcile with the actual price.

## Trust

How it sounds: "Why should I believe you?" "The testimonials look paid."

Copy move: proof, pratfall, guarantee, show the work. Admit a real flaw before claiming a strength.

Example lines:
- "Our first three months of ads lost money. Here is what we changed."
- "No revenue screenshots. Just the system, and you decide."

Proof: real customers with material connections disclosed, the member's own history including the misses, a guarantee with no hidden conditions.

Policy caution: FTC endorsement rules apply. A scripted founder is not a customer testimonial.

## Time

How it sounds: "I don't have time for this." "I'll start when things calm down."

Copy move: shrink the first step. Describe the first week or the first hour, and make it small.

Example lines:
- "Week one is one post and one setting change. That's the whole week."
- "If you have twenty minutes on Sunday, you have enough."

Proof: a first-week checklist on the landing page.

Policy caution: none, if the time estimate is honest.

## Fit

How it sounds: "This isn't for my kind of business." "My market is different."

Copy move: "this is for ... this is not for ..." in two short lists. Exclude the wrong persona on purpose.

Example lines:
- "For communities under five hundred members with a product that already sells. Not for people still choosing a niche."
- "If you already run paid ads, this will bore you. If you've never run one, start here."

Proof: examples from the viewer's category, or a plain statement that the member has not worked in it.

Policy caution: fit statements about the business are fine; second-person personal attributes are not, in regulated categories.

## Priority

How it sounds: "Not the biggest problem right now." "Other things come first."

Copy move: the cost of inaction, stated fairly, in terms the viewer already tracks.

Example lines:
- "Every month without a working ad is a month of growth that comes only from your own posting."
- "Nothing bad happens if you wait. Nothing changes either."

Proof: the member's own timeline in plain words, or a mechanism explanation.

Policy caution: cost of inaction is where fear-mongering starts. Keep it to what the viewer can already see.

## I can do it myself

How it sounds: "I can figure this out." "There are free videos on this."

Copy move: acknowledge that they can, then show the hidden cost of doing it alone: time, wasted spend, no second opinion.

Example lines:
- "You can. Most people who do spend months finding out what we'll tell you on day one."
- "Free videos teach the parts. Nobody tells you which part is wrong for you."

Proof: what the member's own learning curve cost, told honestly.

Policy caution: do not invent the DIY dollar figure. Use the member's numbers or leave it out.

## Tried before

How it sounds: "I bought one of these already." "Nothing changed."

Copy move: accusation audit plus mechanism difference. Say the objection first, then explain why the mechanism differs, not why the brand is better.

Example lines:
- "The last course gave you templates and no feedback. This is the feedback part."
- "The last one taught you to post more. This one teaches you where the posts go."

Proof: a side-by-side of the two mechanisms, or one member with the same history.

Policy caution: name the category of the failed solution, not a competitor.

## Not now

How it sounds: "Maybe next quarter."

Copy move: a reason why now, never fake urgency. A real cohort date, a real price change with a date, or the cost of inaction from the priority objection.

Example lines:
- "The cohort starts on the first because the ads run during the month, not after it."
- "The price goes up when the next module ships. That is the only reason to hurry."

Proof: the date and its reason on the landing page, and a history of honoring them.

Policy caution: a deadline that resets or a "limited spots" line with no limit is a misleading claim. Check a competitor's Ad Library run dates before copying their constraint.
