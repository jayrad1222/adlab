# Klaff frames (Oren Klaff, *Pitch Anything*, 2011)

## The idea in three sentences

Klaff argues that every pitch is a collision of frames, that the stronger frame absorbs the weaker one, and that the frame is set in the first moments. His tools are frame control, status, prizing (the seller as the prize, so the offer is selective and "not for everyone"), intrigue (an open loop the listener wants closed) and the time constraint. Use these with care: each one works only when the constraint behind it is real, and false exclusivity is a trust killer.

Klaff explains the mechanism with a story about the "croc brain" and hot cognition. The behavioural claims are his own account, not cited research; treat them as contested and use the tools on their own merits.

## Spot it in a competitor ad

- "This isn't for everyone," "we turn away most applicants," "apply to see if you qualify." That is prizing.
- Application forms instead of checkout buttons. Check whether anyone is actually declined.
- Intrigue hooks that open a loop and refuse to close it until the end: "The one thing I got wrong for three years."
- Time frames: "doors close Friday," "we open twice a year." Check the Ad Library for how long the ad has run; a permanent Friday deadline is a fake constraint.
- Status cues in the frames: expensive settings, name-drops, a creator talking down to the camera rather than up.
- The seller setting terms: "Here's how this works. You book a call. We decide if we take you."
- A reframe of the viewer's objection as a status move: "If you need to think about it, this isn't for you."

## Use it in ours

- Hook: an intrigue frame that is honest. Open a loop about the mechanism, not about a secret. "There is one reason the first fifty members are the hardest, and it isn't what you think."
- Body: a prize frame only where the member genuinely selects. "We take six communities a quarter because we run the ads ourselves" is a prize frame with a reason-why. The reason is what makes it true.
- Static: "This is for ... This is not for ..." in two short lists. Fit filtering is the one Klaff tool that costs nothing in trust when it is accurate.
- Video: a status frame comes from showing the work, not the lifestyle. Film the process. A rented car is the opposite of status in 2026 creator markets.
- Landing page: a real time constraint with its reason stated. "Enrollment closes on the 30th because the cohort starts on the 1st." The constraint and its reason are one sentence.
- DM or sales page: set the next step yourself, once. "If this fits, reply with your community link and I'll tell you whether we can help." Setting terms is frame control; nagging is not.

## How it backfires

- False scarcity. A deadline that resets, a "limited spots" line with no limit, an application nobody fails. Each one is a misleading claim under Meta policy and a trust loss in the comments.
- "Not for everyone" that excludes everyone. Fit filtering must leave a real group inside the fence.
- Status posturing. Lifestyle shots, name-drops and "I don't need your money" read as the thing they are trying to hide. Costly signaling works when the cost is visible and real; see [costly signaling](../psychology/costly-signaling.md).
- Intrigue that never pays off. If the loop closes on "buy to find out," the viewer feels played.
- Frame control in a DM becomes pressure. The viewer has every right to leave, and copy that pretends otherwise gets reported.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation

- What frame does the competitor set in the first three seconds, and does the member's product support that frame?
- Is the scarcity true? What is the reason for the limit, in one sentence?
- Who does the member actually decline, and why?
- What loop could the hook open that the body can honestly close?
- What is the real cost the member has paid that would signal status if shown?
- If the "not for" list were honest, who would be on it?
