# Gap Selling (Keenan, *Gap Selling*, 2018)

## The idea in three sentences

Keenan argues that nobody buys a product; they buy the move from a current state to a future state, and the size of that gap is the value. The seller's job is to diagnose the current state in detail (the facts, the problems, their impact and their root cause) and to make the future state concrete before saying anything about what they sell. In an ad, that means making the current state vivid and the future state specific, without fabricating a result.

## Spot it in a competitor ad

- A "right now" opening that describes the viewer's week in detail: "Right now you check the member count before you check your messages."
- A pivot word that marks the bridge: "imagine," "picture," "instead," "what if."
- A future state described as a scene rather than a number: "Tuesday. Three new members joined overnight and you had nothing to do with it."
- The gap stated as a sentence: "The difference between those two Tuesdays is one system."
- A static split into two panels of copy, current state left, future state right. In health and body categories, check whether the panels are images; that is a policy problem.
- A quantified gap. Note it as a results claim and check whether the ad supports it.
- Landing pages with a "where you are" section followed by a "where you'll be" section, then the product.

## Use it in ours

- Hook: the current state in one concrete detail the viewer recognizes. Not "struggling to grow" but the specific thing they did yesterday.
- Body: spend most of the body on the current state. Keenan's point is that the diagnosis sells; a thin current state produces a thin gap. Then one or two lines of future state.
- Static: two short copy blocks, "now" and "after," each a scene. No before-and-after imagery in regulated categories.
- Video: first five seconds are the current state, filmed the way it actually looks. Bridge at the midpoint. Future state shown as a routine, not a trophy.
- Landing page: a current-state section the viewer can check against their own life, a future-state section written in the same level of detail, and the product as the bridge between them.
- DM or sales page: ask about the current state first and keep asking. "What does a normal week look like? What happens when a post flops? What did you try?" Only then describe the future state in their terms.

## How it backfires

- A fabricated future state is a results claim. Keep it to scenes and mechanisms, or to outcomes the member has evidence for.
- Before-and-after images are not allowed in health and body categories on Meta. Copy that describes the two states is a different thing from a picture of a body.
- A future state too far from the current one reads as fantasy. The viewer should be able to see the first step.
- A current state painted too dark shames the viewer. Describe the situation, not their character, and keep second-person attributes out of it.
- The gap is the member's opinion. If the viewer does not feel the current state is a problem, the ad is describing someone else.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation

- What does the viewer's current state look like on an ordinary day, in detail?
- What is the root cause of that state, as distinct from its symptoms?
- What does the future state look like on an ordinary day, in the same level of detail?
- Can we describe the gap without a number we cannot support?
- Which part of the gap does the product actually close, and which part is on the viewer?
- Is the first step across the gap visible in the ad?
