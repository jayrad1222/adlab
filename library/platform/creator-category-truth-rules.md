# Creator category truth rules

These are the Operators Workshop house rules, written out so anyone selling courses, communities, coaching, or services can apply them. They sit above platform policy: an ad can pass Meta review and still fail this file.

## Why this category needs its own rules

Courses, communities, and coaching sell a future outcome that depends mostly on the buyer, so every proof claim is fragile. The advertiser knows what they taught; they rarely know what most buyers did with it. These rules stop the best case being sold as the normal case, and a demo as a result.

## The rules

### No earnings guarantees

No ad, landing page, or DM script promises income, a number of clients, a revenue figure, or a timeline to any of them. Not "guaranteed," not "or your money back," not "most of our members." A refund policy is an offer term and can be stated as one. It is not a results promise and must not be framed as one. Meta rejects most income claims; this rule bans the ones Meta might let through.

### A scripted founder is not a customer testimonial

If the founder reads lines written to sound like a customer, that is a script, not an endorsement. Founder-to-camera is a valid format when the founder speaks as the founder. A customer story told by the founder must be attributed ("one of our members, Sam, told me") and must be real. First-person customer lines go only to the customer who lived them.

### A demonstration is not a deployed system

Showing a workflow, an automation, an AI agent, or a funnel running on screen proves that it can run. It does not prove that any customer has it running, that it produced revenue, or that it held up for a month. Label demos as demos. "Here is what the system does" is honest. "Here is the system that books my clients 40 calls a month" is a results claim and needs the result to exist.

### Deposits are not profit

Money collected is not money earned. A deposit, a pre-order, a booked call, a "closed" deal before payment, or gross revenue before refunds and costs are all upstream of profit. An ad that says "made $20k" from a screenshot of a Stripe balance is making a claim the screenshot does not support. If the number is used at all, say what it is: gross sales, deposits, or booked revenue.

### Business spend is not ad spend

"I spent $5k and made $50k" invites the reader to compute a return. If the $5k includes software, contractors, a course, and a laptop, the ratio is false. Ad spend means money paid to an ad platform. Everything else is business spend. Keep the two words separate in copy and in the finance inputs, because the test plan's CPA math depends on the distinction.

### A prototype is not a result

A template, a prompt pack, a draft funnel, or a system built last week is a prototype until a customer has used it and something happened. Prototypes can be shown. They are shown as what they are: a thing you will get, not a thing that has worked.

### Label hypotheses

Every claim about why people buy, what they feel, or what they would do is either sourced or marked as a hypothesis. "Members tell us they join for the accountability" needs a source (a survey, comments, interviews). "We think the real draw is accountability" is a labeled hypothesis and is fine as one. The interrogation and the ad bank both run on hypotheses; the sin is dressing one as a fact.

## The four evidence labels

Every fact in the market map, the personas, the bank, and the test plan carries one of these labels.

- **Observed.** Seen directly by the tool or the analyst: an ad in the library with an ID, a review quoted with its source, a transcript read, a frame watched. The bank entry says what was inspected (copy only, still reviewed, or video watched).
- **Owner-provided.** The member said it: their price, their margin, their member count, their refund rate, their customer story. Recorded as their statement, with the date. Not verified by the tool.
- **Inferred.** A conclusion drawn from observed or owner-provided facts. "This advertiser has run the same hook for 90 days, so it is probably working for them" is an inference, and longevity is not profitability.
- **Hypothetical.** An idea to test. A persona's unstated fear, a predicted objection, a reason a concept might land. Labeled so the test plan can try to falsify it.

A claim with no label is treated as hypothetical until someone labels it.

The copywriter writes to the labels: observed facts can be stated, owner-provided facts with the owner's name on them, inferred claims softened to a mechanism, and hypotheticals kept in the brief as the reason the concept exists.

## What the critic sends back

Any income or outcome promise. Any first-person customer line without a named customer. Any demo described as a deployed result. Any revenue number not labeled as gross, deposit, or booked. Any spend figure that mixes ad spend with other costs. Any "people want X" line with no source and no hypothesis tag.
