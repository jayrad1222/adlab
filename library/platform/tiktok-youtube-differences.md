# How creative differs across Meta feed, Reels, TikTok and YouTube

This file describes how the same concept has to change shape when it moves between Meta feed, Reels, TikTok, and YouTube, so the bank does not ship one cut everywhere. The differences are in native look, sound, pacing, length, and text, and they are craft observations rather than platform rules.

## The short version

A concept travels; a cut does not. Meta feed tolerates ads that look like ads. Reels and TikTok reward content that looks like it belongs to the feed. YouTube gives more time and a viewer who chose to be there. Make the concept once, then make the cut per surface.

## Meta feed (Facebook and Instagram)

- **Native look.** The feed is mixed: friends, Pages, ads. Polished and ugly both work. Barry Hott's point stands here most strongly: a text-only "ugly" static or a screenshot-style ad often beats a designed one because it does not read as an ad.
- **Sound.** Assume sound off. Captions are mandatory and the visual has to carry the message alone.
- **Pacing.** Slower than Reels. A static has a two-second window; a feed video can take a beat before the hook as long as the first frame already says something.
- **Length.** Statics and carousels do a lot of the work. Feed video can run longer than Reels, but the readout usually shows the drop-off early, so front-load.
- **Text overlay norms.** Headlines in the image are normal. The primary text above the ad carries the long copy, so the image does not need to.

## Reels

- **Native look.** Vertical, person-first, shot on a phone. A face talking in the first second, a screen recording with picture-in-picture, or a POV clip looks native. A horizontal video with black bars or a designed card does not.
- **Sound.** Mixed. More sound on than feed, but still a large silent share. Captions stay on. Trending audio matters less for ads than for organic.
- **Pacing.** Fast. The hook is the first frame and the first spoken line. Cuts every few seconds. No intro.
- **Length.** Short. Most Reels ads sit in the 15 to 45 second range as a working guideline; a longer cut should justify each extra beat.
- **Text overlay norms.** Big, short lines inside the safe zone, usually one idea per line, often the hook written on screen in the first frame. Lower-third caption style, not a headline block.

## TikTok

- **Native look.** The strictest. Anything that looks produced gets skipped. Creator-shot, direct address, imperfect lighting, a real room. The best TikTok ads are indistinguishable from the creator's own posts, which is why the UGC brief says "ugly is fine."
- **Sound.** Sound on by default. Voice, music, and sound effects carry more of the message than on Meta. Still caption everything, because the platform's own captions are inconsistent and many people watch muted.
- **Pacing.** Fastest of the four. The hook is often spoken over a jump cut in the first second. Pattern interrupts, confessions, and "nobody talks about" hooks fit the grammar.
- **Length.** Short, with a long tail. Short cuts for the hook test, longer story cuts once a hook has proven itself.
- **Text overlay norms.** On-screen text in the platform's own font style reads as native; a brand font reads as an ad. Text sits in the middle band because the bottom overlay is tall.
- **Research note.** TikTok's public tools showed no Skool-community advertising in the October 2026 probe. Competitor evidence here will be thin for most members; say so rather than inferring from absence.

## YouTube (in-stream and Shorts)

- **Native look.** Two surfaces. Shorts behaves like Reels and TikTok. In-stream ads play before or during content the viewer chose, so a more produced look is tolerated and sometimes expected.
- **Sound.** Sound on. The viewer is already listening. The voice track can carry the argument, and music can set tone.
- **Pacing.** In-stream has a skip button at five seconds on many formats. The first five seconds must give a reason to stay or deliver the whole message to someone who leaves. Shorts follow the three-second rule.
- **Length.** The widest range. Six-second bumpers, fifteen to thirty second skippable cuts, and long founder or explainer videos all exist. A long cut is the only place where a full mechanism story fits in an ad.
- **Text overlay norms.** Less on-screen text than on vertical surfaces. A clear end card with the offer matters more here because there is no primary text block beside the video.
- **Research note.** Google's Ads Transparency Center links the YouTube creative, the best free source for competitor video ads.

## Practical rules for the bank

1. Write the concept and hooks once; note which hooks are spoken-first (TikTok, YouTube) and which are written-first (feed, Reels).
2. Render the 9:16 master first; cut 4:5 and 1:1 from it. Watch every cut muted.
3. Keep an ugly variant of every concept for Meta feed.
4. Log the surface in the asset catalog so the readout compares like with like.
