# Concept variation versus creative iteration (Dara Denney, Motion library and Creative Strategy Bootcamp, 2025 to 2026)

## The idea in three sentences

Denney draws a line between a concept variation, which is a different idea (a different angle, persona, format or mechanism), and a creative iteration, which is the same idea with a different hook, opener, colour or cut. Teams that confuse the two produce twenty versions of one ad and call it testing, and then learn nothing when all twenty perform the same. Her working rule is that the hook is a science with named categories, and the creative strategist's job is to build a concept library, tag it, and read the data back into the next round.

## Spot it in a competitor ad

- **Iteration cluster:** several ads with the same body, proof and offer and only the first line or first frame changed. Example: five videos with identical footage from second four onward and five different openers. Tag as one concept, five hooks.
- **True concept variation:** a different angle family or persona. Example: one ad is problem-led for solo owners, another is proof-led for studios, a third is a comparison against the spreadsheet. Three concepts.
- **Hook category tagged:** read the first line or three seconds and assign a hook type from the taxonomy (callout, question, bold claim, number, curiosity gap, pattern interrupt, and so on). A roster that uses two hook types is thin.
- **Format-only changes:** the same script as a static, a carousel and a video. Format is a concept lever only when it changes the idea; otherwise it is an iteration.
- **Library hygiene signals:** consistent naming visible in ad names or file names in the ad library (angle, persona, hook codes). It suggests a team that reads data.
- **Survivor pattern:** which concepts keep spawning new iterations over months. The parent concept is the winner, not the latest child.

## Use it in ours

- **Hook:** three to six hooks per concept, each from a different hook category. Two hooks from the same category are not a test.
- **Body:** one concept, one body. If changing the hook forces a different body, you have a new concept; file it as one.
- **Static:** iterate statics on headline and visual; vary concepts on angle and persona. Name files so the two are never confused (concept code, hook code, variant code).
- **Video:** shoot the body once and cut multiple openers. That is the cheap iteration. New concepts need new scripts, and the plan budgets for them separately.
- **Landing page:** one page per concept where the promise differs; the same page for all iterations of a concept.
- **Market map:** the format mix and gaps section counts concepts, not ads. A competitor with sixty ads and four concepts has four concepts.

## How it backfires

- Calling everything a concept to look diverse. The critic checks that two "concepts" differ in angle, persona or mechanism, not in colour.
- Iterating a loser. When a concept fails across three hooks, more hooks will not save it; the bank retires it.
- Reading a hook win as a concept win. The best opener on a weak idea still loses to the second-best opener on a strong one over time.
- Her framework is practitioner guidance from a vendor's bootcamp and library, not a measured study. The definitions are useful; any performance claims attached to them in vendor posts are vendor claims.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation

- How many distinct concepts does the member actually have, once hooks and formats are collapsed?
- Which hook categories has the member never tried?
- Which concept keeps earning new iterations, and why has nobody said so out loud?
- Is the next round's budget split between new concepts and iterations of winners, or all on one?
- Who reads the results and writes the next brief?
