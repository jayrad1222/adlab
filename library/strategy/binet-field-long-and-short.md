# Brand building and activation (Les Binet and Peter Field, *The Long and the Short of It*, 2013)

## The idea in three sentences

Binet and Field analysed IPA Effectiveness Awards cases and separated two effects: brand building, which is emotional, broad, slow and compounds, and activation, which is rational, targeted, fast and decays when spend stops. The two need each other, because activation alone gives sales spikes that fall back to baseline and brand alone gives memory nobody acts on. Their 60/40 split in favour of brand is a guide drawn from large advertisers, not a law for a small business; Ad Lab treats it as a direction, not a budget rule.

## Spot it in a competitor ad

- **Activation ad:** offer, price, deadline, specific CTA, rational argument. Example: "Book this week and the setup fee is waived." Expect a short run and frequent replacement.
- **Brand ad:** emotion, story, identity, little or no offer, brand assets prominent. Example: a ninety-second founder story with no price and a soft "learn more."
- **Roster ratio:** count activation versus brand ads per competitor. All activation means a competitor living on retargeting and discounts.
- **Decay evidence:** offer ads change every few weeks while one story ad runs for months. Longevity, with the leaderboard caveats printed, is the signal.
- **Emotional register in the first ten seconds:** humour, pride, relief, belonging, versus features and numbers.
- **Fame devices:** a stunt, a strong opinion, a character built to be talked about. Rare among small advertisers; note it when it appears.

## Use it in ours

- **Hook:** activation hooks state the offer or the number; brand hooks state the identity or the feeling. Build both into every concept, because a small budget cannot run two campaigns.
- **Body:** let the activation ad carry a small brand element (the founder, the colour, a line of belief) and the brand ad carry a clear next step. Pure versions of either are a luxury.
- **Static:** offer cards, comparison charts and FAQ cards activate; founder notes, memes and persona cards lean brand. The bank holds both.
- **Video:** founder story and day-in-the-life build brand; demo, objection handling and offer-led videos activate. Keep the assets constant across all of them.
- **Landing page:** the page is activation. Close the ad's promise fast; do not restart the story.
- **Market map:** record the roster's brand-to-activation ratio. All activation leaves a modest brand layer uncontested; all story leaves a clear offer uncontested.

## How it backfires

- Importing 60/40 into a business that needs cash this month. Binet and Field say the split depends on category, maturity and scale; for a small advertiser the right ratio is unknown and the finance gate decides. See [decision rules](../finance/decision-rules.md).
- Calling any ad with music "brand building." A brand ad still needs attribution and a reason to remember; see [Sharp](sharp-mental-physical-availability.md).
- Reading a long-running competitor ad as proof it works. It may be cheap, forgotten, or kept alive by a lazy account. Print the caveat.
- The IPA data is a sample of award entries, a selection bias the authors acknowledge. Mark the proportions as contested.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation

- If the member stopped all ads tomorrow, what would still be remembered in a month?
- Which of the member's ads produce a spike, and what happens the week after?
- What is the one emotional note the member can truthfully own that the roster does not?
- What is the smallest brand element that can ride on every activation ad at no cost?
- What does the finance model say the member can afford to wait for?
