# Three realities and T-E-E-P (Sarah Levinger, Motion Creative Strategy Bootcamp talk, June 2026)

## The idea in three sentences

Levinger's research system separates three realities: customer reality (what buyers actually feel and say), brand reality (what the brand can truthfully claim) and presentation reality (what the ad shows), and a winning ad is where all three agree. Ads work as mirrors, so the viewer must see themselves in the first seconds, which makes emotional identity a better targeting input than demographics. Her T-E-E-P model (trigger, exploration, evaluation, purchase) describes the buyer's path, and each stage prefers different formats.

## Spot it in a competitor ad

- **Customer-reality language:** phrases that sound lifted from a review or a comment, with the texture of real speech. Example: "I was tired of being the only one who knew the passwords."
- **Brand-reality overreach:** a presentation that promises more than the brand's page or reviews support. Example: a transformation story for a product whose reviews are about convenience.
- **Mirror hook:** the opening shows a person, a situation or a feeling the target would call "that's me." Example: a phone screen showing eleven unanswered client messages at 10pm.
- **Trigger-stage format:** pattern interrupts, identity callouts, memes, POV. Example: "POV: your first slow month since you quit."
- **Exploration-stage format:** education, mechanism, whiteboard, screen recording. Example: "here is how we fill the calendar in three steps."
- **Evaluation-stage format:** proof, comparison tables, objection handling, real testimonials. Example: an us-versus-them chart against the spreadsheet.
- **Purchase-stage format:** offer card, guarantee, FAQ, friction removal. Example: "15-minute setup call, cancel anytime."

## Use it in ours

- **Hook:** write the hook from the persona file's emotional language, not from the product. Test it with the two-second stranger test: would the target say "that's me" before they could say what is being sold?
- **Body:** check the three realities in order. Customer reality from reviews and threads; brand reality from what the member actually delivers; presentation reality is what is left after the first two agree.
- **Static:** trigger wants memes, tweet screenshots and persona cards; exploration wants three-reasons and FAQ cards; evaluation wants review cards, comparison charts and "I was skeptical" quotes; purchase wants the offer card.
- **Video:** trigger wants POV, skit and pattern-interrupt openers; exploration wants whiteboard explainers, demos and screen recordings; evaluation wants real testimonials, objection handling and comparison-with-doing-it-alone; purchase wants founder-to-camera stating the offer and the first step.
- **Landing page:** the page is evaluation and purchase: proof, then offer, then friction removal. Do not re-run the trigger.
- **Market map:** the persona coverage heatmap records which stage each competitor's formats serve. Most small rosters cluster at trigger and purchase and leave exploration and evaluation thin.

## How it backfires

- Mining reviews for emotional language and then quoting them as testimonials. Mined language informs the brief; it does not become an endorsement unless the person consents and the quote is real. See [FTC endorsements](../platform/ftc-endorsements.md).
- Mirror hooks in sensitive categories slide into implied personal attributes. "Tired of being broke?" is a policy flag; "the month the invoices stop" is a situation.
- The three realities and T-E-E-P are a practitioner's model from talks and articles, not a peer-reviewed framework. Her company name is also inconsistent across sources. Treat the model as a useful working structure, not a finding.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation

- What do customers say in their own words when the product works, and where is the quote?
- What can the brand truthfully claim, and what in the current ads goes past that?
- Which stage of T-E-E-P does each ad in the bank serve, and is any stage empty?
- Would the target recognize themselves in the first two seconds, or only the product?
