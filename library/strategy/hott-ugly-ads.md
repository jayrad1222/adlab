# Ugly ads (Barry Hott, public talks and posts, 2022 to 2026)

## The idea in three sentences

Hott's argument is that ugly, native-looking ads often beat polished ones because they do not look like ads, so the viewer's ad-skipping reflex does not fire and the content gets judged as content. Authenticity reads as a signal to the viewer and, by the account of practitioners, to the platform's ranking as well, since the ranking rewards engagement that polished creative rarely earns from cold traffic. Ad Lab keeps "ugly" as a first-class format in the taxonomy, and every bank must include at least two.

## Spot it in a competitor ad

- **Text-only static:** black or white background, system font, no logo, a sentence or two. Example: a plain card reading "we stopped answering DMs after 6pm and bookings went up."
- **Native-looking video:** phone-shot, no music or trending audio, no lower-thirds, natural light, a person talking to the front camera. Example: a founder in a car explaining one thing in forty seconds.
- **Screenshot format:** a tweet, a DM thread, an email, a notes-app page, a calendar. Example: a screenshot of a customer message with the reply. The critic checks these are real.
- **Imperfection kept:** typos left in, a cough, a restart, bad framing. Note when the imperfection looks staged.
- **Platform-native idiom:** green-screen, duet, caption style matching the feed. The ad borrows the feed's grammar.
- **Longevity of the ugly ad:** in the roster, ugly ads that have run longer than polished ones are a direct test of Hott's claim for that category. Print the dates.

## Use it in ours

- **Hook:** the ugly hook is a plain sentence that sounds like a person. "I did the maths on our no-shows" works; "Discover the secret to" does not.
- **Body:** one idea, said once, no graphics. If it needs a chart, it is not an ugly ad; file it as a different format.
- **Static:** the text-only card, the founder note typed in a plain editor, the real DM or email screenshot, the meme. Minimum two per bank. No brand colour, or a single brand element at most.
- **Video:** founder-to-camera on a phone, one take, captions on. Keep the restart if it happened. Shoot in the place the work actually happens.
- **Landing page:** an ugly ad that lands on a glossy page breaks the register. A plain page with the same voice converts the trust the ad built; test it against the polished page.
- **Market map:** record how many competitors run any ugly format. A roster of polished ads leaves the native register uncontested, which is the usual case in local service and community categories.

## How it backfires

- Fake ugly. A staged DM screenshot or an invented customer quote is a fabricated endorsement, and it is the first thing the critic looks for. See [FTC endorsements](../platform/ftc-endorsements.md) and [evidence integrity](../rubrics/evidence-integrity.md).
- Ugly as a brand. A premium service whose only ads are text cards can price itself down; see [costly signaling](../psychology/costly-signaling.md). Mix registers.
- The platform-ranking claim is practitioner consensus, not Meta documentation. Treat "the algorithm rewards authenticity" as contested; the viewer-reflex argument stands on its own.
- Ugly ads fatigue too. A text card that wins does not win forever; refresh on the same schedule as everything else.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation

- Which of the member's existing posts, DMs or notes would work as an ad with no changes?
- What would the founder say about the business to a friend in a car, and has that been filmed?
- Does the roster contain a single ad that does not look like an ad?
- Which real customer message would the customer let the member screenshot?
- What does the member's polish cost, and what does it signal?
