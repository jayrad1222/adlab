# The value equation (Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*, 2021, paraphrased)

## The idea in three sentences

Hormozi's value equation, paraphrased here, says perceived value rises with the dream outcome and the perceived likelihood of achieving it, and falls with the time delay and the effort and sacrifice required. An ad raises the top of the fraction with specificity and proof and lowers the bottom by showing that the first step is small and soon. Ad Lab keeps the equation as an editing tool for offer lines and hooks; members do not import his promises or delivery model, and members do not sell guaranteed outcomes.

## Spot it in a competitor ad

- **Dream outcome stated:** a specific end state, not a feature. Example: "a booked-out Tuesday" rather than "scheduling software."
- **Likelihood signals:** proof, numbers, named customers, a mechanism, a guarantee. Example: "41 studios on it this year" or a demo of it working.
- **Time delay addressed:** the ad says how soon. Example: "first client call on the calendar within a week." Note whether the claim is substantiated.
- **Effort addressed:** the ad shrinks the first step. Example: "we set it up on a 20-minute call; you do nothing before then."
- **Denominator ignored:** a big promise with no mention of time or effort, which leaves the viewer to assume both are large. Common in stage 2 markets.
- **Overclaim pattern:** outcome, speed and ease all maximal at once ("results in 7 days with zero work"). Tag as a policy and truth risk.

## Use it in ours

- **Hook:** choose one term of the equation per hook. A time hook ("by Friday"), an effort hook ("one call, nothing else"), a likelihood hook (a proof number), or an outcome hook (the end state in the persona's words). Do not stack all four in one line.
- **Body:** work the denominator. Most small-business ads overspend on the dream and ignore effort. Show the first week, the setup, what the customer does not have to do.
- **Static:** a "what's inside" flat lay and a "first week" timeline card lower perceived effort; a review card raises likelihood; an offer card states time.
- **Video:** screen recordings and demos are likelihood evidence; a founder walking through the first call is effort evidence. Real testimonials that mention how long it took are time evidence.
- **Landing page:** put the first step and its duration near the CTA. "Book a 15-minute call" beats "Get started."
- **Market map:** in the proof arms race section, note which term of the equation the roster competes on. If everyone sells outcome and nobody sells ease, ease is uncontested.

## How it backfires

- Members do not import his promises or delivery model. Hormozi's examples come from high-ticket offers with guarantees; a Skool community or a local service that promises outcomes it cannot control breaks FTC and Meta rules and its own trust. See [FTC endorsements](../platform/ftc-endorsements.md) and [creator category truth rules](../platform/creator-category-truth-rules.md).
- Shrinking the denominator with claims that are not true ("zero effort") produces refunds and churn, which the finance gate will catch late.
- The equation is a heuristic, not a measured model. There is no study behind the multiplication; treat it as a checklist for what to mention.
- Inflating the dream outcome in categories with sensitive attributes (health, money) reads as sensational under policy review.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation

- What is the dream outcome in the customer's words, and which persona file has the quote?
- What proof raises the likelihood, and is it real, typical or disclaimed?
- How long until the customer feels the first result, honestly?
- What is the smallest first step, and could it be smaller?
- Which term of the equation does the roster ignore?
