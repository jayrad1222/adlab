# The offer stack (Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*, 2021, paraphrased)

## The idea in three sentences

Hormozi's "Grand Slam Offer," paraphrased, is a core deliverable surrounded by bonuses that each remove an objection, scarcity and urgency that are true, a guarantee that shifts risk, and a name that states the magnet reason, the avatar, the goal, the time interval and the container. The stack exists so the buyer compares the whole package against the price rather than the core alone. Ad Lab uses the stack as a construction checklist for the offer-led angle family; members do not import his promises or delivery model, and members do not sell guaranteed outcomes.

## Spot it in a competitor ad

- **Bonus list:** items added to the core, usually with individual values attached. Example: "plus the template pack, plus the onboarding call." Note whether each bonus answers a stated objection or is padding.
- **Scarcity claim:** a capped number. Example: "eight spots." Check whether the cap is stated consistently across the roster and over time; a cap that never fills is a tell.
- **Urgency claim:** a deadline. Example: "closes Sunday." Check whether the same deadline rolls every week.
- **Guarantee type:** unconditional, conditional, performance-based, or "keep the bonuses." Example: "if you do not get X in 90 days, we work free until you do." Performance guarantees are a policy flag in most member categories.
- **Named offer:** a proper noun with avatar, goal and time interval. Example: "The 30-Day Studio Fill for yoga owners."
- **Price anchoring:** total "value" shown against the price. Example: "$2,400 of value for $297." Tag as anchoring; the critic checks the arithmetic is not invented.

## Use it in ours

- **Hook:** the offer-led hook states one element, usually the name or the guarantee, not the whole stack. "The 30-Day Studio Fill is open" is a hook; a list is not.
- **Body:** one bonus per objection from the objection taxonomy. If a bonus removes no objection, cut it.
- **Static:** the offer card and the "what's inside" flat lay are the stack's native formats. Show the core large and the bonuses small; the eye should land on the deliverable.
- **Video:** founder-to-camera reading the stack sounds like a pitch. Instead, show one bonus solving one objection in a demo or a customer clip, and leave the full stack to the page.
- **Landing page:** the stack lives here, in full, with the guarantee near the CTA. Scarcity and urgency appear only when true and are removed when they stop being true.
- **Market map:** the price and positioning map records each competitor's guarantee type and bonus count. A roster with no guarantees makes a modest, honest one stand out.

## How it backfires

- Members do not import his promises or delivery model. Performance guarantees, earnings claims and "work free until" promises are off the table for creator, community and most service categories. See [creator category truth rules](../platform/creator-category-truth-rules.md).
- False scarcity and rolling deadlines are a policy violation and a trust cost. The critic checks the ad library for the same deadline reused.
- Bonus inflation. Ten bonuses signal that the core is weak, and the "value" arithmetic invites an FTC question.
- A named offer with a time interval the member cannot deliver on turns the name into a liability.
- The stack is a construction heuristic, not a tested model; its claims about what "always" converts are untested marketing content.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation

- What is the core, in one line, and would a buyer pay for it with no bonuses?
- Which objection does each bonus remove, and where is the objection documented?
- What guarantee can the member honestly offer and afford? What does the finance model say about refunds?
- Is the scarcity real, and what makes it real?
- Does the name say who it is for, what they get and by when?
