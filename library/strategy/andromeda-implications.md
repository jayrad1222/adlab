# Meta's 2026 ranking shift, "Andromeda" (agency consensus, October 2026 blog posts)

## The idea in three sentences

The guidance in this file is agency consensus drawn from October 2026 blog posts, not Meta documentation; no primary Meta source was found, and the specific percentages in those posts are vendor claims. The sources agree on direction: creative diversity now does the targeting work audiences used to do, detailed targeting is treated as a suggestion, consolidated structures with fewer ad sets and more distinct concepts outperform fragmented ones, fatigue arrives in weeks rather than months, and distinct concepts beat many variants of one. The design consequence for Ad Lab is fixed: each round ships eight to twelve genuinely different concepts, each with three to six hooks, the test plan runs one broad ad set, and the refresh cadence is weeks.

## Spot it in a competitor ad

- **Concept breadth in the roster:** count distinct concepts (different angle, persona or mechanism), not ads. Example: a competitor with forty active ads that collapse to three concepts has adapted to nothing.
- **Persona signals inside creative:** the ad names its audience in the hook ("if you run a two-chair salon") because the creative is doing the targeting. Example: a roster where each ad calls out a different buyer.
- **Launch-date clustering:** many ads started the same week suggests a consolidated launch of a concept batch. Check the ad library dates.
- **Rapid turnover:** ads disappearing after three to five weeks and replaced with new concepts, not new hooks. This is the fatigue cadence the sources describe.
- **Variant farms:** dozens of near-identical ads with minor copy changes. The sources predict this underperforms; it is a sign of an account structured for the old model.

## Use it in ours

- **Hook:** three to six per concept, from different hook categories. The hook set is where cheap diversity lives.
- **Body:** every concept gets a distinct angle family and, where possible, a distinct persona. The critic rejects a batch where two concepts differ only in hook or colour.
- **Static:** at least six formats across the bank and two ugly ones. Statics are the fastest way to reach eight to twelve concepts on a small budget.
- **Video:** pair each video concept with a static of the same idea so the platform can pick a format per viewer.
- **Landing page:** one page can serve many concepts if the promise is shared; concepts with a different promise get their own page or a matched first screen.
- **Market map:** the format mix and gaps section records the roster's concept count, persona coverage and turnover. The plan section states the design consequence in numbers: eight to twelve concepts, three to six hooks each, one broad ad set, refresh in weeks.

## How it backfires

- Treating blog numbers as fact. The sources disagree with each other (four to six, eight to twelve, and ten to fifteen concepts all appear), and a "65% higher ROAS" figure appears only in secondary posts. Cite none of these as findings.
- Over-fragmenting in the name of diversity. The same sources say consolidation wins; eight concepts in one ad set, not eight ad sets.
- Diversity without truth. Twelve concepts means twelve claims to substantiate. The evidence integrity rules apply to every one.
- Assuming the direction holds on TikTok, YouTube or LinkedIn. This is Meta-specific guidance; see [TikTok and YouTube differences](../platform/tiktok-youtube-differences.md).
- Refresh in weeks means production in weeks. A member who cannot produce a new batch monthly should start with fewer concepts and a slower cadence.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation

- How many genuinely different concepts can the member truthfully support, and which personas do they cover?
- What is the member's realistic production cadence, and does it match a refresh-in-weeks plan?
- Which of the current ads are variants of one idea, and which are ideas?
- What will the member measure in week three to decide what to retire?
