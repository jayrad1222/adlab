# Five stages of market sophistication (Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*, 1966)

## The idea in three sentences

A market grows more sophisticated every time it hears the same promise, so last year's claim stops working once it has been seen a hundred times. Schwartz describes five stages, from a fresh market that believes a plain claim to an exhausted one that answers only to identity and experience, each with one required move. Ad Lab's market map names the stage and the move; most categories members enter are at stage three to five.

## Spot it in a competitor ad

- **Stage 1, new market.** Cues: a plain claim, no qualifier, no mechanism, little proof. Example: "Get more clients" as the whole headline. Move: make the simple claim first and clearly.
- **Stage 2, claims copied.** Cues: the same promise across the roster, enlarged with numbers, speed or scope. Example: six ads promise a result, three add a timeframe. Move: a bigger or more specific claim, if provable.
- **Stage 3, claims exhausted.** Cues: "how it works," a named method, a step count, a diagram. Example: "The 3-step intake system that fills your calendar." Move: introduce a mechanism; sell the way the result is produced.
- **Stage 4, mechanisms copied.** Cues: competing named methods, "easier" or "faster" claims, "unlike other programs" comparisons. Example: "Not another 12-week course: a 20-minute daily protocol." Move: a better or easier mechanism, or a bigger promise for the mechanism.
- **Stage 5, market exhausted.** Cues: no claim at all; the ad is about who the buyer is or how the product feels; lifestyle footage, humour, brand assets. Example: "For the two-person shop that still answers its own phone." Move: identification and experience.

**Deciding the stage from a set of competitor ads:**

1. Take the dated roster. Drop anything older than twelve months unless it has run continuously.
2. Tag each ad's primary claim type: plain claim, enlarged claim, mechanism, mechanism-versus-mechanism, identity or experience.
3. Count. The stage is the highest level used by at least a third of current ads; mostly mechanisms with a few identity ads is stage 3 moving to 4.
4. Check the longest runners separately; if they sit a stage below the newest ads, the newer stage is being tested, not proven.
5. Write the stage, counts, ad IDs and required move into the market map, marked as a judgment with the sample size.

## Use it in ours

- **Hook:** stage 3, lead with the mechanism; stage 4, with what the mechanism removes (time, effort, a step); stage 5, with the identification line from the persona file.
- **Body:** a stage 2 bigger-claim body in a stage 4 market reads as a stranger shouting.
- **Static:** stages 3 and 4 favour mechanism cards and us-versus-them tables; stage 5 favours founder notes, memes and persona cards.
- **Video:** stage 3, a mechanism demo; stage 4, comparison with the old way; stage 5, day-in-the-life or a real testimonial.
- **Landing page:** a mechanism ad lands on the mechanism above the fold; an identity ad lands on who it is for.
- **Market map:** state the stage, the move, the counts, and the one move the roster is not making; that gap is a bank hypothesis.

## How it backfires

- Calling a category stage 5 from five ads; small samples see novelty, not exhaustion.
- Inventing a mechanism for stage 3. A renamed ordinary process survives until a buyer asks what is different; the critic checks it matches what the member delivers.
- Bigger claims at stage 2 run into Meta's misleading-results rules and FTC substantiation; see [FTC endorsements](../platform/ftc-endorsements.md).
- Stages are not strictly sequential; a new entrant can reset a category. The ordering is contested, a heuristic rather than a law.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation

- What stage is the category in, and on how many ads does that rest?
- What is the member's mechanism in one sentence, and is it real?
- Which claim has been made so often that repeating it costs money?
- Is there a less sophisticated adjacent category to be judged in? See [Dunford positioning](dunford-positioning.md).
