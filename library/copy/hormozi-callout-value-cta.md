# Hormozi's callout, value, CTA (Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*, 2021, and *$100M Leads*, 2023)

## The idea in three sentences

Hormozi's working ad structure, paraphrased, has three parts: a callout that names who the ad is for so the right person stops, a value section that hooks them, keeps them and rewards them for staying, and a call to action that makes the next step obvious. Behind it sits his value equation: perceived value rises with the dream outcome and the likelihood of getting it, and falls with the time it takes and the effort it costs, so an ad should raise the first two with specificity and proof and lower the second two by showing the first step is small. Ad Lab keeps his frameworks and does not import his promises or his delivery model; members do not sell guaranteed outcomes.

The related pieces, also paraphrased: the Grand Slam Offer stack (the core, bonuses, scarcity, urgency, guarantee, naming), covered in `../strategy/hormozi-offer-stack.md`; the more-better-new scaling heuristic (run more ads and placements, improve what works, then try new platforms and formats); and his own account that his first winning ad was ugly and the offer and the hook did the work.

## Spot it in a competitor ad

- **Callout cue.** The first line names a role, a situation or a condition: "if you run a two-person shop," "for anyone who just closed on a duplex." The callout is doing targeting the ad set no longer does (see `../strategy/andromeda-implications.md`).
- **Value section cue.** After the hook, the ad teaches, lists or demonstrates something usable before asking for anything. Look for a lesson, a checklist or a mechanism that the viewer could act on without buying.
- **Reward cue.** A payoff for watching to the end: the answer to the open loop, the price, a bonus. Note where in the transcript it lands.
- **CTA cue.** A single explicit next step, usually the small one: "comment," "text," "watch part two." Multiple CTAs or a vague "learn more" means the structure is not being followed.
- **Value equation cues.** Dream outcome stated as a picture; likelihood raised with proof; time delay lowered with a stated timeline; effort lowered with "done for you," templates or a first step under a minute. Flag any outcome that is promised rather than shown; that is where this tradition's imitators cross into policy risk.

## Use it in ours

- **Hook:** the callout is the hook, or the hook precedes it by one line. Write the callout from the persona file in the persona's words; it should repel the wrong viewer as clearly as it selects the right one (see `../psychology/dont-design-for-average.md`).
- **Body:** structure as hook, keep, reward. "Keep" is the value: a usable lesson, the mechanism, the proof. "Reward" is the payoff for staying: the specific answer, the number, the demonstration. Raise likelihood with proof the member owns; lower time and effort by naming the first step and how long it takes.
- **Static:** the callout as the headline, one value line (the lesson or the mechanism), one CTA. The offer card format fits the most-aware audience; the problem-named card fits the callout alone.
- **Video:** callout spoken in the first second with the on-screen card matching. Value section carries the middle; cut anything that does not keep or reward. CTA in the last three seconds, one action, destination named.
- **Landing page:** the value equation as the editing tool for the hero. Dream outcome in the headline as a picture, likelihood in the proof block, time delay as a stated timeline, effort as the first step. The offer stack appears after the proof, never before it.

## How it backfires

- Importing the promises. Hormozi's examples come from businesses with sales teams, high-ticket offers and outcome guarantees. Members do not sell guaranteed outcomes; "guaranteed," "results," and income framing trigger Meta policy and FTC rules (see `../platform/meta-policy-pitfalls.md`, `../platform/ftc-endorsements.md`, `../platform/creator-category-truth-rules.md`).
- Importing the delivery model. The offer stack assumes bonuses, scarcity and urgency the member can actually deliver. Fake scarcity and bonus padding are trust killers and the critic rejects them.
- Dream outcome inflation. Raising the numerator with a bigger promise instead of a more specific one fails Harry Dry's falsify test and usually fails policy.
- Callouts that imply personal attributes. "If you're drowning in debt" is a second-person attribute claim on Meta. Callouts name situations and roles, not conditions about the person.
- More-better-new applied before the bank has eight to twelve distinct concepts is "more of one thing," which the 2026 ranking shift punishes.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation

- Who exactly should stop scrolling, and who should keep going? Say both.
- What can the viewer use from this ad without buying anything?
- What is the smallest true first step, and how long does it take?
- Which proof you own raises the likelihood of the outcome without promising it?
- What in the current offer would you have to remove if you could not guarantee anything?
