# CTA patterns

A call to action is the last friction point in the ad, so each pattern below is described by the friction it removes, not by its wording. Pick the pattern from the awareness stage and the objection the persona raised (see `../sales/objection-taxonomy.md`); the words come from the member's own voice. Examples are original and use the same fictional businesses as the rest of this folder: Two Wrenches Plumbing (Dale and Priya) and First Rental (an online course for self-managing one rental unit).

## The small first step

When: problem-aware or solution-aware viewers; any time the full purchase feels large. Friction removed: commitment. The ask is the smallest true action that moves the viewer forward. Example: "Text a photo of the heater label. Priya replies with a price." Hormozi's denominator move; see `hormozi-callout-value-cta.md`.

## The reason-why CTA

When: the viewer needs a justification for acting now. Friction removed: "why now." The CTA carries a true reason attached to a date or an event. Example: "Book the scope visit before the first freeze, forecast for the 14th."

## The reversal CTA

When: trust objections; product-aware viewers who have been burned. Friction removed: the fear of being wrong. The guarantee or the no-cost condition is stated in the CTA itself. Example: "If Dale cannot find the leak on the first visit, the $89 is refunded."

## The specific-destination CTA

When: any stage; always when the landing page is unusual. Friction removed: uncertainty about what happens after the tap. Name what the viewer will see. Example: "The link opens lesson four's clause list, not a sales page."

## The no-oriented CTA

When: skeptical viewers; retargeting. Friction removed: the pressure of saying yes. Voss's no-oriented question invites a safe no that functions as a yes; see `../sales/voss-toolkit.md`. Example: "Would it be a bad idea to see the move-in checklist before you decide?"

## The self-selection CTA

When: identity angles; when the member wants to repel wrong-fit buyers. Friction removed: "is this for me." The CTA restates the fit condition. Example: "If you own exactly one rental, start with lesson one. If you own six, this is not for you."

## The time-boxed CTA

When: the time objection ("I don't have time for this"). Friction removed: the imagined effort. State how long the first action takes, truthfully. Example: "The lease edit takes one evening. Lesson four is 22 minutes."

## The proof-first CTA

When: product-aware viewers who want evidence before a form. Friction removed: distrust of the claim. The action is to see proof, and the purchase comes after. Example: "Watch the last ten scope videos, then decide."

## The named-person CTA

When: small businesses where the founder is the product. Friction removed: the fear of a call centre or a bot. Name who answers. Example: "Priya reads every text herself, usually within the hour."

## The price-stated CTA

When: most-aware viewers; offer-led angles. Friction removed: the fear of a hidden price. State the number and the terms. Example: "$149 until the 31st, one payment, keep it forever."

## The deadline CTA (true only)

When: offer-led angles with a real date or a real capacity limit. Friction removed: deferral. Example: "Dale has four heater slots left this week; the calendar shows them." Fake deadlines are a trust killer and a policy risk; see `../psychology/hyperbolic-discounting.md` and `../platform/meta-policy-pitfalls.md`.

## The comparison CTA

When: the "I can do it myself" or "I'll use the cheaper option" objection. Friction removed: the unknown cost of the alternative. Example: "Compare the property manager's year against the course price. The sheet is on the page."

## The reply CTA

When: platforms where comments and DMs convert; early-stage trust. Friction removed: leaving the feed. The action happens inside the platform. Example: "Comment CLAUSES and Nadia sends the state list." Keep the reply mechanism real and staffed.

## The continuation CTA

When: education and story angles where the ad is part one. Friction removed: the sense that the ad is a pitch. The action is to keep learning. Example: "Part two covers the anode rod. It is pinned on the profile."

## The default-path CTA

When: the viewer is ready but wants to be told the one next step. Friction removed: choice overload; see `../psychology/defaults-and-status-quo.md`. One action, no menu. Example: "Start with lesson one. Everything else unlocks after."

## The ritual CTA

When: memberships and courses where the first week predicts retention. Friction removed: not knowing what using it looks like. Describe the first use as a routine; see `../psychology/rituals-and-ownership.md`. Example: "Day one: print the checklist. Day two: walk the unit with it. Day three: send the lease."
