# Harry Dry's three tests (Harry Dry, Marketing Examples copywriting guide, 2020 onward)

## The idea in three sentences

Every line of copy faces three questions: can you visualize it, can you falsify it, and could nobody else say it. A line that fails all three is deleted, and a line that fails two is rewritten before anyone argues for it. The tests replace taste with a check anyone can run, which is why the critic agent applies them to every hook, headline and CTA in the bank, and why every example line in this folder was written to pass them.

The three tests, spelled out:

- **Visualize.** Can the reader see a picture? "Fast service" is nothing. "Dale's truck in your driveway before the school run" is a picture.
- **Falsify.** Could the line be proven wrong? "Best plumbers in town" cannot. "Nine heater models on the truck" can, which is what makes it believable.
- **Nobody else.** Could a competitor paste the line into their ad unchanged? If yes, it is not about the member.

## Spot it in a competitor ad

The tests work in reverse as detection cues. For each competitor hook and headline, score 0 to 3:

- **Abstraction cue (fails visualize).** Adjectives without nouns: "premium," "seamless," "trusted," "quality." A line with two or more abstractions and no concrete noun scores zero on this test.
- **Unfalsifiable cue (fails falsify).** Superlatives and vague scale: "best," "leading," "thousands," "fast." Note whether any number, date, name or place appears.
- **Interchangeable cue (fails nobody else).** Swap the brand name for a competitor's. If the line still reads true, it fails. Most category conventions fail here, which is why the market map records them (see `../rubrics/market-map-template.md`).

Record the score in the per-ad rubric. A category where most ads score 0 or 1 is a category where a 3 will stand out; that is an opportunity note for the strategist.

## Use it in ours

- **Hook:** run every candidate hook through the three questions before ranking by type. Rewrite until each gets a yes. "Priya has quoted 312 water heater jobs since 2023" passes all three; "we are water heater experts" passes none.
- **Body:** apply the tests line by line, not to the paragraph. Each sentence should carry one picture, one checkable fact or one thing only the member can say. Delete the rest; the slide gets faster (see `canon-halbert-sugarman-bencivenga-collier.md`).
- **Static:** the headline must pass all three. The proof line must pass falsify at minimum. If the static has one line, that line is the test.
- **Video:** the spoken first sentence and the first on-screen card are tested separately. A visual can pass the visualize test on its own; the words still need to pass the other two.
- **Landing page:** the hero headline, the subhead and the CTA button text get the tests. A hero that fails nobody else is a hero that could be a competitor's page, and the viewer will notice before you do.

Writing moves that usually fix a failing line: add a number from the member's records; add a name, a place or a time; replace the adjective with the thing it describes; say what the member does that competitors do not, from the interrogation answers (see `../prompts/interrogation.md`).

## How it backfires

- Specificity theatre. A line can pass visualize and falsify with a number that was invented. The tests check the shape of the line, not its truth; the evidence integrity check does that (see `../rubrics/evidence-integrity.md`).
- Over-application. Not every sentence of body copy must be a picture; connective sentences exist. Run the tests hardest on hooks, headlines and CTAs.
- "Nobody else" can push copy toward quirks that are unique but irrelevant. The thing only the member can say must also be something the persona cares about.
- Falsifiable lines invite falsification. Ship only what the member can back when a commenter asks.
- The tests reward concreteness, and concreteness can collide with policy in sensitive categories (a vivid picture of a health problem). Check `../platform/meta-policy-pitfalls.md`.

## Questions it adds to the interrogation

- Which line on your site could a competitor paste unchanged?
- What is the most specific true number you have about your work, and where does it come from?
- What picture do you want in the viewer's head three seconds in?
- What do you do that you have never put in an ad because it felt too small? (Small and specific usually passes all three.)
- Which claim in your current ads would you be unable to defend in a comment thread?
