# Hook taxonomy

The ad-analyst tags one hook type per competitor ad; the copywriter uses the list as a menu, three to six hooks per concept. Examples are original, for Two Wrenches Plumbing (Dale and Priya) and First Rental (a course for self-managing one rental unit), and each passes Harry Dry's three tests. Stages follow `../strategy/schwartz-awareness-stages.md`; cautions point to `../platform/meta-policy-pitfalls.md`.

## Callout

Definition: names who the ad is for so the right person stops. Cues: opens with "if you" or a named role; the first on-screen card is a condition, not a claim. Plumbing: "If your water heater is older than your truck, Dale wants a photo of the label." Course: "If you own one rental and your tenant has your cell number, this is for you." Stage: problem-aware to solution-aware. Caution: no second-person personal attributes (health, finances, age).

## Question

Definition: a question the viewer answers in their head. Cues: a question mark; the next line assumes a yes. Plumbing: "Did your shower go cold before the dishwasher finished?" Course: "Has your tenant texted 'quick question' after 9pm this week?" Stage: problem-aware. Caution: avoid questions that imply a sensitive attribute.

## Bold claim

Definition: a large promise stated plainly. Cues: a superlative or absolute; no hedge. Plumbing: "Dale finds the leak on the first visit or the scope fee is zero." Course: "A lease your state will enforce, edited in one evening." Stage: solution-aware and product-aware. Caution: must be provable; income and results claims are policy triggers.

## Specific number

Definition: a verifiable figure in the first line. Cues: digits in the first seven words; a unit attached. Plumbing: "Nine water heaters on the truck. Same-day swap." Course: "Twelve lease clauses. One missing cost Nadia a tenant." Stage: all, strongest for product-aware. Caution: the number must come from records.

## Curiosity gap

Definition: withholds the payoff to earn the next three seconds. Cues: "the one thing," "what happened next"; the payoff is later in the transcript. Plumbing: "The part that caused the flood cost four dollars." Course: "The clause every template leaves out is in lesson four." Stage: unaware to problem-aware. Caution: pay it off or the audience learns to skip.

## Pattern interrupt

Definition: something out of place for the feed. Cues: an odd object, silence, a wrong-sounding first word. Plumbing: Dale holding a wet sock to camera: "This is what your crawlspace feels like." Course: a blank lease on screen with "sign here" circled. Stage: unaware. Caution: no sensational or shocking imagery.

## Contrarian or myth ("stop doing X")

Definition: tells the viewer a common practice is wrong. Cues: "stop," "myth," "you don't need." Plumbing: "Stop pouring drain cleaner down that sink. Priya shows the pipe it is eating." Course: "You do not need an LLC for your first rental. You need three clauses." Stage: solution-aware. Caution: the correction must be true and fair.

## Negative or warning

Definition: leads with what goes wrong. Cues: "warning," "before you," "mistake." Plumbing: "Before you buy a tankless heater, check your gas line size. Most houses on our street fail it." Course: "Three things to check before you hand over the keys." Stage: problem-aware. Caution: keep fear proportionate.

## Confession or pratfall

Definition: admits a flaw to buy credibility. Cues: "we got this wrong," a visible imperfection. Plumbing: "Our van is a 2009 with peeling letters. The scope camera is new." Course: "I lost my first tenant over a repair I did not know was mine." Stage: product-aware. Caution: the flaw must be real.

## Story opener

Definition: starts in the middle of an event. Cues: past tense, a time stamp, a named person. Plumbing: "Thursday, 6:40am, a text from Elm Street: water under the stairs." Course: "In June the rent cheque came with a note about the dryer vent." Stage: unaware to problem-aware. Caution: real events only.

## POV

Definition: the viewer sees through someone's eyes. Cues: "POV:" on screen; first-person camera. Plumbing: "POV: Dale opening your crawlspace hatch." Course: "POV: your first tenant's move-in inspection, with the checklist." Stage: unaware. Caution: none.

## "Three reasons"

Definition: a numbered promise of a short list. Cues: a digit plus "reasons," "ways," "signs." Plumbing: "Three signs your water heater has about a year left." Course: "Three clauses your downloaded lease template is missing." Stage: solution-aware. Caution: deliver the promised count.

## "I was today years old"

Definition: a shared discovery framed as late realization. Cues: the phrase itself; a surprise fact. Plumbing: "I was today years old when I learned the shutoff valve is behind the dryer." Course: "I was today years old when I learned the deposit has a legal deadline." Stage: unaware. Caution: the fact must be checkable.

## "Nobody talks about"

Definition: claims an overlooked truth. Cues: "nobody," "no one tells you." Plumbing: "Nobody tells you the anode rod is why heaters die early. Priya pulls one out." Course: "Nobody talks about the week after move-in. That is the week you lose them." Stage: solution-aware. Caution: overused; deliver something genuinely overlooked.

## Comparison ("X vs Y")

Definition: sets two options side by side. Cues: "vs," a split screen, a table. Plumbing: "Tank vs tankless, for a house built before 1980." Course: "Property manager at 10% vs First Rental once." Stage: solution-aware to product-aware. Caution: named-competitor comparisons must be accurate.

## News or "new"

Definition: announces a change. Cues: "new," "now," "just," a date. Plumbing: "New this month: Priya quotes from a photo, no visit." Course: "New lesson: the 2026 deposit rules, state by state." Stage: product-aware and most aware. Caution: must actually be new.

## Social proof ("why 1,214 people…")

Definition: leads with the behavior of others. Cues: a count of customers, reviews or members. Plumbing: "Why 212 households on our side of the river keep Priya's number pinned." Course: "The lesson 400 first-time landlords replayed most." Stage: product-aware. Caution: real counts only; see `../platform/ftc-endorsements.md`.

## Authority ("as a…")

Definition: borrows credibility from a role or credential. Cues: "as a," years of experience, a license number. Plumbing: "As the plumber who has opened 300 crawlspaces in this town, here is what I check first." Course: "After six years managing one duplex, here is my move-in checklist." Stage: solution-aware. Caution: real credentials only.

## Demonstration ("watch this")

Definition: shows the product or skill working. Cues: "watch," hands in frame, a result inside the clip. Plumbing: "Watch the scope camera find the break in 40 seconds." Course: "Watch me edit a lease clause for your state in two minutes." Stage: product-aware. Caution: show typical work, not a one-off.

## Problem-naming ("the 11pm invoice pile")

Definition: names the problem better than the viewer can. Cues: a concrete, time-stamped scene; no solution in the first line. Plumbing: "The bucket under the sink you empty every morning." Course: "The 9pm 'quick question' text from your tenant." Stage: problem-aware. Caution: fairness only.

## Identification ("for the two-person shop")

Definition: describes the viewer's identity so they feel seen. Cues: a self-description; "people like us." Plumbing: "For the homeowner who fixes it themselves until the day they cannot." Course: "For the accidental landlord who kept the old house." Stage: unaware to problem-aware. Caution: avoid implied sensitive attributes.

## Offer-led ("free until Friday")

Definition: leads with the deal. Cues: a price, a deadline, "free." Plumbing: "Scope visit $89 this month, and it comes off the repair." Course: "First Rental is $149 until the 31st, then $199." Stage: most aware. Caution: deadlines and scarcity must be true.

## "What I wish I knew"

Definition: hindsight framed as a gift. Cues: the phrase; a past mistake. Plumbing: "What Dale wishes homeowners knew before the first freeze." Course: "What I wish I knew the week before my first tenant moved in." Stage: problem-aware. Caution: truthfulness only.

## "The real reason"

Definition: promises the hidden cause. Cues: "real reason," "actually." Plumbing: "The real reason your water bill jumped is under the house, not in the shower." Course: "The real reason tenants leave in month three." Stage: solution-aware. Caution: the cause must be supportable.

## "Unpopular opinion"

Definition: a stance the viewer expects to disagree with. Cues: the phrase; a contrarian claim. Plumbing: "Unpopular opinion: most houses here should not get a tankless heater." Course: "Unpopular opinion: your first rental should not be an LLC." Stage: solution-aware. Caution: keep it defensible.

## "Day 1 vs day 30"

Definition: shows change over a stated period. Cues: two time stamps; a split frame. Plumbing: "Day 1: the bucket. Day 30: the receipt, the dry cabinet, the same bucket in the garage." Course: "Day 1: a downloaded template. Day 30: a signed lease, a deposit receipt and a move-in checklist." Stage: product-aware. Caution: restricted in health and body categories; elsewhere show fair context.
