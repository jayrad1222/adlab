# Angle families

An angle is the strategic argument an ad makes; a hook is the first three seconds that earns the argument a hearing. One concept carries one angle and three to six hooks, which is why the bank counts concepts by angle and not by hook (see `../strategy/denney-concept-vs-iteration.md`). Example angle statements below are original and use the same two fictional businesses as the rest of this folder: Two Wrenches Plumbing (Dale and Priya) and First Rental (an online course for people self-managing one rental unit). Awareness stages follow `../strategy/schwartz-awareness-stages.md`; sophistication stages follow `../strategy/schwartz-market-sophistication.md`.

## Problem-led

Definition: the ad is about the problem, named more precisely than the viewer can name it; the product appears late. Differs from the problem-naming hook: the hook is the first line, the angle is the whole argument that the problem is real, costly and specific. Suits problem-aware viewers; sophistication stages 1 to 3. Cues: most of the transcript describes the situation; the product gets under a quarter of the runtime; the persona's own language appears. Angle statements: "The morning bucket under the sink is a leak that gets more expensive every week you empty it." "The week after move-in is when first-time landlords lose tenants, and nobody prepares them for it."

## Desire-led

Definition: the ad is about the outcome the viewer already wants, channelled onto the product (Schwartz: you cannot create desire, only direct it). Differs from a bold-claim hook: the claim is one line, the angle is a picture of the wanted state held for the whole ad. Suits solution-aware viewers; stages 1 to 2, and stage 5 when paired with experience. Cues: future tense; sensory detail of the after-state; little mention of the problem. Angle statements: "A hot shower after the dishwasher runs, without thinking about it." "A rent cheque that arrives without a note attached."

## Mechanism-led

Definition: the ad explains how the product works and why that produces the result. Differs from a demonstration hook: the hook shows it working, the angle explains why it works and why alternatives do not. Suits solution-aware and product-aware viewers; the stage 3 and 4 move. Cues: "because," "the reason," a named part or step; a diagram or a hands-on shot. Angle statements: "Nine heater models on the truck is why same-day swaps are possible; most shops order yours after they visit." "Twelve clauses sorted by state is why the First Rental lease holds up; a generic template is a guess."

## Identity-led

Definition: the ad is about who the buyer is and who they are becoming. Differs from the callout and identification hooks: those select the viewer in a line, the angle makes the whole ad a mirror. Suits unaware and problem-aware viewers; the stage 5 move. Cues: "people like us," self-descriptions, lifestyle footage with the product in the background, status language; see `../psychology/godin-people-like-us.md` and `../psychology/status-and-identity.md`. Angle statements: "For the homeowner who fixes it themselves until the day they cannot, and wants a plumber who respects that." "For the accidental landlord who kept the old house and refuses to be the landlord people complain about."

## Proof-led

Definition: the ad is built on evidence: reviews, counts, footage, credentials. Differs from the social proof and authority hooks: those cite proof in a line, the angle makes proof the structure. Suits product-aware viewers; stages 2 to 4 where claims have stopped being believed. Cues: multiple proof elements; screenshots; real names; a before-and-after with context. Angle statements: "Scope footage from the last ten jobs, shown unedited, is the quote." "Four hundred first-time landlords replayed lesson four; here is what they said about it."

## Offer-led

Definition: the ad is the deal: price, terms, guarantee, deadline. Differs from the offer-led hook: the hook states the deal, the angle argues the deal is the reason to act now. Suits most-aware viewers; any sophistication stage when the audience already knows the product. Cues: price in the first frame; a guarantee; a date; a bonus list. Angle statements: "The $89 scope visit comes off the repair, so looking costs nothing if you fix it with us." "First Rental is $149 until the 31st, and the state lease pack is included until then."

## Contrarian

Definition: the ad argues the category's accepted advice is wrong. Differs from the contrarian and unpopular-opinion hooks: those open the argument, the angle sustains it with reasoning and evidence. Suits solution-aware viewers; stages 3 to 5. Cues: a named convention; "stop," "myth"; a reason the convention fails; see `../psychology/opposite-pass.md`. Angle statements: "Most houses on our side of the river should not get a tankless heater, and we will tell you why before we sell you anything." "Your first rental does not need an LLC; it needs three lease clauses and a deposit receipt."

## News or trend

Definition: the ad rides something that changed: a rule, a season, a price, a platform. Differs from the news hook: the hook says "new," the angle explains what the change means for the viewer. Suits product-aware and most-aware viewers; stage 2 (making the claim bigger with timeliness). Cues: a date, a regulation, a weather event, "this year." Angle statements: "The first freeze is forecast for next week; here is the one valve to check tonight." "The 2026 deposit rules changed the deadline in eleven states; the lesson is updated."

## Comparison

Definition: the ad sets the product against a named alternative, including doing nothing. Differs from the comparison hook: the hook names the pair, the angle does the fair accounting. Suits solution-aware and product-aware viewers; stages 2 to 4. Cues: a table; "vs"; a cost breakdown; the alternative stated fairly. Angle statements: "A property manager at ten percent for a year, or First Rental once; here is the math on a $1,600 rent." "The DIY drain cleaner, the handyman and Priya: what each one costs over a year of the same clog."

## Story or origin

Definition: the ad tells how the business or the product came to exist, or how one customer got here. Differs from the story-opener hook: the hook starts in the middle, the angle is the arc. Suits unaware and problem-aware viewers; stage 5 (experience and identification). Cues: past tense throughout; a named person; a turning point; the product as the resolution. Angle statements: "Nadia lost her first tenant over a repair she did not know was hers; the list of questions she wished she had asked became the course." "Dale bought the scope camera instead of a new van, and the first job paid for it."

## Education or insight (Challenger)

Definition: the ad teaches something that reframes the viewer's problem, then tailors the lesson to them (Dixon and Adamson's commercial insight; see `../sales/challenger-insight.md`). Differs from the "real reason" hook: the hook promises the reframe, the angle delivers the lesson. Suits problem-aware and solution-aware viewers; stages 3 and 4. Cues: "the reason your X is not working is not Y, it is Z"; a short lesson with a diagram; the product appears as the way to act on the lesson. Angle statements: "Heaters do not die of age; they die of a $30 anode rod nobody replaces, and here is how to check yours." "Tenants do not leave over rent; they leave over the first unanswered repair, so the course starts with the response script."

## Cost-of-inaction (policy caution)

Definition: the ad makes the price of doing nothing concrete. Differs from the negative or warning hook: the hook flags a risk, the angle totals the cost over time. Suits problem-aware viewers who are delaying; stages 1 to 3. Cues: a running total; "every month you wait"; a timeline. Caution: the cost must be true, proportionate and free of fear imagery; no implied personal attributes, no health or financial distress framing in the second person; see `../platform/meta-policy-pitfalls.md` and `../psychology/loss-aversion-and-prospect-theory.md`. Angle statements: "A slow leak under the slab adds to the water bill every month and to the repair bill every season; here is what last year's wait cost one Elm Street house, with their permission." "An unsigned lease is fine until the first dispute, and the deposit rules give you a deadline whether you know it or not."
