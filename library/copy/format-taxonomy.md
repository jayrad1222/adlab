# Format taxonomy

Every bank covers at least six formats and includes at least two "ugly" ones; the ad-analyst tags one format per competitor ad and the copywriter picks formats per concept so no two concepts look alike. Each entry gives a definition, what it is good for, production notes at phone size, and whether it should read as native (looks like a post) or polished (looks like an ad). Specs and safe zones live in `../platform/specs-and-safe-zones.md`.

## Statics

### Text-only "ugly" ad
Definition: plain text on a plain background, no design. Good for: problem-led and contrarian angles; testing a line cheaply. Phone: 1:1 or 4:5, system font, under 25 words, high contrast. Native.

### Us-versus-them table
Definition: a two-column table, the member against the category. Good for: comparison and mechanism angles at sophistication stages 3 and 4. Phone: five rows maximum, ticks and crosses legible without zoom. Polished, lightly.

### Three-reasons list
Definition: a headline and three numbered reasons. Good for: solution-aware audiences; education angles. Phone: one line per reason; number as the visual anchor. Either.

### Review or press card
Definition: a real review or press quote set as a card. Good for: proof-led angles; product-aware audiences. Phone: quote under 30 words, source line visible, star row optional. Native if it looks like the platform the review came from; see `../platform/ftc-endorsements.md`.

### Founder note (typed letter)
Definition: a short letter from the founder, typed, with a signature. Good for: story, confession and identity angles. Phone: 4:5, first line is the hook, under 80 words, real name. Native.

### Meme or tweet screenshot
Definition: a post-style screenshot with a joke or an observation. Good for: identity and contrarian angles; unaware audiences. Phone: real post dimensions, readable at feed size. Native. Must be the member's own post, not a fabricated one.

### "What's inside" flat lay
Definition: the product or deliverables laid out and labelled. Good for: offer-led angles; product-aware and most-aware audiences. Phone: top-down, each item labelled in 20-point-equivalent text or larger. Polished.

### FAQ card
Definition: one question and its answer. Good for: objection handling (see `../sales/objection-taxonomy.md`). Phone: question as the headline, answer under 40 words. Either.

### Comparison chart
Definition: a chart of options with costs or features. Good for: comparison angles including "versus doing nothing." Phone: three options maximum, one highlighted. Polished.

### "I was skeptical" quote
Definition: a customer quote that opens with doubt and ends with the result. Good for: proof-led angles; the accusation audit in static form. Phone: doubt line large, result line below. Native. Real quote only.

### DM or email screenshot (real only)
Definition: a screenshot of a real customer message. Good for: proof-led angles; A-pile disguise. Phone: crop to the message, redact personal data, keep timestamps. Native. Fabricated screenshots are a policy and FTC breach.

### Before-and-after (policy caution)
Definition: two states side by side. Good for: demonstration and desire angles in categories where it is allowed. Phone: equal framing, same lighting, dates on both. Either. Not allowed in health and body categories on Meta; see `../platform/meta-policy-pitfalls.md`.

### Offer card
Definition: the deal on one card: price, terms, deadline, guarantee. Good for: most-aware audiences; retargeting. Phone: price largest, deadline second, one line of terms. Polished.

### Problem-named card
Definition: the problem stated in the persona's words, nothing else. Good for: problem-aware audiences; trigger stage in `../strategy/levinger-three-realities-teep.md`. Phone: one sentence, large type. Native.

## Video

### Founder-to-camera
Definition: the founder speaks directly to the viewer, handheld or static. Good for: story, confession, education and offer angles; the default for small businesses. Phone: 9:16, face in the upper two thirds, captions on, first line spoken within one second. Native.

### Product demo
Definition: the product or service shown working. Good for: mechanism and demonstration angles; product-aware audiences. Phone: hands and product fill the frame; one action per shot. Either.

### Screen recording with picture-in-picture
Definition: a screen capture with the presenter in a corner. Good for: courses and software; mechanism angles. Phone: zoom the screen to the active area; presenter bubble outside the UI safe zone. Native.

### Podcast clip
Definition: a segment from a recorded conversation, captioned. Good for: authority and education angles. Phone: 9:16 crop on the speaker, captions large, context card in the first second. Native.

### Street interview
Definition: short questions to strangers, cut together. Good for: problem-led and identity angles; unaware audiences. Phone: subject centred, question on screen, consent on file. Native.

### Day-in-the-life
Definition: the founder's or a customer's day with the product in it. Good for: identity and story angles; sophistication stage 5. Phone: fast cuts, time stamps on screen, voiceover carries the argument. Native.

### Mini-documentary
Definition: a short filmed story with interviews and b-roll. Good for: origin and proof angles; brand building (see `../strategy/binet-field-long-and-short.md`). Phone: 9:16 or 4:5, captions, a hook card before the slow open. Polished.

### Whiteboard explainer
Definition: the founder draws the mechanism. Good for: mechanism and education angles; stage 3 and 4 markets. Phone: thick marker, three elements maximum per frame, camera close. Native.

### Listicle
Definition: a numbered list delivered to camera or as cards. Good for: three-reasons and "what I wish I knew" hooks. Phone: the number on screen each time it changes. Either.

### Skit or humour
Definition: a scripted scene that acts out the problem. Good for: problem-led and identity angles; unaware audiences. Phone: the joke lands inside three seconds; captions carry the dialogue. Native.

### POV
Definition: first-person camera through the customer's or founder's eyes. Good for: pattern interrupt and demonstration. Phone: "POV:" card in the first frame, stabilised but not smooth. Native.

### Reaction or duet
Definition: the founder reacts to a clip, a review or a question. Good for: myth correction and contrarian angles. Phone: split frame with the founder on the larger side; only react to content you have rights to. Native.

### Real testimonial
Definition: a customer, on camera, in their own words. Good for: proof-led angles. Phone: name and context on screen, unscripted, consent and disclosure on file; see `../platform/ftc-endorsements.md`. Native. A scripted founder is not a testimonial.

### "How it's made"
Definition: the process behind the product or service. Good for: mechanism and costly-signaling angles (see `../psychology/costly-signaling.md`). Phone: close shots of hands and materials, step labels. Either.

### Mistakes and lessons
Definition: the founder lists what went wrong and what changed. Good for: confession and education angles. Phone: one mistake per cut, the lesson on screen. Native.

### Objection handling
Definition: the founder answers the top objection directly. Good for: product-aware audiences; retargeting; see `../sales/objection-taxonomy.md`. Phone: the objection on screen first, in the viewer's words. Native.

### Myth correction
Definition: a common belief stated, then corrected with a reason. Good for: contrarian and education angles. Phone: the myth crossed out on screen, then the correction. Either.

### Comparison with doing it alone
Definition: the product against the DIY path, costed fairly. Good for: the "I can do it myself" objection. Phone: a running tally on screen. Either.

## Carousel

### Tutorial steps
Definition: one step per card. Good for: education angles; courses. Phone: step number top left, one image and one line per card, last card is the CTA. Either.

### Persona cards
Definition: one card per customer type ("for the accidental landlord"). Good for: identity angles; fit objections. Phone: the persona name as headline, two lines of their situation. Native.

### Timeline
Definition: the first week or month of using the product, card by card. Good for: the time objection and ritual framing (see `../psychology/rituals-and-ownership.md`). Phone: day or week label large, one outcome per card. Either.

### "Swipe to see" reveal
Definition: card one poses the question, later cards answer. Good for: curiosity hooks; mechanism reveals. Phone: card one must work alone; pay off by card three. Native.

### Before-during-after process (with fair context)
Definition: the job or transformation shown in stages with dates and conditions. Good for: demonstration and proof angles outside restricted categories. Phone: same framing on every card, context line on each. Either. Same policy caution as the static before-and-after.
